This visit was one of the finest dining experiences I have ever had. I got to try things I never thought I would eat, such as reindeer heart and rotten mushrooms.

Maaemo is one of three restaurants in the Scandinavian countries to hold the prestigious three Michelin stars. Another is Geranium in Copenhagen, and in the latest ranking (2018) Frantzen in Stockholm was also promoted to three stars. Even the legendary Noma held only two stars before it closed. Beyond the Michelin stars, the restaurant also ranked 79th on The World's 50 Best Restaurants list (2017).

Although I booked almost three months ahead, out of the three or four days I spent in Oslo I only just managed to get a table, at lunch. It was a day of unseasonal rain. I walked about 10 minutes from Oslo Central Station to the restaurant. On arrival, the waiting staff stood lined up to welcome us, perfectly in step; here there is no need for a reservation list, as every member of staff can recall our booking details from our name alone. After taking my umbrella and coat, a member of staff led me to my table and introduced themselves.

The system at Maaemo is among the highest in standard, even compared with restaurants of the same level. Besides one waiter looking after only one or two tables, every dish is carried down by the chef responsible for it from the kitchen on the restaurant's second floor and served at our table. That is not all: if we choose the wine pairing or the juice pairing, the resident sommelier personally serves and explains each one.

In summary, this meal was one of my finest experiences. The standard of every dish is very high, and every ingredient is specially controlled. Besides superb menu creation, the chef also handles ingredients in a very planet-friendly way, along with service that is polished yet warm.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Duck feet, duck liver cream and cherry
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/01.jpg | A row of purple-red appetiser beads on a cream ceramic stand, Maaemo, Oslo
The first dish has duck feet underneath, chewy, springy and gelatinous, and on top a cream made from duck liver, classic in flavour like foie gras but considerably less rich. It goes very well with the sweet-and-sour cherry juice reduced into the duck liver. It is topped with shoots of local vegetables, which give a faintly pungent green note so that it never becomes too cloying. The first dish surprised me; I never thought the chef would dare to serve something like this from the very first plate, and the flavour is balanced and resolved, with superb creativity.

### Amuse brioche
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/02.jpg | A small brioche topped with young shoots and yellow flower petals, on a cream ceramic stand, Maaemo, Oslo
The second bite, the Amuse brioche, is a meringue with a gentle sourness, eaten with an intense pickled garlic. The pickled garlic here doesn't taste anything like the pickled garlic we are familiar with; its intensity is closer to black garlic. On top are young shoots from a farm called Bergsmyrene, which grows vegetables only for this restaurant. This dish surprised me again. The intensity of the black garlic is not excessive and balances well with the mild vegetables, whose shoots carry a little spiciness. A bite that is complex in flavour yet where nothing clashes, done in perfect balance.

### Cornet with lumpfish roe
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/03.jpg | A crisp cone filled with orange roe, stuck into dried flower petals of many colours, Maaemo, Oslo
The last bite is a cornet with lumpfish roe. I think this was the most ordinary of the meal; it was done well, as expected, but there was nothing special about it the way there was about the other dishes.

### Razor clam from Nordskot
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/04.jpg | Razor clam in a long shell, on crushed ice and kelp, Maaemo, Oslo
The first proper dish is razor clam from Nordskot, eaten with a sauce of green strawberry and woodruff. The cool, fresh fragrance of the woodruff goes beautifully with the sweet aroma of the strawberry. Although it is strawberry, the taste is not sweet; it is rather plain with a slight sourness, which brings out the very fresh clam, crisp but not tough, wonderfully.

### Lompe with cured trout and horseradish
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/05.jpg | Lompe topped with pink cured trout, sliced shallots and dill, on a cream ceramic plate, Maaemo, Oslo
Next is lompe with cured trout and horseradish, a Norwegian folk dish like a pancake but made with potato instead. What makes this version special is that the chef cures the trout himself by a special method that takes longer than ordinary curing, so the flavour is not heavily fishy as usual but more concentrated than usual. Eaten with horseradish, its gentle pungency covers the smell of the trout perfectly. The dill on top gives a mild, cool fragrance that sets off the dish beautifully.

### Oysters from Bømlo
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/06.jpg | A dark green dill sauce covering an oyster, in the centre of a white ceramic plate, Maaemo, Oslo
Fresh oysters caught by divers at Bømlo and sent straight to the restaurant day by day are very sweet and fresh, and go well with the sauce of dill and mussels, which has a light saltiness and a faint aroma of seafood and dill. The sauce, served lukewarm, plays in the mouth against the slight chill of the oyster. Another dish done very well.

### Scallops from Nordskot with mead and elderflower sauce
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/07.jpg | Sliced scallops layered in a clear brown broth, in a dark grey ceramic bowl, Maaemo, Oslo
Here again the restaurant uses diver-caught scallops, this time from the Nordskot area, cooked evenly throughout without the outside turning tough. The scallop meat is so sweet that it would taste good with no seasoning at all. The sauce is made from mead, a honey liquor, and elderflower, mixed with a dashi of dried scallop and seaweed, giving a gentle sweetness with a little saltiness and a faint aroma of the sea from the dried scallop and the seaweed, which have natural umami.

### Langoustine claws from Midsund in seaweed broth
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/08.jpg | Langoustine claw meat in an orange-brown broth, in a tilted cream ceramic bowl, Maaemo, Oslo
The next dish shows the chef's use of ingredients at its very best, as mentioned above. The chef takes very fresh langoustine claws from Midsund, whose flesh is not mushy as it is in ordinary restaurants, and cooks them slowly in a seaweed broth. The taste is like a lobster bisque, but gentler and softer, with an Eastern note from the seaweed broth. Superb.

### Langoustine tail grilled with pine
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/09.jpg | Grilled langoustine tail resting on green pine branches, Maaemo, Oslo
As for the tail, the chef grills it with pine, which gives a faint, fresh fragrance of pine needles. The langoustine meat is so sweet it needs no seasoning at all, yet the chef adds a little sauce made from caramelised langoustine fat, which adds real dimension to the dish.

### Mackerel from the Oslo bay
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/10.jpg | Mackerel under herbs and flowers of many colours, on a green sauce, on a cream ceramic plate, Maaemo, Oslo
The next dish is mackerel from the bay right here around Oslo, lightly cured, which intensifies the flavour of the flesh, with a sauce of ramson and apple and herbs from the restaurant's own farm. The intense flavour of the fish goes well with the sauce, lightly sweet and sour, the apple bringing freshness and fragrance. Eaten with the herbs and flowers, it adds texture in the mouth and a variety of aromas.

### Homemade bread and butter
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/11.jpg | A round loaf scored with a cross, in a ceramic cup at the centre of a tray of wheat grains, Maaemo, Oslo
The bread at this restaurant is unusual. It is not served first, or as a stop-gap against hunger as elsewhere; here the bread is a dish in its own right. Freshly baked bread is brought out piping hot. The restaurant uses only traditional native Norwegian wheat for its bread, and bakes it day by day only. It is eaten with homemade butter made from just one of the three cows the restaurant keeps on its farm. That day the waiter even told me the name of the cow whose milk made the butter. He added that the butter differs slightly from day to day according to the cow's mood, which affects the flavour of the milk and in the end the butter. The bread has a light brown crumb, a plain taste with sweetness lingering at the tip of the tongue, and the full aroma of wheat. With the homemade butter it goes superbly.

### Rømmegrøt with smoked reindeer heart
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/12.jpg | Sour cream porridge topped with grated dark brown smoked heart, in a cream ceramic bowl, Maaemo, Oslo
The next dish may look simple, but it is sour cream porridge with smoked reindeer heart (Rømmegrøt), a Norwegian traditional dish that at first I thought I would not be able to eat. The porridge is rice boiled with sour cream; overall the taste is like congee mixed with yoghurt, rich and creamy with a refreshing sourness of yoghurt. The smoked reindeer heart is grated; its flavour is mild and crunchy, like pig's heart but not at all gamey, with a faint woodsmoke at the tip of the tongue and a saltiness from the salted butter placed lightly on top. Finally comes a sweet-and-sour taste like umeshu, from a plum vinegar that I think is the gentlest vinegar I have ever tasted, which cuts the heavy, creamy richness of the porridge and the butter well.

### Quail egg with lamb and finely chopped onion
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/13.jpg | A quail egg yolk in a green-gold broth sprinkled with tiny pearls, at the centre of a cream ceramic plate, Maaemo, Oslo
The next dish is a quail egg cooked just at the surface in animal-bone stock, eaten with diced lamb and finely chopped onion. I like the attention to detail here: the lamb and the onion are cut very finely to match the size of the quail egg and of the small sago-like starch pearls that come with it. In the mouth the flavour of the half-raw quail egg comes first, followed by the aroma of the bone liquid, rich and fragrant like a bone broth, and a light sweetness from the onion at the tip of the tongue. Besides that there is the texture of lightly salted lamb and the crunch of the sago-like pearls. Overall it is another dish I liked a lot, though I wanted a little more intensity.

### Duck heart, cherry blossom petals and salt-pickled cherries
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/14.jpg | Three pieces of duck heart on a light brown cream sauce, on a black ceramic plate, Maaemo, Oslo
Duck heart with cherry blossom petals and salt-pickled cherries, dressed with a cream sauce made from duck offal. The beautiful picture I had in my mind was shattered by the chef's description of the dish, for someone like me who doesn't much like offal, but since we had come this far we had to try. The first bite had no off-putting smell, but I personally dislike the crisp, springy texture of heart anyway. The salt-pickled cherry impressed me most in this dish: it tastes like cherry jam with a slight saltiness at the tip of the tongue, goes well with the duck heart and cuts the richness of the cream sauce, whose flavour and aroma I thought resembled foie gras. Overall the flavour keeps its superbly high standard flawlessly. The pairing of pickled cherry and sauce blends well without being too heavy or cloying.

### Frozen blue cheese with pickled eryngii
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/15.jpg | A small mound of white-grey frozen blue cheese flakes, in a thick-rimmed ceramic bowl with a ring pattern, Maaemo, Oslo
Next is another of the dishes that impressed me most in this meal. Many people may hate blue cheese for its fearsome smell, but hold on: seeing this photo you may not think this dish is blue cheese. Yet yes, it is frozen blue cheese, grated into ice-like flakes, eaten with pickled king oyster mushrooms. It is like eating a blue-cheese-flavoured bingsu, with only the intensity of the cheese and not much of its pungent smell. Freezing reduces the smell that many people dislike very well. The pickled king oyster mushrooms add a crisp texture and a gentle sweetness at the tip of the tongue that match the blue cheese superbly.

### Milk ice cream, rowan flowers and rhubarb
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/16.jpg | White milk ice cream under cream-coloured rowan flowers, in rhubarb liquid, in a black ceramic bowl, Maaemo, Oslo
On to dessert. It is a milk ice cream using milk from just one cow, selected from one of the three the restaurant keeps in order to obtain the most stable flavour. The ice cream is not sweet but rich and very fragrant, not the richness of the scientific kind but the richness of fresh milk, which can't be found in milk sold in the market, so the ice cream here needs almost no seasoning at all. Rowan flowers (Rowan Flower) add playfulness in texture when chewed and a cool fragrance to the ice cream, but personally I would want to showcase the flavour of the milk as much as possible. The sweetness in this dish comes from a warm rhubarb syrup, which goes rather well.

### Butter ice cream with molasses sauce and hazelnuts
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/17.jpg | Butter ice cream drizzled with golden syrup over crushed hazelnuts, in a grey-green ceramic bowl, Maaemo, Oslo
Butter ice cream topped with a sauce from a syrup made of molasses, eaten with roasted hazelnuts. This dish is delicious too. The butter flavour is very rich, salty and fatty. The molasses syrup brings an intense sweetness in which I could smell faint sugarcane. The roasted hazelnuts go excellently with the butter flavour, adding fragrance and crunch to the dessert.

### Reindeer fat waffle with brown cheese
image: ./images/maaemo-2018/18.jpg | A heart-shaped waffle on a blue-and-white porcelain plate, beside a cup of brown cheese, jam and whipped cream, Maaemo, Oslo
The last dish of the meal is a waffle. Not an ordinary waffle: it is made with reindeer fat blended with koji, and eaten with brown cheese, which tastes like butter but richer, berry jam and whipped cream. This dish is delicious. The reindeer fat makes the waffle rich as if it had foie gras in it, with a slight pungent gamey smell of reindeer. Eaten with the berry jam it is a perfect match and very tasty, but I find that eating it with whipped cream or butter is a little too cloying; the waffle on its own is already delicious. A warm waffle is truly a hearty way to close the meal. The waiter even said that if we were not full, they were ready to refill us, but personally I was already warm and full from this meal.
