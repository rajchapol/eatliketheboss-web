To be honest, I cannot read the name of this restaurant, so let me simply call it LZ. First, the reason I chose it. My friends and I were on a trip and had to transit in Amsterdam, waiting a night for our flight, so we decided to try a proper fine dining restaurant in Europe. I did my research and ended up here for these reasons: 1. It is a modern Dutch restaurant, which suits a visit perfectly, because having arrived in Holland, whatever we tried would surely be the national cuisine.

2. The executive chef is Sidney Schutte, who trained at De Librije, the three-Michelin-star restaurant, before becoming executive chef at AMBER in Hong Kong, where he also earned the restaurant two Michelin stars (it now holds three). 3. It is the first restaurant in the history of the Michelin Guide in the Netherlands to receive two stars at its first assessment. 4. It is the sister restaurant of the legendary Dutch restaurant De Librije, which has received the highest honours a restaurant can hope for, such as three Michelin stars (2018), Gault et Millau Restaurant of the Year (2011) and No. 34 on the World's Best Restaurants list (2018).

We stepped into the building of the Waldorf Astoria, the legendary hotel group, neither small nor large. A waiter who had been waiting to welcome us from the first step through the door guided us to the restaurant, which is one floor down from the lobby. After our coats were put away neatly, the waiter led our party to the table. On the table sat white wood ear mushrooms, which was surprising and left me wondering why (the answer comes at the end). The bread and butter, made in-house from cow's milk and goat's milk, were delicious beyond words: not gamey, but soft, rich and very fragrant. Since we came as a group of more than four, we had to order the restaurant's tasting menu, which is what most people who come to a restaurant like this order anyway. Then we moved quickly on to the wine list. The wine list here is about 100 times thicker than the menu (the menu has two pages, while the wine list has nearly three hundred A5 pages), ranging from red wine from Israel to Lafite (Chateau Lafite Rothschild) in magnum. The prices are rather high with a hefty mark-up, which is normal for a restaurant of this level. However, there are always gaps in the pricing and good-value wines on the list, and if you are too lazy to hunt, the sommelier here is extremely knowledgeable and recommends wines and other drinks very well. Our group settled on a bottle of Burgundy that was not too expensive and of reasonable quality, though certainly not the 1929 Ponsot (a wine-drinkers' joke; if you don't get it, skip it, no offence). The service is superb by the standards of a restaurant of this level. Every dish was served and explained by a most friendly Maître d', who had previously worked at the famous three-Michelin-star restaurant in Shanghai, UltraViolet.

Let's see what is on the menu. We nearly crawled out of the restaurant because the chef went all out, and this meal took us almost five hours.
