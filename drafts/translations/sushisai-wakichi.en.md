Wakichi is another sushi omakase I would like to recommend in Hokkaido. It is not too difficult to book, and the price is not very high by today's standards, at around 36,000 yen, with quality that suits the price. Wakichi once held two Michelin stars in the most recent Hokkaido ranking, and its Tabelog score of 4.3 is the third highest in Sapporo, which is another reason not to miss this restaurant. Among Hokkaido's high-end sushi restaurants, Tamura-san is one of the chefs who speaks the most English, which makes the atmosphere of the meal fairly relaxed and warm inside this small restaurant of 8 seats in the Maruyama Koen area. For foreign guests, the restaurant has a picture book introducing the various ingredients, so that foreigners who are not deep into the subject can enjoy this place too. Tamura-san's sushi is made well, to the standard of the restaurant. The rice is mixed with red vinegar, and at the start it is fairly firm and moist, gradually improving as the course goes on. What impressed me especially were the signature tsumami, such as the steamed egg custard with oyster and kobako crab, which still impresses me to this day.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Chawanmushi
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/01.jpg | Oyster chawanmushi dressed in crab ankake sauce in a ceramic cup beside wasabi, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
Chawanmushi with oyster, dressed with an ankake sauce made from kobako crab. At first I thought this dish would be heavier, but it was light and easy to eat. The gentle richness of the crab fat mixed with dashi made the sauce poured on top. The egg custard was soft and light, the egg aroma not strong, putting the emphasis on the texture and flavour of the oyster. What impressed me in this bowl was that the chef did not overcook the egg, leaving it fairly watery so it would not cover the texture of the oyster. The sweet, pungent wasabi at the finish left a sweet aftertaste in the mouth. A very good dish. Delicious.

### Tako Yawaraka-ni
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/02.jpg | Simmered octopus pieces in sauce on a round cream-coloured plate, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
The tako yawaraka-ni here has a clearly distinctive texture. If you look at the photo, you can see two parts to the texture: an outer skin that is jelly-like but not small, and a core that is crisp but not tough. The sauce is reduced gently, leaning on the saltiness of soy sauce to bring out the sweetness of the octopus. Another dish that was done very well.

### Hirame Kombujime
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/03.jpg | Thinly sliced hirame kombujime sashimi beside orange konowata sauce, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
Today's sashimi began with hirame kombujime, eaten with a sauce made from salt-pickled sea cucumber, konowata and konoko. This was first-class fish. Fine hirame, with firm, crisp flesh. I recommend eating it with soy sauce first. There are two kinds of soy sauce used here, and for this dish the chef used the milder one, which I think brought out the sweetness of the white fish very well. The salt-pickled sea cucumber sauce is more intense and salty, with a slight spicy finish similar to shiokara.

### Hotate
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/04.jpg | Two pieces of scallop sashimi with lime and scallop roe in a ceramic dish, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
The hotate was served in 2 ways. The sashimi came with salt from Awaji Island and lime. For this piece the chef lightly scored the surface, so the scallop is crisp on the outside and soft within, giving contrasting textures. The excellent salt and Japanese lime brought out the sweetness of the scallop wonderfully. Another dish done very well. As for the roe, the chef seasoned it with spring onion oil and soy sauce.

### Kinmedai
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/05.jpg | Charcoal-grilled kinmedai served with daikon oroshi and ponzu sauce on a ceramic plate, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
The next dish was kinmedai, lightly grilled over wood charcoal and served with daikon oroshi, hana hojiso flowers and ponzu sauce. The flowers are spicy. The fish in this dish was again of superb quality, one of the best kinki I have eaten in years. The flesh was juicy, rich and sweet, yet still crisp. The sauce was lightly sour and rather light, so it did not cover the flavour of the fish, and the sweet daikon heaped on top went perfectly with the sauce and the fish. I recommend trying it with the hojiso flowers on the second bite, so that their spiciness keeps the richness from sitting heavily in the mouth, an interesting change of flavour.

### Tachiuo
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/06.jpg | Grilled tachiuo beside fuki no to miso on a reddish-brown plate, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
Next the chef served grilled tachiuo, eaten with fuki no to miso, or fuki flower-bud miso. At first I thought the skin would not be crisp, but it was done very well. The flesh was soft and juicy, and the skin was neither tough nor burnt at all. I recommend trying the fish plain for a little, then with the miso, which is sweet first, then sharply, pungently green, with a distinctive fragrance. It is delicious and goes together very well too.

### Takenoko and Hokke
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/07.jpg | Fresh bamboo shoot from Kumamoto and hokke in agedashi broth with sliced spring onion, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
Next the chef served fresh bamboo shoot, or takenoko, from Kumamoto, with hokke from Okhotsk, served in the style of agedashi. This dish was cooked to the standard of the restaurant, but compared with a good kaiseki restaurant, I was more impressed by the other dishes today.

### Chutoro
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/08.jpg | Deep red chutoro sushi on a pale blue plate beside wasabi, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
Chutoro. This piece was cut fairly thick. The fish was of a quality that suits the price, but the chef ages it for a very long time, making it soft and intense. The flesh may not be very fatty, but it was done well. The rice here uses akazu and is rather hard to shape, with a crunchy texture yet fairly moist, so at the start of the course it may not be so enjoyable. But rice like this is precisely what becomes delicious towards the end of the course.

### Ezo Mae
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/09.jpg | Ezo mae, or cherry salmon, sushi topped with small leaves on a pale blue plate, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
The next piece was one of the distinctive sushi, ezo mae, or sakura masu. The chef served it zuke-style, eaten with negi shoga. It had an intense flavour, and the fish was as soft as butter, which, eaten with the fairly firm rice, made for tremendous fun in the mouth. This dish is quite strongly flavoured, perhaps a little too strong for the start of the course.

### Nishin
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/10.jpg | Nishin sushi topped with vinegar jelly on a pale blue plate, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
Next the chef served nishin. For this piece the chef did not pickle the fish at all, but served it with a vinegar jelly on top. The fish was of superb quality, fresh and soft with no fishiness, and the sour jelly on top, slightly crisp, added texture to the flesh and to the piece in an interesting way. The chef uses this piece to put the brakes on the flavours of the preceding pieces and bring balance to the course, linking it to the next piece, the mild torigai.

### Hokkigai
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/11.jpg | Hokkigai (surf clam) sushi on a pale blue plate beside wasabi and pickled ginger, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
Hokkigai was the next piece, the torigai. The clam was of good quality. The chef scored it slightly to make the flesh softer while keeping it crisp and not tough. The salt and lime seasoning on this piece brought out the sweetness of the clam wonderfully. One of the very delicious pieces.

### Kujira
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/12.jpg | Smoked kujira sprinkled with sliced spring onion on a glass plate beside wasabi, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
Kujira, the unesu cut, smoked, eaten with spring onion, Japanese mustard and shichimi. This is Chef Tamura's signature and must be ordered as an extra. Frankly, it is a perfect match for alcoholic drinks. The whale meat, with its skin scored and blanched before being smoked, was crisp and not too hard or tough, yet still left some texture to chew. The whale meat has a faint smell, softened by a light wood smoke, and the more you chew, the more the umami gradually releases. Eaten with sharp, pungent things like mustard and spring onion, which cover the smell of the whale meat, it was another dish done well.

### Kinki
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/13.jpg | Kinki sushi with orange-red skin on a pale blue plate, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
The next piece was kinki, which the chef lightly seared before serving. I noticed across several dishes, including this piece, that the grilling here gives a fragrance of grilling but no smoky smell. The fish was crisp but not hard, slightly rich and soft. Eaten with spring onion, it went together very well.

### Ebi
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/14.jpg | Prawn sushi on a pale blue plate beside wasabi and pickled ginger, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
The next piece was prawn. The chef served it with grilled prawn fat inside. The prawn was of good quality, to the standard of the restaurant, with the grilled prawn fat giving a gentle fragrance.

### Uni
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/15.jpg | Bafun uni in a small glass cup on a brown saucer, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
Uni, and of course, being in Hokkaido, it had to be the bafun variety. Today I thought the uni was of okay quality, but compared with the restaurant's grade and the other pieces today, this piece seemed to drop off a little.

### Anago
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/16.jpg | Warm anago sushi glazed with sweet sauce on a pale blue plate, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
Anago. The chef served this piece warm. The flesh was fluffy and soft, and the sourness of the rice contrasted with the sweet sauce, which carried a light fragrance of citrus zest. A good piece.

### Maki
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/17.jpg | Chopped tuna and spring onion maki wrapped in seaweed, hand-rolled, held in the hand, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
The restaurant's maki was chopped tuna with spring onion, sesame and julienned takuan, a very generous portion.

### Shiro Miso Soup
image: ./images/sushisai-wakichi/18.jpg | A ceramic bowl of shiro miso soup on a dark table, Sushisai Wakichi, Sapporo
The soup was a light shiro miso.

### Tamago

Sweet omelette.
