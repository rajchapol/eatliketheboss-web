logy is one of the hardest restaurants to book in Taipei. It is helmed by Chef Ryogo Tahara (田原諒悟), has held two Michelin stars for a long time, and is Taiwan's highest-ranked restaurant in Asia's 50 Best Restaurants 2026, at number 22. Since early 2025 the restaurant has moved from a small lane off Anhe Road to the Neihu (內湖) district. The new location is quite a way from the tourist areas, so if you are going, please allow plenty of travel time.

The new dining room was designed by Keiji Ashizawa Design in earthy ochre tones, with wooden furniture by Karimoku. Seating has nearly doubled, there are round tables in the style of Taiwanese Chinese banquet tables, and an open kitchen lets you watch the chefs at work.

As for its origins, logy opened in 2018 when Chef Hiroyasu Kawate of Florilège in Tokyo sent Chef Tahara, his right hand in the kitchen, to open a restaurant in Taipei. The name comes from the English suffix "-logy", meaning the study of something, as in Biology or Psychology: this restaurant is the science of Asian flavour. It also sounds like the Japanese word "roji" (路地), meaning an alley, a nod to the original location. The restaurant earned its first star in 2019 and its second the very next year.

Chef Tahara is from Hokkaido. He began cooking Italian food in Sapporo, then trained in Italy from 2010, before returning to serve as Sous Chef at Florilège for three years. His food is therefore Asian in flavour with a Japanese touch, built on Italian and French technique, using Taiwanese produce that he has travelled to source from almost every district, including the outlying islands. The chef has said he does not want to cook flashy food or use exotic ingredients; he would rather pair familiar things in unexpected ways. This comes through as logy's clear culinary signature, which makes it feel different from its parent restaurant while still carrying a fair amount of Florilège's spirit.

For me, logy's combinations feel more balanced and more interesting, blending Chinese and Japanese influences beautifully. This is without doubt one of the best restaurants in Taiwan, and in the world. No wonder logy is so very hard to book.

Let's take a look at what was served.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Soymilk / Lactic acid / Pine nut
image: ./images/logy/01.jpg | Cream-coloured soy milk custard scattered with pine nuts in a white petal-shaped porcelain cup on a rust-coloured stone slab, logy, Taipei
The first bite is a soy milk tofu custard, similar to annin tofu yet still very much a tofu, a key ingredient in both Chinese and Japanese cooking. On the palate, sourness from clarified tomato leads, before it closes with the rich, nutty notes of pine nut and the distinctive aroma of soybean. An unusual flavour. Personally I felt it was not quite rounded yet: the taste is bright and sour and nutty, with no sweetness. But is it delicious? Yes, it is. An intriguing palate opener.

### Bottarga / Scallop / Butternut
image: ./images/logy/02.jpg | Crisp golden cup topped with a fluffy mound of grated orange mullet roe, on a clear acrylic stand on a rust-coloured stone slab, logy, Taipei
This bite comes as a crispy golden cup. Inside is scallop with a Japanese-leaning yuzu mayo, probably seasoned with a touch of shoyu, topped with Taiwanese mullet roe, or karasumi. In the centre is a wonderfully silky foam that goes beautifully with the softness of the scallop.

### Caviar / Hijiki / Cannelé
image: ./images/logy/03.jpg | Dark brown cannelé topped with large caviar pearls and fine green strands, on a clear acrylic stand, logy, Taipei
A cannelé, the pastry from Bordeaux, made in a savoury version with hijiki seaweed and topped with lightly salty French caviar, cut by a gentle, cool sharpness of wasabi. Delicious.

### Oyster / Mint / Hazelnut 🕶️
image: ./images/logy/04.jpg | Fresh green peas, green cream, a purple borage flower and white and pale green granita on a cream plate, logy, Taipei
An unusual combination that comes together brilliantly. The oyster has a pronounced taste of the sea, and with the hazelnut oil it simply works, in a way that is hard to explain. There are sweet, crunchy peas, a creamy ricotta granita and a cool, refreshing mint granita. A clever play of flavours with real depth: the tastes seem to contrast, yet stay rounded and easy to understand. Excellent.

### Abalone / Apricot kernel / Gamtae
image: ./images/logy/05.jpg | Pale creamy soup in a white porcelain bowl with abalone, white wood ear fungus, morels and an orange seaweed crisp, logy, Taipei
A soup of Chinese almond milk, or apricot kernel (杏仁), simmered with chicken and abalone stock, with abalone, white wood ear fungus and morels stuffed with minced chicken, topped with saffron oil. Gamtae is a Korean seaweed. Served piping hot. Unusual, fragrant with almond, with umami and collagen from the chicken.

### Mackerel / Bamboo shoot / Blue cheese
image: ./images/logy/06.jpg | Mochi covered in shaved black truffle with a light brown cream sauce, on a white plate, logy, Taipei
Mochi filled with mackerel grilled over a rice-straw fire in the warayaki style, with diced bamboo shoots. The sauce is made from Gorgonzola and Kyoto miso, with truffle shaved generously over the top. In the mouth there is a hint of smoke, the rich, tender mackerel against the crunch of bamboo shoot, and a creamy sauce that matches the glutinous rice aroma of the mochi perfectly. The chef balances the Gorgonzola and the sweet miso very well indeed. Delicious.

### Eel / Chocolate / Green sichuan pepper 🕶️
image: ./images/logy/07.jpg | Clear brown soup being poured over eel tempura topped with fresh mint leaves, on a green herb sauce in a cream bowl, logy, Taipei
The restaurant's signature crispy eel. Crisp eel tempura with a light scent of sansho, with mushroom soup poured over at the table. The soup leads with sourness, then sweetness and umami, with a gentle mala oil lingering on the tip of the tongue, and it goes well with the crisp-skinned grilled eel. It is eaten with fresh mint leaves and a cream purée of 100% Taiwanese chocolate. Great depth, light, crisp batter. Excellent, and delicious. The bitter, fruity chocolate goes unbelievably well with the kabayaki-style grilled eel.

### Lamb / Mala-Spiced sauce / Taiwanese tea rice
image: ./images/logy/08.jpg | Grilled lamb topped with a seasonal salad, white and yellow flowers and thinly shaved vegetables, on a white plate, logy, Taipei
The main course comes with rice cooked in a pot with Taiwanese oolong tea leaves, using Yumepirika rice from Hokkaido, served with lamb saddle and a seasonal salad. The sauce is the restaurant's own mala blend of 15 herbs; it tastes like a jus, with no numbing heat, only the aroma. The grilled lamb is crisp and rich with no gaminess at all, though personally I found the meat not quite as tender as it might be. It is a style with texture, something to chew on.

### Loquat / Oriental beauty / IPA beer
image: ./images/logy/09.jpg | Orange dome of jelly sprinkled with thyme in amber tea, in the centre of a wide-rimmed white plate, logy, Taipei
Loquat jelly with Dongfang Meiren tea, or Oriental Beauty, a Taiwanese oolong with notes of honey and fruit, mixed with IPA beer and thyme. Light and refreshing, not very sour, but with a complex, cool fragrance of hops, tea leaves and mint. Unusual.

### Buckwheat / Lily bulb / Olive oil
image: ./images/logy/10.jpg | White domed dessert on a base of puffed buckwheat, topped with yellow olive oil, on an oval white plate, logy, Taipei
This dessert brings together puffed buckwheat, soba seed ice cream and olive oil, eaten with lily bulb (百合根), which the Japanese and Chinese like to use in sweets. The soft, gently sweet lily bulb and the earthy soba go together surprisingly well, with the olive oil tying them together.
