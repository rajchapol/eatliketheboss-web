Shanghai is the birthplace of xiaolongbao, so for this meal let me take you to experience true craftsmanship at Wu You Xian (屋有鲜), a name that translates literally as "a house brimming with freshness". This is not a place that simply serves everyday dim sum. It raises xiaolongbao into a work of gastronomic art, enough to earn a Michelin Star, unlike the typical street-food spot, which usually gets no further than a Bib Gourmand.

Behind this finesse is Chef Chen Lina, a seasoned master of dim sum. She began her career at a legendary Shanghai restaurant, then left at 19 to gain experience in Chinese restaurants in Japan, before finally coming home to open her own. The influence of the Land of the Rising Sun shows clearly in her philosophy, from the care of picking fresh crab meat every morning to a spirit of "Omotenashi" hospitality: the chef still stands on duty, warmly guiding guests herself.

The menu offers only a handful of dumplings, but every one is made to a high standard, with the focus on pure ingredients and meticulous cooking, and on a deliberately designed progression of eating and of shifting flavours on the palate. Each xiaolongbao has its own sauce: the restaurant serves specially blended vinegars, aged for different lengths of time, a different one for each dish, along with lemon water to cleanse the palate afterwards. Every basket is seasoned with flavours that seem light and delicate, yet cleverly hide real depth and dimension. The care taken over the ratio of dough, filling and each type of vinegar, and the separation of different parts of the crab for each bite, gives every mouthful its own flavour and character. That is why this is the benchmark for xiaolongbao that any true food lover must experience in person, and I can say with full confidence that this restaurant truly deserves its star.

Wu You Xian turns xiaolongbao from ordinary dim sum into an art for the palate. If you are planning to explore Shanghai, this is a destination not to be missed.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Huntun Tang (Chinese Wonton Soup)
image: ./images/wu-you-xian/01.jpg | Tiny wontons in a clear broth sprinkled with sliced spring onion in a white porcelain bowl, Wu You Xian, Shanghai
Tiny wontons filled with superb crab meat, served in a gentle chicken broth with a thin film of ginger oil on top. The soft sweetness of the broth melds beautifully with the fine soy sauce that stands out in the filling. A warming bowl, and a wonderful way to wake up the palate.

### Hairy Crab Xiaolongbao
image: ./images/wu-you-xian/02.jpg | Six hairy crab xiaolongbao in a bamboo steamer, their pleated tops crowned with orange crab roe, Wu You Xian, Shanghai
The skin looks thick, yet it is soft in the mouth and holds the broth without tearing. What makes it special is that each basket uses three different parts of the crab, each with its own aroma and texture. The staff suggested adding two spoonfuls of vinegar from the green cup. Bite in, and the crab broth bursts with sweetness and a gentle umami, followed by the fragrance of ginger and a sweet-sour sauce that cuts through the richness. The crab roe is as rich as a fine sauce, yet melts away cleanly, leaving no lingering fishiness.

### Dalian Abalone Xiaolongbao 🕶️
A meeting of superb ingredients: baby abalone from Dalian and conpoy (dried scallop). The abalone has a crisp, springy bite without ever feeling tough, set skilfully against the softness of the dough. It comes with the vinegar in the black bowl, richer than the green one, yet it never masks the umami of the abalone and conpoy packed into the broth.
