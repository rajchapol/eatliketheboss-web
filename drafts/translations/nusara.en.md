This is my return visit after the restaurant won its star. Amid the timeless charm of the historic Tha Tian district, which feels solemn, sacred and romantic all at once, **Nusara (นุสรา)** was born in an old shophouse brought back to life as a temple of fine dining Thai food. The restaurant weaves together the love and memories of **Chef Ton Thitid Tassanakajohn** and his younger brother, Khun Tam (Chaisiri), created in honour of their late grandmother, "Khun Yai Nusara".

The heart of the place is **"Modern Thai Cuisine"**, taking traditional Thai recipes and reinterpreting them with modern, refined techniques, to showcase Thailand's finest ingredients to international guests. The flavours here remain Thai and punchy, but adjusted a little to be contemporary and international. Tastes rarely found in Thai food, such as astringency, are used to balance the meal across the course in an interesting way. Chef Ton, who graduated with the highest honours from the Culinary Institute of America (CIA) and has worked at three-Michelin-star restaurants such as Eleven Madison Park and Jean-Georges, has led Nusara to a great many international awards, including a Michelin Star, Asia's 50 Best Restaurants and Tatler Best of Asia. Chef Ton is also the only Thai chef in the world to hold Michelin stars at two restaurants, Le Du and Nusara.

The journey and experience across the meal here are among the best in Thailand. It begins in the Lounge downstairs with a Welcome Drink, before you are walked through the kitchen to enjoy a Starter. Then you move up to the Rooftop for another set of canapés with Champagne Krug, while taking in the priceless panoramic view of the prang of Wat Arun and Wat Pho at sunset, before moving into the Main Dining Hall for the main menu. Many dishes are presented with the real ingredients shown to the international guests, a wonderful detail that impressed me greatly. Although the foreign waiting staff may sometimes make the context in which Thai food is presented drift a little from the roots of the food, the service is very attentive and tidy. Another highlight of the restaurant is the Wine Pairing by Khun Tam, which is done well, setting Old World wines against Thai food with its heat and shrimp paste in an impressive way. In particular, pairing a Clos de la Roche grand cru and a white wine with the crab curry impresses me every single time.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Grilled Pork with Wild Betel Leaf
image: ./images/nusara/01.jpg | Grilled pork with wild betel leaf served on banana leaf in a clay pot, Nusara, Bangkok
It starts with a powerful bite: grilled pork tossed in a curry paste with clear herbal notes and the aroma of the paste itself, topped with sliced cha muang leaves (garcinia). The curry paste is not too strong, while the gentle astringency of the cha muang leaves cools the heat and stimulates the appetite very well.

### Longan with Squid Tentacles
image: ./images/nusara/02.jpg | Chilled stuffed longan on crushed ice, Nusara, Bangkok
A dish inspired by an ancient recipe, stir-fried longan, but served in a surprising cold form. Longans are pitted and stuffed with coconut milk, nuts and squid tentacles, giving a complex texture that is fun to chew.

### Carrot and Chor Som
image: ./images/nusara/03.jpg | Carrot and chor som in a cut-glass bowl under an orange carrot sauce, Nusara, Bangkok
A dish designed to go with an appetite-stimulating drink such as Krug. It stands out with a clear aroma of dried squid that is not salty, and is served with "chor som", adapted from the ancient Thai dish chor muang, filled with minced snakehead fish and topped with a sauce made from carrot juice and calamansi (som chi) to add brightness (Bright), giving a flavour that is Thai and cosmopolitan at once.

### Tartare Laab Mueang (Northern-style Tartare)
image: ./images/nusara/04.jpg | Laab mueang tartare in a red cone stuck into a black stone bucket, Nusara, Bangkok
A northern-style beef tartare served in a cone made of tomato juice, featuring delicious Sakon Nakhon wagyu seasoned with Chiang Mai laab spice and phak phaeo (Vietnamese coriander), giving a well-rounded, balanced flavour.

### Varieties of Lotus
image: ./images/nusara/05.jpg | Varieties of lotus arranged as a purple lotus flower and stalks on a plate, Nusara, Bangkok
A dish that shows a "decisive" commitment to presenting every part of the lotus as several small dishes. It starts with lotus stems, both underwater and above water, seasoned with coconut milk and shrimp paste, firm-fleshed surf clams and som saa (Thai bitter orange), giving full umami and a crunchy bite. Next comes a smooth lotus root purée, eaten with dried fish and a lightly crisp curry paste, then lotus seeds, softly sweet with honey. It closes with a clear soup of lotus petal tea seasoned a little like tom khlong, with a light astringency and a faint lotus aroma, which cleanses the palate superbly.

### Spicy Tropical Fruit Salad & Whole Squid
image: ./images/nusara/06.jpg | Stuffed squid with fruit on a blue ceramic plate, Nusara, Bangkok
A dish inspired by turning a dessert into a savoury course. Chewy squid is stuffed with firm sticky rice stir-fried in coconut milk, brightened by several kinds of fruit, including mangosteen, chilled watermelon jelly and pineapple. It is refreshing, complex, very multi-dimensional, and not fishy at all.

### Blue Swimming Crab Curry & Crispy Garuda Leaves
image: ./images/nusara/07.jpg | Golden-yellow crab curry with oozing roe in a blue-and-white bowl, Nusara, Bangkok
A yellow curry with gleaming golden turmeric, packed with perfectly cooked crab meat, sea crab roe and horseshoe crab eggs. The flavour leads with sweetness, with heat that is fiery and punchy to the core, the characteristic aroma of a coconut curry paste, and an astringent finish. It is served with fried garuda leaves (bai lep khrut) tossed in curry paste, whose bitterness and distinctive aroma cut the flavour well, along with crispy fried kung ten (tiny river shrimp) for added texture.

### Tom Kha, Gourami Fish & Poached Mushroom
image: ./images/nusara/08.jpg | Tom kha with gourami fish in a white ceramic bowl with a brown rim, Nusara, Bangkok
A tom kha that arrives as a thick, creamy body like an espuma. It looks dense, but on the palate it is light, not heavy as you might expect. Slightly sweet, finishing with a gentle sourness and the cool fragrance of galangal, it has every flavour of a tom kha. It is made more distinctive by a mild chilli oil, springy river prawns and crispy fried gourami that adds texture.

### Guwa Malayan Curry & Dry-Aged Fish
image: ./images/nusara/09.jpg | Guwa Malayan curry with crisp-skinned dry-aged fish and vegetables in an orange broth, Nusara, Bangkok
A southern curry influenced by the Malay tradition, with no coconut milk and a clearer broth than a typical southern curry. It is fiery and more sour than southern kaeng som. It is served with dry-aged fish, soft and firm with crisp skin, alongside an assortment of vegetables, including okra, lotus stem, pickles and mushrooms, where the sweet-and-sour pickles act as a brake on the heat, making this dish complete in flavour and wonderfully rounded.

### Fiddlehead Salad & Smoked Clam
image: ./images/nusara/10.jpg | Fiddlehead salad with smoked clams in a black bowl, Nusara, Bangkok
Fiddlehead fern blanched to just the right point, dressed in a yam that leads with sweetness, with salt and heat, full in flavour, and doing well as a reducer of the chilli heat. A lightly smoked, springy clam adds another dimension and makes this salad even more complete.

### Nam Phrik of the Four Regions
image: ./images/nusara/11.jpg | Nam phrik of the four regions arranged as small bites on a glossy black plate, Nusara, Bangkok
Four nam phrik from four regions, such as nam phrik num and nam phrik pla ra, presented as beautiful small bites with their accompaniments. The flavour is Thai but perhaps not very punchy. These are refined nam phrik that are fun to eat, good for a little taste.

### Sour Leaf Soup
image: ./images/nusara/12.jpg | Clear sour leaf soup being poured into a blue bowl, Nusara, Bangkok
A clear, light soup with umami and a mild sourness, not very astringent, easy to sip and gentle on the stomach.

### Thai Wagyu and Red Holy Basil
image: ./images/nusara/13.jpg | Stir-fried wagyu in red holy basil sauce on a blue-and-white plate, Nusara, Bangkok
The chef chooses Thai wagyu, whose beefy aroma leads, topped with a rich sauce that is sweet first with heat at the tip of the tongue, and the fragrance of red holy basil appearing now and then.

### Varieties of Mangoes
image: ./images/nusara/14.jpg | Varieties of mango sorbet in a free-form white ceramic bowl, Nusara, Bangkok
Refreshment through mango in many forms: mango sorbet from young green mango (mamuang bao), mango candy, ripe mango mousse and fresh mango. The sourness of the sorbet contrasts with the sweetness of the cream perfectly.

### Sago & Young Coconut
image: ./images/nusara/15.jpg | Sago with young coconut sprinkled with almonds in a ceramic bowl, Nusara, Bangkok
The restaurant's Signature dish, using true tree sago, which gives a texture different from ordinary sago.

### Petit Four & Seasonal Fruit
image: ./images/nusara/16.jpg | Petit fours and seasonal fruit on a three-tier rattan stand, Nusara, Bangkok
The meal closes magnificently and completely with a Thai tea ice cream sandwich, fish sauce caramel chocolate, layered pandan cake with sangkhaya, tong ten jelly, and guava with sweet fish sauce.
