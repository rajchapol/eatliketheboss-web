Another restaurant that has everything except the final Michelin star. The experience today was impressive, from before entering the restaurant right to the end of the meal.

Kadeau is another restaurant that has held two Michelin stars for a long time, along with a Green Star, and it appears on the World's 50 Best list of the top 100 restaurants, at No. 91.

Even before we entered, the reception team standing at the door welcomed us warmly, joking and trying to chat with us. They then took us past the restaurant's open kitchen before finally leading us to our table.

Kadeau tries to take care of every detail: light bulbs that photograph beautifully without the moiré of some LEDs, an aroma diffuser of lavender mixed with geranium that matches the hand towels, and, the point that impressed me most, a waiter who, though not a sommelier, could expertly recommend wines from a massive wine list according to the taste profile we gave. Today I tried Taille aux Loups, 'Clos de Mosny; Montlouis-sur-Loire. For drinks here, I recommend wine by the bottle, as many bloggers do, which seems better value than the pairing, whose fierce price is almost as much as the course itself.

When I went it was the start of winter, and the restaurant was serving the Preservation menu, which leans heavily on fermentation and pickling of all kinds, for a total of 5100 DKK. I must admit this is one of the most expensive restaurants in the world, but the menu served is very generous and uses truly good ingredients, especially the seafood. There were several interesting dishes, such as oysters and leaves, raw shrimps and canned figs, and cherry leaf with havgus and ham.

The dish I loved most today is the signature smoked salmon, which the chef cures with shio koji, then cold-smokes over cherry wood for three days, then hot-smokes every day to add texture. The dish is served in two ways: the slightly crisp skin comes with a variety of pickled vegetables, while the inner flesh, which almost melts in the mouth, is served with lavender butter.

Honestly, the restaurant divides opinion; some love it very much and some dislike it entirely. Personally, I think the food here, especially this dish, is very fermented. The technique is at about the level of a three-star restaurant, but the service starts to falter when the restaurant is busy, the cutlery is unsuited to certain dishes, and there were tiny specks of dust on the wine glass that stood out because of the restaurant's lighting.

## The art of taste in this meal

### tomatoes, crab and gooseberries
image: ./images/kadeau/01.jpg | A sun-dried tomato stuffed with crab on a round plate set in a nest of twigs, Kadeau, Copenhagen
The first dish is a sun-dried tomato stuffed with brown crab, eaten with dried gooseberries and elderflower butter. It is served at room temperature. We get a sweet-and-sour lead, set against the sweet-and-sour of the cape gooseberry; two different chewy textures contrast in the mouth, and the crab provides savouriness, before everything is brought together by the richness of the butter, which doesn't feel heavy or cloying because it is cut by the sourness of the pickles. Superb.

The next dish was a seafood platter. Apologies for the photo, which I took after I had wiped it clean.

### razor clam with rose and saffron
image: ./images/kadeau/02.jpg | Empty razor clam shells and seaweed in a nest of twigs, Kadeau, Copenhagen
Razor clam from the Netherlands, braised with pear and kombu, with a clear sweet-and-sour taste from the pear and the umami of the seaweed adding balance, before being seasoned with a little salt. Very delicious.

### cockles and hazelnuts
Danish cockles served with hazelnut milk. This one is odd, but good. The almond milk helps tame the bloody smell of the cockles, which are of excellent quality with a springy, chewy flesh, interestingly done.

### queen scallop with blackcurrants
Scallop from Norway, very intensely sweet, its intensity matching that of the slightly astringent pickled blackcurrants. Done very well again.

### cured squid
Squid cured with kombu and salt, served with magnolia oil and rice bran. The squid's texture is creamy and chewy, not fishy, with a salty umami.

### oysters and leaves
image: ./images/kadeau/06.jpg | White foam with a black mussel and green leaves in a brown earthenware dish, Kadeau, Copenhagen
For the next dish the chef chose to serve cabbage with oysters that have been confited, before serving them with an oyster emulsion. In this dish we get the crisp, sweet, but not hard cabbage, the firm sweetness of top-class oysters, and a finish of salty sea aroma from the emulsion.

On the other side is pickled shiso leaf stuffed with leek, served with coriander seeds. This bite is more intense than the first and should be eaten afterwards. You get the full distinctive flavour of the vegetable, a salty intensity led by umami, before closing with the crunch of the coriander seeds. This dish may be a little strange, but it is very delicious.

### cherry leaf with havgus and ham
image: ./images/kadeau/07.jpg | A cherry leaf on a dark brown round of dough on a wooden tray, Kadeau, Copenhagen
Cherry leaves from Bornholm, which the restaurant's team harvested themselves in spring and dried, served with havgus cheese aged 24 months, lightly sun-dried pork, sesame, yeast emulsion and beetroot, and cherry blossom oil.

At first I thought it would be dry in the mouth, but not at all. The cherry leaf is crisp but not hard, which is odd. The toasted black sesame inside adds fragrance and crunch. The meat is rustic, gently salty and mellow; there is the aroma of the yeast foam, the earthiness of the beetroot, and a cherry aroma made clearer by the oil. A dish that looks simple but is complex and well resolved.

### raw shrimps and canned figs
image: ./images/kadeau/08.jpg | Thinly sliced raw shrimp and figs on white cream in a round dish, Kadeau, Copenhagen
This dish is puzzling, but it is delicious and fits together effortlessly. The chef serves shrimp from Norway with two kinds of fruit that have been slightly dried to concentrate them, and nuts.

What I liked is the cream underneath, which smells a little like green curry but isn't spicy, mellow and creamy, yet it fits perfectly with the other ingredients and helps blend all the flavours together. The lightly salted roasted walnuts bring out the shrimp superbly and add texture to the dish, an interesting way of bringing out the sweetness of shrimp with nuts. The figs and strawberries add dimension with an intense sweetness.

**hot and cold smoked salmon with lavender**

### Signature
image: ./images/kadeau/09.jpg | Smoked salmon in a stone bowl in front of a slab with orange salmon, Kadeau, Copenhagen
The smoked salmon, which the chef cures with shio koji, then cold-smokes over cherry wood for three days, then hot-smokes every day to add texture. The dish is carved at the table and served in two ways: the slightly crisp part, the salmon crust, is served with pickled vegetables such as rapeseed, white asparagus and cucumber.

To call it the best smoked salmon in the world would not be an exaggeration for this dish. The inner flesh, which almost melts in the mouth, still lingers in my memory today: the aroma of salmon, the juiciness of the flesh, a gentle saltiness, and the cherry-wood smoke blending perfectly in the mouth with lavender, with a crisp onion that has a slight pungency and a little sweetness to help cut the salmon.

The skin has a texture similar to Taro fish snacks yet remains juicy, chewy, with a more intense flavour than the inner flesh and a clearer woodsmoke. I recommend eating it as a salad with the various pickled vegetables, which help cut the fish. Personally, the flavour of this dish may be a little too intense.

### horse mussel waffle
image: ./images/kadeau/10.jpg | An orange waffle on a patterned plate beside a shell on a wooden table, Kadeau, Copenhagen
The waffle is served with buckwheat, pumpkin seeds, pumpkin and horse mussel emulsion.

The waffle here has the fragrance of roasted buckwheat, a crisp skin and a dry crumb, slightly sweet, a little buttery, with a faint smoky aroma like dried fish, truly distinctive. Eaten with the assorted grains it is very fun in the mouth. Only one flaw: some hard buckwheat grains came loose.

### apples with osetra royal caviar
image: ./images/kadeau/11.jpg | Apples with caviar in a glass bowl decorated with ears of dried grass, Kadeau, Copenhagen
The chef serves apples cooked in brown butter, with generous caviar, sunflower seeds and sour cream. This dish is up to the restaurant's standard: sweet apple, salty caviar, with cream helping to blend the flavours, a little richness and the crunch of sunflower seeds, and an apple oil that makes the aroma more complex.

### grilled root vegetables with bee pollen
image: ./images/kadeau/12.jpg | Grilled root vegetables with bee pollen in a yellow bowl surrounded by foam, Kadeau, Copenhagen
Assorted root vegetables such as carrot, beetroot and dried beetroot, with a sauce of mussel jus, yeast and brown butter. The sauce in this dish is done very well: savoury, mellowly salty with a light creaminess, and the yeast aroma links the flavour of the butter and the vegetables interestingly. The vegetables are sweet, with many textures, crisp, soft and chewy, and the pickles on top give sourness. Very good. A little ham is tucked in, adding saltiness, chewiness and smoke. Meaty, and very delicious.

### maitake pie
image: ./images/kadeau/13.jpg | Maitake mushroom resting on red autumn leaves, Kadeau, Copenhagen
The pastry is made from rye flour, served with duck leg and maitake mushrooms. The duck in this dish is wild duck from Denmark. I like the use of rye flour, which gives the pastry an interesting, distinctive aroma. The earthy aroma of wild mushrooms and the aroma of butter blend perfectly with the springy, crisp duck meat.

### wild duck with chestnuts
image: ./images/kadeau/14.jpg | Sliced grilled wild duck with sauce and berries on a ceramic plate, Kadeau, Copenhagen
The chef chose breast of Danish wild duck, grilled gently over charcoal before seasoning with salt, served with autumn produce such as chestnuts, walnuts and plums, and a sauce made from duck bones, chicken wings and wild berries.

In this dish the duck is firm and chewy, with a smoky aroma and a light saltiness. The sauce has dimension and is very distinctive: beyond the umami of an ordinary jus, it has a viscosity from the collagen in the chicken bones, a faint sweet-and-sour of berries, and a herb aroma that I'm not sure is sage or lemon balm, which adds dimension to the dish with finesse. Personally I felt this dish was too plain compared with the others.

Preserved fruits. A variety of berries from Bornholm, such as blackcurrant, white currant, green strawberry, mulberry and pear, served in many ways: in syrup, dried, frozen, candied. A variety of berry flavours is perhaps the best description of this dish.

### berry kelp
image: ./images/kadeau/15.jpg | A black round sheet on white cream in a round ceramic dish, Kadeau, Copenhagen
Here the chef serves pork fat, seaweed, cream and pine nuts underneath. The seaweed is similar to kombu, firm and chewy, seasoned lightly sweet. The pork fat is lightly seasoned like Italian lardo, served chilled, fudgy and chewy; a good idea. The more you chew, the more the umami of the seaweed comes out, but personally I found the texture too hard and the cream didn't go.

### cloudberry tart
image: ./images/kadeau/16.jpg | A small tart topped with orange cloudberries on a white plate beside a cup of cream, Kadeau, Copenhagen
A wheat tart with cream and cloudberries. The tart shell smells of butter and berries, distinctive, sweet and not very sour; the cream gives a yoghurt-like sourness. A light way to close the meal.
