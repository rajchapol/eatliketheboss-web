Ta Vie is like a simple family business: the husband cooks and the wife serves, and including the owners there are only 12 staff in all, yet the restaurant has been honoured with the highest distinction, three Michelin stars. It is rumoured that this is why the restaurant took so long to win its third star, but after today's experience I have to say that in technique and subtlety, Ta Vie is truly world-class.

This Hong Kong trip was the first of the year, a spur-of-the-moment trip because A had got a free ticket. We had barely 24 hours on the island, not counting sleep, so my main goal was the three-star restaurants I had not yet tried in HK, and Ta Vie was one of the two. Thanks to A, this review came to be.

I had almost made it to Ta Vie twice, but for various reasons chose another restaurant, so this place means a great deal to me.

Ta Vie won its first star in the year the guide opened, 2016, then its second the following year, and last year, 2023, it finally earned three, the fruit of Chef Hideaki Sato's efforts at last. I had read the story of the chef practising making Peking duck hundreds of times during Covid to make it perfect, and I corresponded by email with the chef's wife, Hiromi Takano, from booking the table and sending the menu so we could flag allergies or dislikes, to rescheduling the reservation when a flight was cancelled. I could tell that the chef and his wife are extremely dedicated, thoroughly prepared and attentive to the details of the food, so that the dining experience would be flawless. Though the team may be short-staffed, I felt that today's experience lacked nothing. The chef serves and clears our plates himself at every course, alongside his wife, who is in charge of the front of house and recommends the wines, where we found several interesting bottles from China and Japan. The one I tried on her recommendation was the Izutsu barrel-aged Chardonnay, from one of the oldest wineries in Japan.

Ta Vie is a Western restaurant with a blend of several nations: French, following the chef's background, a touch of Italian, and Japanese, the chef's homeland. The price may be a little high (well, not a little) at 2,980 HKD, but the ingredients are superb throughout, with several so good that I was stunned, such as the torigai and the scallops, better than at most omakase restaurants, or the veal from Piedmont. Above all, though, it is the superb cooking of Chef Hideaki Sato. Every sauce that day impressed me enormously: complex and multidimensional yet rounded and very balanced. Another highlight is the bread, made with a special dough from fermented rice bran, inspired by Japanese pickles, and fine flour from Hokkaido, producing a bread with a special texture unlike anything I have encountered.

Let's see what the meal holds.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Sourdough ricotta olive oil & homemade butter
image: ./images/ta-vie/01.jpg | Sourdough bread, ricotta and homemade butter in a blue-and-white porcelain bowl, Ta Vie, Hong Kong
The bread here is served with ricotta cheese, olive oil and homemade butter. It is a special recipe using natural yeast and a special dough made with fermented rice bran, inspired by Japanese pickles, and fine flour from Hokkaido, producing a bread with a special texture unlike anything I have encountered. The inside is soft and chewy like mochi, fragrant with wheat flour, and the thin crust is beautifully even and crisp. Superb.

### Japanese winter turnip salad, 36 months aged Parma ham, and grilled Hokki clam
image: ./images/ta-vie/02.jpg | Kabu turnip salad with Parma ham on a white shell-shaped plate, Ta Vie, Hong Kong
For this dish the chef served a salad of diced kabu turnip, lightly dressed, with torigai in two ways, grilled and raw, topped with three-year-aged Parma ham. The chef chose to serve it with a consommé made from the torigai, and recommended drinking the soup first.

This dish looks like nothing, yet it is divine. I can still recall the flavour of that day. When the restaurant sent the menu, I could not imagine how kabu, Parma ham and torigai would come together.

First, the ingredients here are superb. This may well be one of the most delicious torigai dishes I have ever eaten. The clam is very sweet and juicy. The chef chose to grill it lightly, with a faint charcoal smoke, seasoned with salt and a peppery heat that lifts the clam's sweetness perfectly. Since the clam is served at two levels of doneness, we get both chewiness and crispness in the mouth. The distinctive, earthy aroma of kabu (to me it is like a cross between daikon and turnip) is blended gently into a lemon butter sauce with diced tomato. Eaten with the Parma ham, its soft saltiness lifts the clam's sweetness and adds dimension wonderfully. Brutal.

### Winter snow crab and artichoke gratin
image: ./images/ta-vie/03.jpg | Snow crab gratin baked in an artichoke on a white plate, Ta Vie, Hong Kong
This dish is snow crab from Tottori baked as a gratin with French artichoke. The gratin was served warm, almost hot, and the chef chose to season it lightly, focusing on the flavour of the ingredients. The light, gently creamy flavour brings out and blends the distinctive taste of the artichoke with the sweetness of the superb crab meat perfectly. On top, a thin layer of mild, melting cheese, breadcrumbs and sesame not only add fragrance but also dimension and texture, and lend the dish a beautiful Asian touch. The chef balances this dish with great subtlety and precision. Superb.

### Burnt Japanese winter leek with Oscietra caviar
image: ./images/ta-vie/04.jpg | Grilled Japanese leek topped with caviar and mushrooms on a blue wave-patterned plate, Ta Vie, Hong Kong
Next the chef chose to serve Yamanashi winter leek with caviar, onion and croutons. This was again very delicious, and the ingredient is excellent. The leek is exceptionally sweet, with a very soft, almost melting texture. The crunch of the caviar brings a complex saltiness, and its gentle sea aroma blends perfectly with the leek's own distinctive scent, before thin crisp bread adds dimension. The cream sauce beneath ties the dish together, rounded and balanced. Another very good dish, though compared with the complexity of the previous two it may seem a little simple.

### House-made fresh pasta with "Aonori" sauce topped with premium Hokkaido uni
image: ./images/ta-vie/05.jpg | Fresh pasta topped with Hokkaido sea urchin in a wide white dish, Ta Vie, Hong Kong
Today's uni was the bafun variety, from the "Ogawa" brand, harvested in Hamanaka village, on top of an aonori sauce. Although several restaurants make this dish, I must say that of all I have eaten, none comes close to this Ta Vie dish. The house-made fresh pasta is of excellent quality, chewy but not too firm, evenly cooked through, slippery and springy against the teeth, and fragrant with flour. The seaweed sauce leads with saltiness and a gentle umami, bursting with seaweed and the sea in the mouth just like in a cartoon, contrasting with the sweet, delicate uni, and finishing with the freshness of olive oil. Superb.

### Pan-seared fresh Brittany blue-lobster with pied blue mushroom, "Tubu clam" Japanese whelk, and "Gobo" burdock
image: ./images/ta-vie/06.jpg | Grilled lobster with mushrooms and cream sauce on a white plate with a wavy rim, Ta Vie, Hong Kong
Next the chef chose to serve Brittany blue lobster, seared to perfection to three-star standard, the flesh bouncy, crisp and intensely sweet. The tsubugai (Japanese whelk) is grilled delicately, evenly cooked throughout with a very soft texture. The chef seasoned it slightly salty to cut against the sweetness of the lobster. Another thing worth praising is the beurre blanc, which is excellent, with the aroma of white wine and cream butter blending perfectly with the distinctive earthy scent of the burdock (gobo). Don't think of it as an ordinary beurre blanc: it is rounded, balanced and very multidimensional, bringing out the flavour of the various ingredients superbly without stealing the spotlight. Once again the ingredients in this dish are excellent. The accompaniment, pied bleu mushroom, is hard to find, but above all it is the formidable skill of Chef Hideaki Sato.

### Roasted "Fassona" veal from Piemonte, with pumpkin gnocchi and celeriac, served with black truffle
image: ./images/ta-vie/07.jpg | Roast veal with black truffle, greens and pumpkin sauce, Ta Vie, Hong Kong
The main course today was Fassona veal, the native breed of the Piedmont region. This breed is distinguished by having little connective tissue, which makes the meat very tender, and being veal, it must be more tender still. The chef chose to serve it with Alba black truffle and pumpkin gnocchi.

This dish was well made, but compared with the earlier ones I felt it was a little ordinary. The superb veal is roasted very tender, with a faint aroma of veal, and the sauce is rounded and not so heavy that it overpowers the delicate flavour of the veal and the fragrance of the excellent truffle.

### Palate-Cleansing Tea Blend
image: ./images/ta-vie/08.jpg | A blended tea in a clear glass pot on a warmer in the centre of the table, Ta Vie, Hong Kong
Our palate cleanser was the restaurant's own tea blend, made from osmanthus flowers, orange peel and roselle. It was delicious: A and I refilled twice. The orange peel aroma and the sweet-and-sour roselle, served cold, really kept our stomachs from feeling too full.

### Japanese white strawberry with pistachio cream pistachio sable, white meringue
image: ./images/ta-vie/09.jpg | Two white strawberry desserts on a white plate, Ta Vie, Hong Kong
For this dish the chef served white strawberries with strawberry sauce, meringue and pistachio sherbet. A simple dish, but done well. The strawberry sauce is gently sweet and does not overpower the excellent white strawberries, whose mouth-watering fragrance hits the nose. The slight creaminess of the pistachio and the sweetness of the meringue go well with it.

### Chocolate crêpe soufflé, with Tasmania cherry jam, Tonka beans flavored ice cream
image: ./images/ta-vie/10.jpg | Chocolate dessert with red cherry jam and vanilla ice cream on a grey stone plate, Ta Vie, Hong Kong
A crêpe cake and soufflé in the centre. The crêpe skin is crisp, the centre fluffy and light, neither mushy nor too dry, with the fragrance of excellent cocoa. The Tasmanian cherry and wine sauce has a precious sweet-and-sour taste, and the cherry's distinctive aroma stays remarkably fresh, which pairs interestingly with the wine. The tonka bean ice cream is lightly rich, slightly sweet and not very creamy.
