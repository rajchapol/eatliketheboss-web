> A grand restaurant comes with a grand price

Our first restaurant of this trip was Le Palais, Taiwan's first three-Michelin-star restaurant, which has held its three stars for a long time. It is one of the most expensive Chinese restaurants in the world, and the reviews from several Western bloggers seemed rather negative, which made me want to try it for myself. Ha!

The restaurant is on the top floor of the Palais de Chine hotel, decorated with luxurious black wooden furniture in a Chinese style with a hint of the West. The décor is truly superb, even compared with restaurants of the same level, and for me the atmosphere here is a genuinely special place.

On service, Le Palais does not let down the three-Michelin-star institution. The staff are attentive to detail, polished and constantly watching for guests' needs. There is even a beauty light for taking photos, and the menu recommendations and dish descriptions were delivered so patiently that we never felt uncomfortable. There were small touches too: today I ordered water, and the waiter brought jasmine tea and served it with extra sweets.

The prices start with dim sum, which is considerably expensive, and set menus start at 4,980 TWD per person and go up to 30,880 TWD per person. Today I ordered the cheapest course because I was travelling alone and wanted to try a variety of dishes. Every dish was exquisitely and delicately prepared, whether in temperature or in the doneness of the various meats. The dishes that impressed me most were the abalone soup with morel mushrooms, and the steamed lobster with soy sauce and wood ear mushrooms, which were delicious, and the restaurant clearly steams with soy sauce very well. Much as I love steaming with soy sauce, the menu today was unusual in having three steamed-with-soy dishes, the grouper and the vegetables as well, which may have made the meal a little less fun. Even so, the Chinese food at this top-tier dining room is excellent and well worth coming to try.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Welcome Drink: Hibiscus Vinegar
The welcome drink was a hibiscus vinegar. It was not as sour or pungent as you might expect; to my mind it was like a concentrated roselle drink.

### Tomato in Rose Water
image: ./images/le-palais/01.jpg | Peeled tomato with clear pink jelly in a tall white porcelain bowl, Le Palais, Taipei
While we waited for the food, the restaurant served a little snack. Ordinary nuts would have been boring, so this dish was a peeled tomato soaked in rose water, served with jelly. The sweet scent of rose fills the whole mouth, and the sweet-and-sour tomato flesh whets the appetite nicely.

### Le Palais Assorted Appetizers (迎賓四小碟)
image: ./images/le-palais/02.jpg | Four appetizer bites on a long white plate: jellyfish in a clear glass, chicken, century egg on cucumber, and a fried dumpling, Le Palais, Taipei
This assorted appetizer plate came with the waiter's advice to start from the left.

The first bite was a fried Taiwanese fish dumpling served with daikon. The filling has an aroma like karasumi, with a seafood scent and a gentle creaminess, softly salty and lightly seasoned with pepper and garlic. The wrapper is not hard, and the daikon then cuts the richness.

The quail egg, a century egg: there is a Chinese-French restaurant in Thailand that does something close to this. The egg has a jelly-like texture and no odour at all, and the yolk is slightly creamy, never crumbly or dry. Compared with the Chinese restaurants in Thailand that I know, Le Palais's version has more intensity but is less clean. There is a mild chilli sauce, cut by cool, crisp cucumber. Interesting.

The Hainanese-style chicken with spring onion, ginger and sesame oil. This was my least favourite of all the bites. The chicken breast served was rather dry and bland, like poached chicken in clean eating, and the sauce was very thin. I did not much like this bite.

The jellyfish salad with vinegar. When the waiter presented it, this was the dish I expected least from, but after tasting it, it is probably one of the most delicious jellyfish dishes I have ever eaten. The chef chose to serve only the tentacles, lightly soaked in brine. It has a distinctive crunch that I had never encountered, yet the flesh inside is very soft. Superb. The black vinegar used to season it has a complex flavour, with a gentle aroma and a little saltiness, and a unique sweet-and-sour taste that is outstanding. Heavy in flavour, but it does not weigh on the mouth.

### Abalone and Conpoy Soup with Morels (羊肚菌鮮鮑燉瑤柱脯)
image: ./images/le-palais/03.jpg | Clear amber soup with sliced abalone and a stuffed daikon, in a white porcelain bowl with a floral pattern, Le Palais, Taipei
A dried scallop soup with morels, on a chicken base, with thinly sliced abalone and daikon stuffed with dried scallop.

A mouth-watering aroma rose the instant the lid came off the soup. What is this fragrance? It is wonderful, appetising, with a slight creaminess from the chicken that blends perfectly with the alluring scent of morel mushrooms and the gentle seafood note of the dried scallop.

The soup has a rich umami with layers of complexity from the dried scallop and the chicken, with the crunch and sweet, chewy flesh of the abalone. The dried-scallop-stuffed daikon is delicious, juicy in the mouth, the sweetness of the chewy daikon meeting the dried scallop. Wonderful, wonderful, wonderful! A truly superb soup.

### Steamed Lobster with Wood Ear Mushrooms (金銀雲耳蒸龍蝦) 🕶️
image: ./images/le-palais/04.jpg | Half a lobster steamed with soy sauce, topped with wood ear mushrooms and shredded ginger, on a grey ceramic plate, Le Palais, Taipei
The lobster steamed with soy sauce. The first thing to admire is the soy sauce, which is wonderfully fragrant. The moment it was brought to the table, the aroma hit the nose, with a deep, complex, appetising fragrance. The saltiness is soft and layered, with a touch of gentle sweetness, and not overly salty. The mellow umami lifts the sweetness of the lobster well. The lobster flesh is crisp and sweet, done okay, contrasting with the crunch of the wood ear mushrooms, which makes the texture in the mouth more fun.

### Stir-Fried Pork Ribs with Vinegar Sauce (陳醋五花骨)
image: ./images/le-palais/05.jpg | Two pieces of fried pork in dark vinegar sauce, garnished with a cut-out green leaf, on a black plate, Le Palais, Taipei
A hit dish of the restaurant according to the waiter: sweet and sour pork in a vinegar sauce. The chef chose pork ribs, nicely cut into two bite-sized pieces. It was fried well, with a slightly thick coating that is still not hard or crisp all over, and the bouncy, chewy pork contrasts perfectly with the crispness of the coating. The sauce is complex: in the mouth you first get the wok aroma, then a gentle saltiness, followed by the sweet and sour of the black vinegar, and finally a touch of sweetness from sugar that lingers as the aftertaste. Well done, but I felt there was still a slight pork smell, and it would have been better if the meat were more tender. Some pieces took quite a while to chew.

### Steamed Marble Goby (清蒸筍殼魚)
image: ./images/le-palais/06.jpg | Steamed marble goby with soy sauce, topped with chopped spring onion and chilli, on a wide-rimmed white porcelain plate, Le Palais, Taipei
The next dish was steamed marble goby with soy sauce. The flesh is very soft, juicy and slightly rich. The soy flavour seemed a little stronger than the previous dish, perhaps because of the spring onion and chilli served with it. What was done well is the fish skin, which has a wonderful texture, slippery, soft and slightly chewy, with a full layer of fat that adds to the deliciousness of the dish. A small detail I want to praise is that a plate is provided for discarding bones.

### Poached Seasonal Vegetables with Soy Sauce (白灼時蔬)
image: ./images/le-palais/07.jpg | Seasonal vegetables steamed with soy sauce, a cabbage parcel and shiitake, on a wide-rimmed white porcelain plate, Le Palais, Taipei
The next dish was steamed with soy sauce yet again: seasonal vegetables such as lily buds, shiitake, bell pepper, asparagus, and a hand-wrapped cabbage parcel, steamed. This dish is mild and vegetable-focused, so compared with the other dishes it may feel a little ordinary.

### Glutinous Rice Balls in Fermented Rice Soup
image: ./images/le-palais/08.jpg | Two white porcelain bowls, one with fermented rice soup and one with tiny glutinous rice dumplings, beside a Chinese teacup, Le Palais, Taipei
Sweet glutinous rice balls in fermented rice soup. The tiny dumplings, like bubble tea pearls, have a black sesame filling and are quite delicious this way, and easier to eat. The soup was served light, with a gentle fragrance of fermented rice and a slight sweetness. Since I was drinking wine and sparkling water, the waiter offered jasmine tea to have with the dessert.

### Almond Milk (頤宮杏仁茶)
image: ./images/le-palais/09.jpg | Hot white almond milk in a porcelain cup on a saucer, Le Palais, Taipei
An almond milk that is extremely rich.

### Deep-Fried Cheese Pastry (炸豆腐奶)
image: ./images/le-palais/10.jpg | Fried cheese puff pastry dusted with icing sugar in a paper cone, beside a glass of sparkling wine, Le Palais, Taipei
We closed with another of the restaurant's signatures, the fried cheese: a large piece of puff pastry stuffed with stretchy mozzarella, dusted with icing sugar.

It goes well with the very rich almond milk, though some may find it too heavy. It is curious; I never expected to meet a fried cheese like this, least of all in a Chinese restaurant.
