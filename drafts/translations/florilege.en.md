This is an innovative restaurant in Aoyama, a chic district like that of the restaurant I visited last night, Miyasaka. It is on the B1 floor, near Seizan, another two-star, Tabelog Gold restaurant. The weather today was lovely, so I stopped for a latte before walking over.

Florilège is a restaurant whose prices are so good they are almost annoying. Not to mention that it is in Aoyama; such prices are hard to find anywhere in Tokyo. In this age of inflation it is even better value: lunch is just 7,500 yen. Considering that it holds two Michelin stars, plus a Green Star, and is on Asia's 50 Best and Tabelog Silver, Florilège is a good choice for anyone who wants a fine dining experience in Tokyo.

As for the food of Hiroyasu Kawate, it is done well, with no flaws, but not at a standard that makes you go wow. Personally, I think the gimmicks and the innovation may look a little dated nowadays. I believe everyone can enjoy Florilège's food without difficulty. There are slightly offbeat dishes such as the Wagyu mother-cow carpaccio, and fairly safe ones such as the pork or the dessert crêpe. There are quite a few interesting ingredients, such as aged Wagyu mother cow and pork raised on cashew nuts. The time between dishes seemed a little long, perhaps because guests didn't arrive together. The flavours are French, fairly punchy but easy to understand. Personally, I think the course here is a bit too heavy: eating very intense dishes such as the cheese egg custard and the Wagyu with consommé back to back seems rather cruel. A dish to act as a brake would have balanced it better.

As for service, all the waiters and chefs are attentive, quietly observing all the time, to the point that I felt stressed about whether the guest is lacking anything. They ask for comments on every dish, some two or three times, so that I sometimes felt rather scrutinised. I could also sometimes notice the order of service being jumped to serve me first. Let me say here that I am not a World's 50 Best voter or a Michelin inspector; I am just a person with money who likes to eat.

Let's see what the food was from the photos.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Edamame churros
image: ./images/florilege/01.jpg | A golden churro-shaped pastry on a grey stone, topped with white cream and green apple, Florilège, Tokyo
Edamame, churros, yoghurt espuma: light and sour, not creamy, with the sweetness of the beans. The dough is fragrant, slightly hard and crisp at the surface, warm and fluffy inside. A lightly flavoured dish that opens the senses well.

### Prawn, frozen cauliflower cream
This is my favourite dish today. Prawn, frozen cauliflower cream and yuzu jelly, served with a shallot and quinoa chutney and wasabi oil. What is cool about this dish is the daring to serve it ice cold, because the cauliflower is ice flakes, which contrast interestingly with the springy texture of the chopped prawn, the crumble at the base, and the jelly. The flavour is intense, with the sea taste of bisque, the creaminess of the cauliflower, and a small citrus sweetness from the yuzu, before finishing with the shallot and quinoa, which brake the intensity with bitterness and the freshness of vegetables; or, if that is not enough, the wasabi oil does this job very well. A complex dish, very well resolved. Superb.

### Steamed bread
image: ./images/florilege/03.jpg | A round cream-coloured steamed bun in a wooden bowl, Florilège, Tokyo
The bread here is soft and fluffy, like bread mixed with mantou, slightly sweet.

### Sustainability Beef
image: ./images/florilege/04.jpg | Thinly sliced carpaccio-style beef on a white cream sauce, on a white plate, Florilège, Tokyo
Sustainability beef is called sustainable because the chef chose to use aged Wagyu mother cow instead. It is served as a carpaccio, with smoked potato purée, parsley oil and beef consommé.

Although it is a good idea, I find the dish not very balanced in flavour. The beef is lightly cured, similar to prosciutto, which makes the already aged beef (to say "old" even softly hurts) saltier and tougher. Eaten with the strongly flavoured consommé, it becomes saltier still. There is an attempt to use the parsley oil and the sweetness of the rather runny potato purée to match the consommé and to act as a brake, but personally I find it too intense.

### Shiitake and cheese
image: ./images/florilege/05.jpg | Creamy shiitake soup topped with thinly sliced mushrooms and truffle, in a black wooden bowl, Florilège, Tokyo
Shiitake and cheese is served warm. At the base is a mushroom egg custard, eaten with mushroom soup and cheese, fresh cheese, shiitake and truffle, with all the ingredients except the truffle coming from Japan. It is a dish with a smooth, creamy flavour from the egg and cheese and an intense flavour from the cheese. It tastes rounded, yet separates into many dimensions. The shiitake has a crisp flesh with an earthy aroma, but I find the cheese soup too intense, masking the mushroom aroma too much, and especially when eaten after a very heavy dish, the course seems to lack balance.

### Grilled Kurobuta pork
image: ./images/florilege/06.jpg | Sliced grilled pork with red wine sauce and small bites on a white plate, Florilège, Tokyo
Today's pork is Kurobuta, raised on cashew nuts.

Our main course is grilled pork served with a red wine and onion sauce, eaten with a meatball of pig's head meat stuffed into a dough mixed with parsley, and a wrap of kombu and yoghurt with ham.

I like this dish very much. It is rare to find a restaurant that does pork so impressively. The pork here may be one of the best I have ever eaten. The flesh is tender and sweet with no off-smell at all, very good. The sauce is also done very well; though it is called an onion sauce, there is no onion smell at all, only a rich, sweet umami and a light taste of red wine. I guess it is because they used shin tamanegi, new-season onion that is just coming into season, to make the sauce (luxurious). One small gripe is the pork skin, which I find a bit tough; if it had crisp skin, this would be even more delicious.

The meatball is done very well. The dough is soft and not heavy, the pork is crunchy, with sweetness from onion and lightly pickled mustard seeds, probably pickled in the Dijon style, helping to cut the flavour.

### Passionfruit
image: ./images/florilege/07.jpg | A round purple dessert sprinkled with white ice, on a cloth and a wooden tray, Florilège, Tokyo
Our first dessert is Passionfruit. The chef serves the passion fruit as a jelly and an ice cream, with extra texture added by including its seeds, before topping it with a foam of mint and lemongrass. I like this dish: the light sweet-and-sour of the passion fruit and the varied textures. The foam gives a cool fragrance that goes superbly with the passion fruit. Delicious.

### Omelette Chocolate
image: ./images/florilege/08.jpg | A chocolate omelette on a grey-green plate with cream and espresso sauce, Florilège, Tokyo
The last dish is a chocolate omelette drizzled with espresso sauce, served with cream and cocoa beans.

The chocolate the restaurant uses is from Peru. I think it is a very gentle dish. The omelette is filled with chocolate cream, which matches the bitterness of the coffee well. The Peruvian chocolate has a fragrance that is neither very fruity-sour nor very bitter, mild in flavour but very fragrant. A plain dessert, put together in good balance, and delicious.
