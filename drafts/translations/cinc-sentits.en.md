Cinc Sentits is the restaurant of Chef Jordi Artal, on Carrer d'Entença on the western side of Barcelona's Eixample district. It currently holds two Michelin stars in the Michelin Guide España 2026. The restaurant won its first star in 2008 and its second in the Michelin Guide 2021.

As for the name, Cinc Sentits means "five senses" in Catalan, and the restaurant aims for the whole meal to engage all five: sight, hearing, touch, smell and taste. It first opened in 2004, run by Chef Jordi and his sister Amèlia Artal, before moving to its current site in 2018. The space is divided into several areas. At the front is a lounge with the mood of a tapas bar, where we enjoy the house-made vermouth with snacks, before walking on to the dining room, decorated in white alternating with black, with a view of the kitchen.

Chef Jordi Artal is a self-taught chef, born in Canada to a Catalan family and raised between Canada and California, who worked in technology in Silicon Valley for about ten years before turning his back on the office and returning to open a restaurant in Barcelona. His family has had its roots in the same village for centuries, La Torre de l'Espanyol in the province of Tarragona, so memories from both Catalonia and Canada appear in many of the dishes.

The food here is Modern Catalan in the chef's own style, using produce from small producers with a clearly Catalan identity. The restaurant's menu book even introduces each producer, from a fourth-generation fisherman who sails along the Catalan coast and organic farmers on the Llobregat delta to the Gramona family, who make sparkling wine from the native Xarel·lo grape. There are two menus to choose from, Light and Tasting, with wine pairing and a non-alcoholic pairing.

Another charm of the place is the menu card that comes with every dish, each telling the origin of the ingredients and the story of the chef's family: the oxtail stew from a dish his mother made in winter, the brioche from his sister's recipe, and the Cinc Sentits Shot that reflects the chef's childhood in Canada. The vegetables in the course come from three organic farms in Catalonia, and the sea bass was chosen from the cold Atlantic coast, so as you eat you read, and come to understand the chef himself.

Let's see what was on the menu.

🍽️ The art of taste in this meal:

VERMOUTH & SNACKS

## The art of taste in this meal

### Our Faifó Olive
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/01.jpg | Tray of snacks and vermouth at Cinc Sentits: an olive on an olive branch, and a tart on a stone
A warm olive made into a sphere, the technique of turning a liquid into a ball with a thin membrane, with olive juice inside, coated in cold white chocolate.

### Red Prawn, Sea Urchin, Tangerine Dust and Vinegar
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/02.jpg | Red prawn tart with uni foam, and the vermouth snack set on a wooden tray, Cinc Sentits
A red prawn tart with uni foam, tangerine powder and vinegar. The uni aroma is sweet, and the prawn is sweet with the aroma of its own fat.

### Roasted Chicken, Chicken Liver and Plum
A tart of the chef's mother's chicken liver recipe, made with Pyrenean wine, with roast chicken and plum, topped with candy. The tart shell is hard, the liver not at all gamey, its aroma not strong, with smoke leading. The candy on top adds texture, like fried chicken skin, and it is not very sweet.

### Vermut Nostre
The set of snacks closes with the restaurant's own vermouth from Pla de Bages, a wine aromatised with herbs. Light.

DINNER

### Langoustine
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/05.jpg | Langoustine tart, foie gras, and wagyu tartare topped with caviar, on a wooden vessel
A tart of langoustine, the small lobster, with mustard seeds and capers. The langoustine is sweet. The flavour is close to the previous bite, but personally I liked this one more. This dish was paired with Gramona Celler Batlle 2015 Corpinnat, a sparkling wine from the Corpinnat group in Penedès, made from hand-picked organic grapes and aged for at least 18 months.

### Foie Gras
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/06.jpg | Cream of foie gras with green apple and blackberries in a wooden cup, beside the langoustine tart
A light-textured cream of foie gras with smoked sardine, green apple, blackberries and carquinyoli, the hard-baked Catalan almond biscuit. Light, with a faint aroma of sardine but not too sour, rounded and harmonious. A good and very interesting foie gras dish.

### Truffled Wagyu
Wagyu tartare on grilled brioche, with oyster foam, black truffle and caviar. The caviar flavour is pronounced and there is little aroma of beef. Personally, I did not much like that the flavour of the bread led a little.

### Teardrop Peas 🕶️
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/08.jpg | Teardrop Peas: small peas in a pea sauce with grilled baby sea cucumbers in a white bowl
Small peas, steamed and then simmered in a pea sauce, served with baby sea cucumbers grilled until crisp, confit spring garlic, picada, the Catalan paste of pounded parsley and garlic used to add flavour and body to dishes, and pea essence. This pea has a short season, and the restaurant chooses to use it when the peas are at their peak, so they are very sweet. Eating fresh green peas was once a luxury at the court of Louis XIV in the 17th century. In this bite garlic and parsley lead, yet the sweetness is intense. The tiny sea cucumbers are crunchy and very good, contrasting with the texture of the peas, and the picada cuts the sweetness with its natural green freshness.

### Sourdough and Brioche
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/09.jpg | Sourdough and brioche on the table, Cinc Sentits, Barcelona
Sourdough made with three flours from Catalan grains, and the brioche from the chef's sister's recipe, served with Arbequina olive oil from Castillo de Canena and Picual from the Alsina family's grove at Castelló de Farfanya.

### The Garden
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/10.jpg | The Garden: colourful Catalan organic vegetables, raw, pickled and grilled, with flowers
A dish celebrating the peak of the Catalan vegetable season, using organic vegetables from three farms: Hort de Tamuntana, eBiofarm and Parc Agrari. Each is prepared separately, raw, pickled and grilled, and served with a herb yoghurt sauce and nitrogen-frozen tomato. Most of the vegetables are crisp, with varied sweetness, some sour, some pungent and some green. The tomato adds umami.

### Oxtail
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/11.jpg | Braised oxtail covered with a black truffle sheet stamped with a bull's head, on parsnip foam
Oxtail marinated in wine for 12 hours, braised in mushroom stock and then sautéed with dried herbs, served with baby potatoes, herb butter, parsnip foam, crisp mushrooms and sliced black truffle. This dish is a refined version of fricandó, the Catalan stew of beef and mushrooms dating back to the Middle Ages, and a dish the chef's mother made in the middle of winter. Light, not strong, with sweetness leading. The baby potatoes are very tender, with the aroma of mushrooms and truffle, and the parsnip foam gives the palate a break.

### Sea Bass
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/12.jpg | Sous-vide sea bass rolled in black skin, crisp black fishbone, white wine sauce and piquillo sauce
Sea bass from the cold Atlantic coast cooked sous vide, in a vacuum bag at a constant temperature, on a squid sofrito, with a mosaic of squid, piquillo pepper and white wine sauce. The piquillo is a sweet red pepper from Navarre. The fish has a very good texture, tender, rich and melting, not fishy, with a slight springiness. Its softness contrasts with the springy chew of the finely diced squid beneath. The creamy sauce of wine and butter is mildly sour, while the pepper sauce is sweet and pungent but not spicy.

### Venison
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/13.jpg | Grilled venison with carrot, a cylinder of roast potato and a green sauce
Grilled venison, served with almond miso and cauliflower that the restaurant ferments itself, elderberries pickled in raspberry vinegar, trinxat, the Catalan mash of vegetables, made here with spinach, garlic and pork belly, and a cylinder of roast potato. It is served with a croquette made from the rest of the venison, and a purée of beans with truffle.

The venison was a little dry for me, perhaps from a leaner cut. The almond-scented miso that goes with the cauliflower is creamy and light, the elderberries sour and the trinxat sweet. I was quite confused; personally I think this dish lacks a little saltiness.

### Cinc Sentits Shot
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/14.jpg | Cinc Sentits Shot: maple syrup, cream and cava sabayon in a shot glass on a wooden stand
Warm maple syrup, cold cream, a sabayon of egg yolk made with cava, and sea salt. This shot was inspired by the chef's background as a Catalan born and raised in Canada, and it is the only dish served completely unchanged since the restaurant opened 21 years ago. You get the maple flavour, and the sabayon is light because it is made with cava, the Spanish sparkling wine.

### Wood Sorrel
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/15.jpg | Wood sorrel and white chocolate mousse with a green gel and wood sorrel leaves
A mousse of wood sorrel and white chocolate, a crisp sorrel sheet, green apple juice, fennel, wood sorrel, dill oil and freeze-dried apple powder. Wood sorrel, or oxalis, is a wild herb with a natural sourness. This bite is cold, with the aroma of green apple and oxalis.

### Nougat
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/16.jpg | Nougat with Catalan whisky ice cream, topped with a lattice chocolate sheet
Nougat, the chewy sweet made from sugar or honey with nuts, served with Catalan whisky ice cream, cocoa and dried mushrooms. The whisky aroma overwhelms everything.

### Últimas Tentaciones
image: ./images/cinc-sentits/17.jpg | Small closing sweets: chocolate, cream puff and bonbon on a white hexagonal plate
We finish with small sweet bites: chocolate with olive oil, bread and salt; a cream puff filled with salted caramel ganache; a baked hazelnut cookie; and a coffee liqueur bonbon.
