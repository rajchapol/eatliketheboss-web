T+T stands for "Tapas Tasting". It is the contemporary Asian restaurant of Chef Johnny Tsai (蔡元善), in a lane off Dunhua North Road in Songshan (松山), near the Taipei Arena. The concept is a tasting menu served in tapas-sized bites in a relaxed, bistro-style setting. The restaurant has held one MICHELIN star since 2021, every year through 2026, and made Tatler's Best 20 in Taiwan for 2026.

Chef Johnny graduated from Kai Ping Culinary School (開平餐飲學校) and worked with Chef Justin Quek and at Restaurant André in Singapore before opening his own restaurant, and he received the MICHELIN Young Chef award in 2022. During his time in Singapore he saw how fine dining there makes use of Southeast Asian flavours, and this blending of many food cultures really is the signature of the Lion City's food scene. Back home, he wanted to blend Taiwanese flavours with the cooking of other countries too.

Every year the chef still travels to Malaysia and Korea to eat his way through street food and walk the markets. So today the T+T menu takes in flavours from Taiwan, Malaysia, Singapore, Korea and even Thailand, built on French technique, and it changes every season. For me, the strength of this place is how many cuisines the food draws on, and yet the menu never feels confused in the slightest.

The food here is full of complexity and balance. I think the chef has a remarkable memory for flavour profiles: the flavours in each dish come from memories of his travels. Good food, not expensive, and fun. Personally I found the desserts a little simple compared with the savoury dishes. The service is fairly laid-back, not as polished as at a typical starred restaurant, but relaxed and friendly. The wine pairing is textbook, on the classic side, and generous. All in all, another restaurant with good value and fun food.

Let's see what was on the menu.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Quail Egg / Sesame Oil / Seaweed (鵪鶉蛋 麻油 海苔)
image: ./images/t-plus-t/01.jpg | A brown quail egg on fried seaweed and salad leaves, in a white ceramic bowl lined with cream-coloured beads, T+T, Taipei
This dish brings together Korean and Taiwanese flavours, and it is intriguing. Crisp, salty fried seaweed, a gentle bitterness from perilla leaf, saltiness from an egg-yolk sauce, finished with young spring onion shoots and a quail egg braised in soy the Chinese way, with the aroma of sesame oil. It was paired with a sparkling sake from Gifu with a pear aroma, whose well-judged sweetness rounds this bite off beautifully.

### Anise Hyssop / Glass Noodle / Chili (茴藿香 冬粉 辣椒)
image: ./images/t-plus-t/02.jpg | Two bites on a white plate: chicken with charred skin on red sauce, and a crisp shell with filling topped with green shoots, T+T, Taipei
A fun dish, built mainly around chicken and served as two bites. The first is a sauce of star anise and coriander leaf in an Indian pani puri shell, with a hint of lemongrass and a Chinese-leaning minty note from anise hyssop, a herb of the mint family that smells rather like star anise. It tastes green and refreshing, not spicy. The other bite is Thai-style stuffed chicken with glass noodles, using the thigh, the meat nicely bouncy, grilled like Thai gai yang. The chilli sauce comes as a jelly, sweet and sour, like Thai sweet chilli dipping sauce mixed with a yum dressing.

### Oyster
image: ./images/t-plus-t/03.jpg | An oyster wrapped in fried glass noodles in its shell on crushed ice, in a round wooden vessel, T+T, Taipei
Inspired by Taiwan. The oyster is marinated in yeast and sake, then wrapped in glass noodles and fried, and served in its shell on crushed ice, with an ajillo-style garlic sauce and katsuobushi. It looks odd but somehow it works: the smokiness of the dried fish and the garlic go well together. The sake marinade gives the oyster's sweetness more depth, and the coating is not thin but crisp, without masking the oyster's soft texture. The sauce has a gentle chilli heat.

### Abalone / Mustard Greens / Shaoxing (鮑魚 酸菜 紹興) 🕶️
image: ./images/t-plus-t/04.jpg | Abalone and tofu skin in a creamy pale soup topped with fine strands of herbs, in a white porcelain bowl, T+T, Taipei
Inspired by Malaysian fish-head soup. The base is a fish-bone soup, yet not fishy at all: creamy, with a light aroma of Shaoxing wine. It comes with abalone that is tender with a slight crunch, never chewy, and tofu skin stuffed with takana (Japanese pickled mustard greens), which goes beautifully with a soup that is a little like tonkotsu. I think the chef combines Asian flavours with real intelligence.

### Duck / Truffle / Wheel Pie (玫瑰鴨 松露 車輪餅)
image: ./images/t-plus-t/05.jpg | A golden-brown wheel cake stamped with T+T on patterned paper, on a black plate, T+T, Taipei
A signature served every season. It is modelled on the wheel cake (車輪餅), a Taiwanese street snack influenced by Japan's imagawayaki, shaped rather like a dorayaki, with a pancake-like batter and slightly crisp edges. The filling is bouncy diced duck with a light truffle aroma, black pepper and a cream sauce. Gentle flavours that suit all ages.

### King Crab / Singaporean Chili Sauce
image: ./images/t-plus-t/06.jpg | Two golden breadcrumbed fried king crab legs on dark red chilli sauce, topped with shoots, in a scalloped black dish, T+T, Taipei
An add-on served after the duck. King crab legs are coated in breadcrumbs and fried, with a Singaporean chilli sauce in the style of chilli crab. The sauce is not too sweet and only gently spicy, never so hot that it masks the crab. The crab meat is bouncy and juicy, a good contrast with the crisp coating around it.

### Lamb Saddle / Black Garlic / Jungle Garlic (羊鞍 黑蒜 居林果)
image: ./images/t-plus-t/07.jpg | Two pieces of pink lamb with charred skin, a black sauce and a spring roll topped with herbs, on a white plate, T+T, Taipei
This dish comes with a lamb spring roll, lightly herbal, the meat bouncy and free of any gamey smell. The main piece is charred grilled lamb from New Zealand, with a jus made with goji berries and black garlic, eaten with a mountain vegetable called chuanqi, whose distinctive green, herbal aroma neatly cuts through the lamb's gaminess. The lamb is very tender, probably slow-cooked before grilling. The skin was a touch too hard, but the meat was very tender. An interesting lamb dish.

### Spanish Mackerel / Yellow Curry / Koshihikari Rice (馬鮫魚 黃咖哩 越光米)
image: ./images/t-plus-t/08.jpg | Breadcrumbed fried Spanish mackerel with white flesh and a prawn in yellow curry, in a gold bowl, T+T, Taipei
Spanish mackerel with curry and rice. The rice is lightly seasoned, mixed with cashew nuts and seasoned tamarind juice, which reminded me of Thai pineapple fried rice. The curry tastes like a Chinese-style curry but thicker; the chef said it is Malaysian style. The fish is very firm, and two prawns came alongside, but their texture was poor, tough from overcooking. Personally I preferred the fish.

### Malt / Osmanthus / Sticky Rice (麥芽 桂花 糯米)
image: ./images/t-plus-t/09.jpg | Light brown ice cream in white foam, in a fluted ceramic bowl, T+T, Taipei
Unusual. Sticky rice flavoured with osmanthus, and malt ice cream in a sake kasu (sake lees) foam. The rice aroma stands out and the sweetness is light. The osmanthus barely came through, perhaps because the sake lees overwhelmed it for me.

### Honey / Ginger / Sesame (蜂蜜 老薑 芝麻)
image: ./images/t-plus-t/10.jpg | A golden-brown sesame-coated ball beside white ice flakes, on a white plate, T+T, Taipei
A sesame-coated ball with a sponge-cake centre, surrounded by honey ice cream, with ginger shaved ice on the side.
