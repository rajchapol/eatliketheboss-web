Oryouri Fujii (御料理ふじ居) is the Japanese restaurant of Chef Hironori Fujii (藤井寛徳), set in Higashi-Iwase, a quiet old quarter on the harbour of Toyama. It holds two stars from the MICHELIN Guide Hokuriku 2021 Special Edition, has won the Tabelog Award Silver every year from 2024 to 2026, and is also on The Japan Times' Destination Restaurants 2023 list.

As for the setting, Iwase was once a prosperous port town thanks to the Kitamaebune trade, the fleet of merchant ships that sailed the Sea of Japan between Osaka and Hokkaido in the Edo period. The restaurant occupies a renovated house that belonged to a shipping merchant of that era, with its Japanese garden preserved, an eight-seat counter and a private tatami room. Personally, I think it is a very beautiful space.

Chef Fujii was born in Toyama, into a family that ran a fish shop. After graduating from the Tsuji Culinary Institute in Japanese cuisine, he trained for about five years at Zeniya in Kanazawa, then for about six more at Mashita in Kyoto's Gion district, before returning to open his own restaurant in Toyama in 2011. In August 2019 he moved to Iwase at the invitation of the owner of the Masuizumi sake brewery, a key figure in the revival of the neighbourhood.

The cooking here is built on dashi, and relies mainly on Toyama's seasonal produce, from both Toyama Bay and the region's rivers. The chef himself describes his aim as food that is simple and direct. The drinks are tied to the neighbourhood too: Masuizumi, from the Masuda Shuzo brewery founded in 1893, is made right here in Iwase. The restaurant has its own exclusive sake and its own exclusive wine, and the drinking water is mineral water from the same mountain range used to brew Masuizumi sake.

Personally, I find a Kyoto kaiseki flavour in the food here, which fits with the chef's six years at Mashita in Kyoto. To me the cooking is gentle in the Kyoto way, yet deep. As for service, the staff are lovely and full of energy, and all of them did their best to communicate with us and introduce the food throughout.

Let's see what was on the menu.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Yamaimo Cake with Murasaki Uni
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/01.jpg | Fried yamaimo cake topped with murasaki uni, in a dashi-shoyu sauce, in a blue-and-white porcelain bowl
The chef grinds yamaimo, the Japanese mountain yam, such as nagaimo, and fries it into a cake, topped with murasaki uni and finished with a dashi-shoyu sauce, served beneath a large leaf. It is gently salty, with a texture like turnip cake (radish cake) but softer and less springy, mildly sweet with no root-vegetable smell, and it suits the pronounced sweetness of the uni.

### Shiro Ebi Soup
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/02.jpg | Clear dashi soup in a lidded bowl with winter melon, shiro ebi and junsai, topped with sliced sudachi
A dashi soup with winter melon, shiro ebi and junsai. Shiro ebi are tiny white shrimp nicknamed the "jewels of Toyama Bay", while junsai is a young aquatic plant shoot wrapped in clear, slippery mucilage. The dashi is delicate, but the winter melon added to it holds its flavour in full. Umami leads, followed by a fresh, green taste that plays against the very pronounced sweetness of the shiro ebi. The floating junsai is excellent. Delicious.

### Hata Sashimi
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/03.jpg | Hata sashimi on a glass plate with shiso leaf, wasabi, and a dish of salt with sudachi
Sashimi of hata, the Japanese grouper, served with salt, sudachi, red shiso leaf, wasabi and shoyu. The flesh is springy and crisp. Eat it alternating with the salt and sudachi, or accompany it with myoga; none of it would be wrong.

### Kuro Awabi
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/04.jpg | Chilled black abalone from Toyama on shredded yellow butternut squash in a glass bowl
Black abalone from Toyama, served cold, perhaps because of the hot weather, with yellow strands of finely shredded butternut squash. The strands are crisp, while the abalone is springy yet tender, not tough. There is almost no sauce; the deliciousness comes from the abalone itself, with umami and a faint aroma of sake. The chef seasons the abalone lightly with salt.

### Chilled Vermicelli with Grated Yamaimo
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/05.jpg | Pale green chilled vermicelli topped with white grated yamaimo in a black bowl
Pale green chilled vermicelli topped with grated yamaimo. The noodles are made by grinding mountain vegetables and mixing them with tuber starch, chewy and soft like Chinese egg noodles. Tasty.

### Jinzu River Ayu 🕶️
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/06.jpg | Two charcoal-grilled ayu on a wave-patterned ceramic plate with a green dish of tadezu
"This dish was so delicious that the Boss had to take his glasses off to taste it." Ayu from the Jinzu River, which flows through Toyama into the bay, grilled over charcoal with two chefs on the job, standing and watching the charcoal, the fish and the fire with full concentration, then smoked with tea leaves before serving. It is eaten with tadezu, a vinegar sauce blended with tade leaves, the Japanese water pepper that is the traditional partner of grilled ayu. The tadezu is bitter, sour and slightly sweet. The fish is fluffy, the skin crisp, lightly salted, and it goes well with the sauce. I think the focused control of the fire made a great difference to this dish. It is one of the best ayu I have ever eaten. Excellent.

### Hassun
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/07.jpg | A long hassun tray lined with maple leaves, holding sweet egg, miso-grilled aubergine, monaka and several other seasonal items
A plate of seasonal bites within the kaiseki course. This time it consisted of the following.

**Summer Maguro Avocado** Summer maguro with avocado and seaweed. The flavour was fine, but personally I thought the seaweed turned it a little dark, so it looked less appetising.

**Unagi Kabayaki Cucumber** Grilled eel brushed with a sweet sauce, served with myoga, or Japanese ginger flower, a vinegar-based sauce, and thin salted pickled cucumber, scored with the knife in a lovely pattern. Sweet and sour, the eel well grilled, matching the pickled cucumber nicely. The scoring of the cucumber also improves its texture, so it does not stand apart from the fluffy, tender flesh of the eel. Delicious. With this dish I paired IWA 5, a special label made for the restaurant, and they went together extremely well.

**Miso-Grilled Aubergine** Aubergine grilled with miso. Light, with a clear miso aroma and sweetness leading. I think it was probably a local miso.

**Beetroot Monaka** A crisp rice-flour wafer sandwiching a filling, or monaka, filled here with beetroot. Earthy and slightly sweet. Personally, I am still puzzled as to why this dish was made.

**Shirobai Gai** Shirobai gai, the white whelk, with miso and green vegetables. This one is clearly salty, the whelk crunchy and not very tough, with the green scent of the vegetables slipping in from time to time. Personally, I am still puzzled by this one too.

**Manganji** Manganji sweet peppers, a local pepper of Kyoto, with whitebait and sweet egg. The sweet egg has a light, pudding-like texture and a neutral sweetness. Good.

### Tako
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/08.jpg | Octopus in a lidded glass bowl with clear jelly and tiny purple hanahojiso flowers
Octopus steamed for two hours and then simmered, served with jelly and hanahojiso. The octopus is sweet and tender, not tough at all. The sweetness is cut by the sour-sweet jelly and the hanahojiso. Good.

### Yoshikawa Nasu
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/09.jpg | A whole peeled Yoshikawa aubergine in dashi broth, sprinkled with sudachi zest
Yoshikawa aubergine from Fukui, peeled whole, fried and then simmered in dashi, sprinkled with sudachi zest. This round aubergine is a native variety of Sabae city that is registered as a Geographical Indication. It is naturally sweet, plain yet profound. Well fried, it does not collapse, and the flesh is juicy with broth, while the broth brings out the aubergine's sweetness. Good.

### Corn Rice
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/10.jpg | A clay pot of rice topped with scorched grilled corn across the whole surface
Rice cooked with corn grilled until charred, which covers the whole top of the clay pot. The rice is soft with no hard core, and the very sweet corn contrasts with the lightly salted rice. Delicious.

### Masuizumi Nigori Sake Sorbet
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/11.jpg | White nigori sake sorbet in a celadon bowl
A sorbet made from nigori, the cloudy sake, from Masuizumi, the brewery in this very neighbourhood.

### Yokan
image: ./images/oryouri-fujii/12.jpg | Deep red yokan on a blue-and-white porcelain plate, decorated with a maple leaf
We finish with yokan, the Japanese red bean jelly. Pleasantly sweet, not firm, silky and easy to eat.
