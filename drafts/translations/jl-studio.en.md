"Some people travel to eat", as I like to say when eating with Boom. The line is surely no exaggeration for JL Studio, one of the best Singaporean restaurants in the world, yet it sits in Taiwan. Confusing, isn't it?

JL Studio is in Taichung. The easiest way there is the high-speed rail (HSR) for about an hour to Taichung Station, then a taxi for about 15 minutes. Round trip, it takes more than two and a half hours, which is to say that getting here took nearly my whole day.

The reason is simple: JL Studio is the only restaurant in the world in the Singaporean category to hold three Michelin stars. It is also on both the OAD list and Asia's 50 Best.

JL Studio is the brainchild of Chef Jimmy Lim, who not only reinterprets classic Singaporean dishes in interesting ways but also takes Malaysian and Indonesian food and recasts it with Western technique, befitting the melting pot of South East Asia.

The first thing I want to praise is the evident dedication of the service team, from before I arrived to after I left. They emailed beforehand to ask for details, most importantly our rough dining time so that we could book our train correctly. Afterwards I left a jacket behind at the restaurant; it was an old one, bought at 80% off, yet they were happy to send it back to me in Thailand. During the meal, when the air-conditioning was dripping water onto me, they adjusted it with great care. That is the true spirit of hospitality.

The staff here know wine well and offer their own opinions in an interesting way. The pairing is good value, though personally I felt many of the glasses were not quite settled. The glass that impressed me today was the Cogno Barolo Riserva, which matched the short rib rendang perfectly. I like the style of the decoration and the table setting very much. Even the water glasses are from a brand close to the restaurant's logo, and the plates, bowls and cutlery are impeccable, all from the French brand JL Coquet, which sits beautifully with the chef's colourful plating. The attention to detail is remarkable: a beef plate like this usually shows smears of grease, but here it was spotless, and I did not find a single mark.

My favourite dish had to be the signature chicken rice, served in an exciting way. It uses red grouper cooked rosé, with a springy texture and the scent of chopped ginger, and a rice sauce made from jasmine rice that goes with the green chilli, echoing the chilli dip served with chicken rice. The fish and the bamboo shoot had no off-smell at all, completely.

There were some dishes I did not understand, such as the LAKSA, and some where the cooking was not yet perfectly polished, such as the bak kut teh tart shell, which was rather [word missing in the Thai] and chewy (the waiter said it was like dipping a pa thong go into bak kut teh), or the sauce on the beef dish, whose quantity did not suit the amount of meat. But overall, JL Studio impressed me with a playful concept running through the whole meal, and it will surely impress everyone who comes to try it.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Amberjack | Star Fruit | Jackfruit (紅甘 | 楊桃 | 波羅蜜)
image: ./images/jl-studio/01.jpg | Amberjack sashimi in an orange sauce of star fruit and jackfruit, with chilli oil being dripped onto the plate, JL Studio, Taichung
The chef said this dish was inspired by sweet-and-sour fish soup. It is amberjack sashimi served with a sauce of star fruit and jackfruit, accompanied by daikon, and topped with chilli oil. The sweetness comes together harmoniously in the mouth. Tasted on its own, the sauce may be a little too sweet, but cut with thinly sliced raw daikon it goes together surprisingly well. It is a rather light opening dish, sweet with a touch of sourness and a hint of chilli, which eased the fatigue of the journey and stimulated the appetite very well.

### Penghu Kuruma Prawn | Butterhead | Sesame (澎湖明蝦 | 奶油萵苣 | 芝麻)
image: ./images/jl-studio/02.jpg | Kuruma prawn garnished with edible flowers and prawn-head tempura, beside butterhead lettuce and a green sauce, JL Studio, Taichung
This dish uses kuruma prawn from Taiwan, one of the island's treasures the chef wanted to showcase. It is stuffed with mandarin and grapefruit, served with butterhead lettuce, then dressed with a sauce of peanuts, sesame and spring onion oil. The side is prawn-head tempura.

This salad carries a hint of gado-gado, the famous Indonesian dish, but westernised into something chic. All the ingredients are of excellent quality: fresh, crisp, sweet vegetables set against prawn cooked to just between cooked and raw and lightly salted. The vegetables and prawn are delicious on their own, but the nut and sesame sauce, which has a whiff of Japanese sesame dressing and gado-gado, ties everything together with the mandarin and grapefruit, which add a sweet-sour freshness and a bitterness at the tip of the tongue in perfect measure. This dish is almost perfect. The one small thing I did not like was the oil in the deep-fried prawn heads, which sadly dulled the freshness of the dish.

### Daily Fish | Bamboo Shoot | Jasmine Rice (市場鮮魚 | 竹筍 | 茉莉香米) 🕶️
image: ./images/jl-studio/03.jpg | Red grouper topped with thinly sliced bamboo shoot, on a white jasmine rice sauce beside green chilli purée, on a white plate, JL Studio, Taichung
The most impressive dish of the day: the chef's signature, the chicken rice without the chicken.

The dish uses red grouper cooked rosé, springy in texture, with a faint scent of chopped ginger like chicken rice, and a white sauce that tastes like rice cooked in jasmine rice. Choosing jasmine rice adds the cool fragrance of jasmine rice to the sauce in a distinctive way, and it goes with the green Fushimi chilli, grown in Taiwan, served as a purée. Its taste is like the chilli sauce that comes with Singaporean chicken rice, but greener and fresher, so it matches the fish perfectly. The bamboo shoot on top is sweet and crunchy and completely free of any off-smell, which suits the fish flawlessly. It is unbelievably good, absolutely superb.

### Abalone | XingShe Mushrooms | Porcini (南非鮑魚 | 新社香菇 | 牛肝菌)
image: ./images/jl-studio/04.jpg | Tart filled with mushrooms and abalone in a dark sauce, topped with sliced mushrooms, in a brown sauce on a white plate, JL Studio, Taichung
JL Studio's take on bak kut teh is served as a tart. The filling is Taiwanese-grown mushrooms and abalone stir-fried in bak kut teh soy sauce, with green peppercorns and Sichuan pepper (huajiao) on top. The abalone has the dark, sticky soy flavour of bak kut teh, the sauce is more concentrated, and the taste stays in the same register but with a clearer aroma of garlic and the heat of green peppercorn. Although the dish looks Western, its character is unmistakably bak kut teh.

I did not like one thing: the tart shell turned chewy from absorbing the liquid from the filling, which the waiter said resembles dipping a pa thong go into bak kut teh.

With this dish the chef also serves a mushroom stock seasoned in the traditional bak kut teh way.

### Maine Lobster | Cuttlefish | Laksa Leaves (緬因龍蝦 | 透抽 | 叻沙葉)
image: ./images/jl-studio/05.jpg | Lobster and cuttlefish noodles topped with herbs, on a pale green laksa sauce in a curved white plate, JL Studio, Taichung
This is laksa, another famous dish from South East Asia. Chef Jimmy chose to serve Maine lobster with laksa sauce, cuttlefish noodles and kai-lan stems.

Every element in the dish was cooked to three-Michelin-star standard, but personally I felt the sauce and the components were too green to be called laksa wholeheartedly.

### Palate Cleanser
image: ./images/jl-studio/06.jpg | Orange dome-shaped foam topped with herb leaves and flower petals over small tomato pieces, in a wide-rimmed white bowl, JL Studio, Taichung
The palate cleanser is made from tomato and burrata cheese.

### Angus Beef Short Rib | Rendang | Achar (安格斯無骨牛小排 | 仁當 | 馬來醃菜)
image: ./images/jl-studio/07.jpg | Pink Angus short rib beside black rendang sauce and pickled vegetables, next to a bowl of nasi lemak rice, JL Studio, Taichung
For this dish the chef serves Angus short rib with rendang sauce, the Indonesian speciality, along with Singaporean pickled chillies, pickled daikon, a ginger-based chabayong sauce, and nasi lemak rice. I like this dish very much. It is intense and powerful. The sauce has a fiery heat which, with the richness of the short rib, goes together very well, so it does not feel that spicy. The beef is lightly salted, and with the pickles, which are slightly spicy and sweet, it cuts the richness nicely and brings the dish into balance across all the flavours. One small detail I liked was that the chef put rose apple blossom stamens on the rice. Besides softening the heat of the dish, it adds an interesting freshness and dimension.

### Palate Cleanser: Dragon Fruit
image: ./images/jl-studio/08.jpg | Deep pink dragon fruit sorbet with a candy sheet in a clear embossed glass bowl, JL Studio, Taichung
Today's palate cleanser was a dragon fruit sorbet, served with dragon fruit pearls and dragon fruit candy.

It tastes fully of dragon fruit, yet it is fun in its range of textures. My one gripe is that I felt the sugar today was so sticky it clung to my teeth.

### Milo | Dark Chocolate | Milk Chocolate (美祿 | 黑巧克力 | 牛奶巧克力)
image: ./images/jl-studio/09.jpg | Chocolate sphere dusted with Milo powder on a white plate, with chocolate dots around the plate, JL Studio, Taichung
I would call this dish Ovaltine, no, wrong, Milo! A hi-so version of the Milo volcano. It looks simple, but its components are superbly made. The sphere has a thin, very crisp shell, and the Milo chocolate powder sprinkled over it has the clinging, sticky quality of a Milo volcano. Inside are a thin, crisp brownie and soft ice cream that is cold and sticky, all of it tasting of Milo.

### Yaw Kun Roti Kaya (耀坤咖啡)
image: ./images/jl-studio/10.jpg | Egg-shaped dessert in a wavy ceramic basket beside a white hen-shaped dish, JL Studio, Taichung
Besides taking the Milo commission, the chef also took one from Singapore's famous kaya toast. The kaya toast has been transformed in appearance and is served in the form of a hen's egg. Inside is a pandan foam with a cool, green fragrance and the feel of egg custard, lightly sweet. Dig down and underneath is a brown kaya-flavoured ice cream, light in texture and deeply sweet. P.S. The eggshell is a hard cookie and can be eaten.

### Sweets: Bandung | Ambon (玫瑰球 | 蜂巢糕)
image: ./images/jl-studio/11.jpg | Dark square sweets on a wooden box lined with red rose petals, JL Studio, Taichung
The last dessert is named by the chef after two cities in Indonesia. The Bandung is a grass jelly sweet, unusual to me: it resembles a Thai crisp jelly, but tastes of grass jelly and has a slightly crisp skin. The Ambon is a rose-flavoured ice cream.
