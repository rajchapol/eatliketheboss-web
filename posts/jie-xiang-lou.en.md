Jie Xiang Lou (解香楼) is a two-Michelin-star restaurant beside Hangzhou's West Lake. It was recently promoted from one to two stars in the MICHELIN Guide Shanghai, Jiangsu and Zhejiang announced in April 2026, making 解香楼 the second two-star restaurant in Hangzhou.

The restaurant is run by Chef Yu Bin (俞斌), who is both the general manager of the resort and the restaurant's executive chef. Thai diners may know his name from Purple Laurel (紫桂轩) at Gaysorn Amarin, his first restaurant abroad.

Jie Xiang Lou is the chef's flagship. He started out at the century-old 楼外楼, or Lou Wai Lou, which in those days was called "the Whampoa Military Academy of Hangzhou chefs". He worked there for almost 10 years before leaving to co-found Jie Xiang Lou in 2005, and he has stayed here ever since.

The chef also received the Michelin Mentor Chef Award (导师厨师奖) in the 2026 guide. Michelin's reasons: he has worked with Zhejiang cuisine for decades, blending modern techniques with ingredients from around the world, spreading Zhejiang food culture at home and abroad, and is committed to nurturing young chefs. He shares his recipes freely. He has even recorded a video revealing the recipe for the restaurant's signature braised beef shank, and he welcomes fellow chefs who come to learn, once saying "I'm happy to share with everyone". In his own kitchen he has trained several young chefs born in the 1990s, going as far as taking his team to art galleries to train their artistic eye, and holding seminars with young chefs.

The restaurant sits inside the Seven Villas Hangzhou resort (紫萱度假村) on Bapanling hill, on the western side of West Lake. The resort has seven Chinese villas decorated in the Jiangnan style of the Ming and Qing dynasties, surrounded by bamboo forest, and it is also a member of Relais & Châteaux.

The style of food here is called "creative Jiangnan cuisine" (江南创意菜): local Zhejiang ingredients reinterpreted, bringing together Huaiyang, Hangzhou and Shanghai cooking (the Jiangnan region). My favourite line is something the chef once said: Chinese food can be made creative along two paths. One is local ingredients with the world's cooking methods, such as dry-aging a Jiangnan fish. The other is the world's ingredients with traditional local cooking methods, such as pairing Spanish ham with a dried pickled-vegetable sauce. Everyone knows these approaches, but blending them well is not easy. It sounds simple, but for me it is a definition of creative cooking that strikes just the right balance.

The dish that best sums up the chef's style is the braised beef shank. When he was a child, every Chinese New Year his grandmother would make beef braised in soy in the rich 浓油赤酱 style, the warmth of the festival in his memory. When he became head chef at 解香楼, he reworked this "grandma's dish", and it has been one of the restaurant's best sellers since 2006.

The head chef now running the kitchen has experience at a two-star restaurant in Paris, so the food has a taste profile like French cooking. The restaurant has the beauty of an old Chinese house beside West Lake, and the stone floors, the forest and the curtain of rain at night make it a true destination for lovers of Jiangnan food.

Thank you to Jie Xiang Lou (解香楼), Chef yu.bin, wanyu.yin.7 and ck_csq.

Let's take a look at what was served.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Longjing Tea (龙井)
We began the meal with Longjing, Hangzhou's famous green tea. After walking along the lake in the cold air as the rain came down, it warmed us up before the meal.

### Crab Tartare with Orange and House-Made Fruit Vinegar (新派蟹酿橙)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/01.jpg | Crab tartare in a halved orange, topped with jelly and purple flowers, on a white plate, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
Adapted from 蟹酿橙, crab baked in an orange, a classic Hangzhou dish dating back to the Song dynasty. It is normally served hot, but the version here is a cold crab tartare, with aroma from the orange peel and Chinese wine. Light and fragrant with orange, the jelly is not too sweet or sour, just gentle, which brings out the sweetness of the crab well. There is finely chopped spring onion and water shield (junsai). A beautifully contemporary dish with a Western touch, and a superb evolution of a Hangzhou classic.

### Grilled Corn and Cuttlefish Salad Tart (碳烤玉米墨鱼盏)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/02.jpg | Black tart cups filled with cuttlefish salad and topped with golden crispy threads, on a bed of corn kernels in an earthenware dish, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
Made with cuttlefish from the East China Sea and Hangzhou corn. This bite is light and creamy, the corn chewy, the cuttlefish springy and a little salty, with a gentle kick from garlic and ginger. Another beautifully flavoured, contemporary bite.

### Forest Mushroom Tower (森林菌菇塔)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/03.jpg | Mushroom tarts topped with ridged avocado slices, arranged on fine green strands on a grey ceramic plate, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
A cold tart mixing Yunnan mushrooms with Yunnan avocado, topped with avocado and lemon juice. Sweet and sour from the lemon, slightly creamy, not much mushroom aroma but a crunchy texture to chew on. A sweet-and-sour, slightly rich bite with a contemporary feel.

### Hangzhou-Style Braised Duck and Goose Roll (杭味酱卤双禽卷)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/04.jpg | Slices of duck and goose roll glazed in a red-amber sauce, arranged in a ring on a pale blue scalloped plate, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
This dish is refined from a Hangzhou classic, with goose neck and duck meat simmered in soy sauce and mushrooms. In the mouth the goose neck is crunchy and the duck tender, salty first then sweet, crunchy, chewy and jelly-like, with a faint hint of star anise and a cool fragrance. A cold dish that feels light rather than heavy despite being all protein, with a very beautiful flavour profile.

### Foie Gras Steeped in Rice Wine (米酒浸鹅肝) 🕶️
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/05.jpg | Triangular slices of foie gras terrine in an amber rice-wine sauce, garnished with small white flowers, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
This dish has evolved since the restaurant's early days. It used to be made with yellow wine, but now uses Chinese rice wine, which is lighter in aroma and sweeter than yellow wine, and which I personally think suits foie gras better. The sauce has the saltiness of soy with a gentle sweetness. The foie gras terrine is smooth, soft and light, not overly fatty, with a faint cool fragrance of jasmine. A very good dish, and a really interesting flavour profile for foie gras.

### Sunflower Chicken in Wine-Lees Marinade (糟卤葵花鸡)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/06.jpg | Chopped poached chicken in pale yellow pieces, arranged on a white plate with a red patterned rim, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
葵花鸡 is a Cantonese chicken breed raised on sunflower seeds, here steeped in a marinade made from Shaoxing wine lees. This is a classic of the Jiangnan cold menu. It uses a Hangzhou technique, soaking the chicken in yellow wine and the Shaoxing wine-lees marinade, which gives a gentle bitterness and pungency and a deep flavour like an oxidative-style wine. The chicken is rich but never cloying, with little chicken smell, a light saltiness and a clear aroma of wine.

### Sour Soup with Fish Maw and Jingjie (酸汤荆芥鳘鱼胶)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/07.jpg | Thick yellow soup in a white bowl with a square piece of fish maw, pork tripe and green leaves, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
A seasonal dish. 鳘鱼胶 is the swim bladder of the 鳘鱼, or croaker, while 荆芥, jingjie, is a herb popular in Henan cooking and rarely found in Jiangnan food. The leaf is a little like holy basil. The soup is creamy from fish bones, lightly sour from pickled bamboo shoots and Chinese vinegar, with fish maw and pork tripe. The jingjie leaves give a gentle spicy-bitter note in the throat, cut any gaminess, add depth and make this dish different. A very beautiful flavour profile.

### Jiangnan Crispy Little Fish (江南童子鱼)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/08.jpg | Small golden-brown fried fish arranged on a white plate with a raised dot pattern, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
A classic, served like fried iwashi. Salty and slightly sweet, well fried and not oily. Delicious.

### Braised Mi Yu with Hairy Crab and Shiso (紫苏焖蟹烧米鱼)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/09.jpg | Fish braised with whole hairy crabs in an orange sauce, scattered with shredded shiso, on an oval white plate, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
米鱼, mi yu, literally means "rice fish", the same fish whose swim bladder went into the soup earlier. The flesh is soft and fatty, sweet and rich like cooked rice. The chef braises it with hairy crab in a hairy crab sauce, topped with shiso leaves. Rich in flavour, with heat up front, but the broth is clean and well balanced.

### Lobster Braised with Tofu Skin and Basil (龙虾金不换豆皮煮)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/10.jpg | Red lobster head and tail with lobster meat, bamboo shoots and basil leaves in a pale yellow broth, on a white plate, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
Here the chef uses Australian lobster with bamboo shoots and Hangzhou tofu skin, topped with basil leaves. The young bamboo shoots are not pungent, the broth is creamy with a slight nuttiness from the beans, the tofu skin soft and a little chewy, with a light basil fragrance. The lobster meat is very sweet, with slices of young ginger slipping in a faint warmth.

### Hangzhou-Style Stir-Fried Squid with Chilli (金辣椒菜炒鲜鱿)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/11.jpg | Squid stir-fried with long beans, green soybeans, red chilli and bamboo shoots, in a white bowl, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
The chef stir-fries squid from the East China Sea with beans. The squid is chewy and tender, sweet first, cut by the heat of green chilli, with fat from pork belly and a light note of preserved mustard greens (na chai). A very beautiful flavour that brings out the squid's sweetness well.

### Steamed Wild Duck with Lotus Seeds, Wine-Lees Pork Ribs and Dried Tangerine Peel (陈皮糟骨头莲子蒸水鸭)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/12.jpg | Steamed duck topped with lotus seeds and minced meat in a clear sauce, on a white plate with a floral rim, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
Hangzhou style, topped with lotus seeds, with duck, minced pork and minced duck underneath, preserved tangerine peel and a touch of Shaoxing wine. The duck is firm but not tough, the lotus seeds soft, with a gentle sweetness.

### Signature Beef Shank (金牌小牛肉)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/13.jpg | Glossy dark brown pieces of braised beef shank topped with red leaves, on a blue-and-white porcelain plate, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
The chef's signature that has become a regional dish: the braised beef, the "grandma's dish" I mentioned above, originally named 江南文火小牛肉, Jiangnan slow-fire braised beef shank. The chef recorded a video revealing the steps and standard weights for this dish back in 2009, and it has since been cooked all over the country. The beef is braised until meltingly tender, the marbled shank fragrant with beefy aroma, with a caramel flavour that is not too sweet. A classic dish done truly superbly.

### Stir-Fried Pork Neck with Oil-Preserved Termite Mushrooms and Dried Tofu (油鸡枞豆干炒松板肉)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/14.jpg | Pork neck stir-fried with mushrooms and dried tofu and shredded red chilli, on a blue-and-white porcelain plate, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
油鸡枞 is Yunnan-style termite mushroom preserved in oil, and 松板肉 is the marbled cut of pork neck, stir-fried over high heat with Hangzhou dried tofu. The pork neck is crunchy, mildly spicy, the tofu tastes of five-spice braise, with the sweetness of the mushrooms coming through, a light aroma of Chinese celery and crunchy mushrooms.

### Wagyu Mapo Tofu with Hangzhou Peppers (杭椒和牛麻婆豆腐) 🕶️
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/15.jpg | Bright green mapo tofu with cubes of tofu and minced beef in a clay pot, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
Mapo tofu is a Sichuan dish that normally has to be mala, numbing and hot. Here, instead of mala, it uses wagyu beef from Dalian and Hangzhou green peppers (杭椒). No numbing, just a gentle, fresh green heat. The tofu is creamy and very soft, the flavour fresh and mellow, the beef crisp yet tender, a delicate aroma and a balanced taste. Finely chopped pickles add saltiness and sourness on the finish, adding real depth.

### Pea Shoots with Egg White and Clams (蛤蜊蛋白扒豆苗)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/16.jpg | Pea shoots in an egg-white sauce with clams arranged around them, in a ceramic bowl with a studded rim, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
Light, gently green-bitter and mellow, with umami and chewiness from the clams.

### Milk Skin Yoghurt with Wild Apricot Sauce and Lotus Seeds (莲子奶皮子酸奶配野杏子酱)
image: ./images/jie-xiang-lou/17.jpg | Milk skin yoghurt with an orange apricot sauce, topped with lotus seeds and a green leaf, in a white cup, Jie Xiang Lou, Hangzhou
The only dessert. 奶皮子 is a Mongolian-style thick milk skin, or Urum. Gently sour and creamy, eaten with the apricot jam, which suits it well. The lotus seeds, with their cool fragrance, add a Chinese touch.
