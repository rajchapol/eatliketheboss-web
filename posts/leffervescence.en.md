This is a restaurant that impressed both A and me enormously. We came on a day of such heavy rain that we had to take a taxi, but the experience here is hard to forget, and I will definitely be back.

The first sentence of this review: everything was perfect, worthy of three MICHELIN stars, a Green Star and a Tabelog Silver that really should be Gold.

The service, from the wine pairing and the tableside service to the overall experience, was flawless. It starts at the door, where a server stands waiting for you. The service is precise. You can tell they care about every detail and quietly watch for what guests need without making you feel watched. Believe me, once a candle has burned down by a third, someone comes to replace it. The tableside service is beautiful and fluid, down to setting every plate at the same angle. For the wine pairing you just say how many glasses you want, and the sommelier puts it together beautifully, especially the Morey-Saint-Denis, whose sweet cherry notes lifted the port sauce of the duck perfectly. One of the best meals of the year.

The kitchen is led by Chef Shinobu Namae, winner of the Icon Award at Asia's 50 Best Restaurants 2023. Before opening this restaurant, Chef Namae worked with Michel Bras at Lake Toya (2★) and was sous chef and pastry chef at The Fat Duck, the famous restaurant in England. He is not only interested in cooking but goes very deep into ingredients, to the point of earning a degree in agricultural science.

Every dish shows real care, and the cooking is perfect across the board. I can't find anything to criticise. The food is classic French with a light Japanese air. Some combinations puzzled me personally, like the restaurant's signature turnip with parsley sauce, but I have to admit it is extremely well made.

On ingredients, compared with the other Green Star restaurant on this trip, the quality of the vegetables and the care for sourcing at L'Effervescence go beyond impressive. They do it better than many farm-to-table restaurants. If you look at the restaurant's website, you will find a long list of producers, from the water to the duck, and even more remarkable, all of them are in Japan. Every dish is well rounded, without big jumps in flavour, rising smoothly to the main course, the Nanatani duck, so delicious I was screaming inside. The food may look classic and simple, but believe me, making simple-looking food this impressive is harder than hard.

L'Effervescence is one of those meals that kept me happy for the whole day.

Let's start the meal.

The room is black against bright white tablecloths, with a large window onto a small Japanese garden. The green of the trees and the candlelight make it genuinely relaxing.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Bouquet of vegetable crisps, tofu sour cream
image: ./images/leffervescence/02.jpg | Thin, colourful vegetable crisps in a paper cone on a metal stand beside a cup of tofu cream, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
The welcome drink comes in two versions, with and without alcohol, and is mixed at the table. It is the restaurant's original cocktail of Japanese mugwort tea (yomogi), lemon, honey and Empirical spirit. Before serving, a lemon peel is squeezed over it so the aroma gets your mouth watering. Slightly fruity, a little cherry, gently sweet-sour, light, without much taste of alcohol, and very smooth. A very good welcome drink.

### Sasanishiki risotto with sea lettuce and hokki-clam, sea urchin, housemade caviar, shiso flowers
image: ./images/leffervescence/03.jpg | Risotto topped with uni, caviar and shiso flowers in a shell on seaweed, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
It may look simple, but it isn't. Even the seaweed underneath is there to add the scent of the sea. Served warm, the large Sasanishiki grains are cooked just right, soft and slightly chewy, neither chalky nor mushy. The light cream brings together the sweetness of the uni and the saltiness of the caviar, and the soft rice keeps its texture in line with the uni, before the light, sharp sourness of shiso flowers and tiny pieces of gently chewy clam cut through. A rounded yet complex dish, and excellent.

The chef then presents the vegetables used today. He is all smiles, but judging by the tense hands of the person carrying them, they must be heavy.

Today's breads: first a potato focaccia, soft and fragrant with potato but not very sweet. The texture is light and spongy, unlike the dense potato breads I usually come across. The other is a sourdough, not very sour here, slightly chewy but not much.

### Artisanal vegetables
image: ./images/leffervescence/04.jpg | A salad of many green and red vegetables on a clear glass plate, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
The chef serves a salad of fifty vegetables with a kombu and soramame dressing. Each vegetable is cooked differently to bring out its best. The dressing, gently sweet with umami, lifts the character of each vegetable well. All the aromas and textures in the mouth are great fun: citrus, oregano, leafy greens, beetroot, sweet pepper, even seaweed. A very good dish, but I am still more impressed by Chikyu, the signature of a three-star restaurant in Osaka.

### Tokyo turnip complexity and simplicity
image: ./images/leffervescence/05.jpg | A whole turnip on a white plate with a line of green parsley sauce, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
The server explains that this is Chef Namae's signature. The turnip is slow-cooked for four hours (I would guess baked at a low temperature), finished meunière, and served with parsley and leek sauce.

This dish is well cooked too. It doesn't look hot, but the moment you cut it, steam bursts out. The turnip is very juicy, full of its own sweetness and fragrance. The sauce leads with the green of parsley, followed by the depth of leek, with ham adding further richness.

My one small criticism: the two plates that day didn't taste the same, probably because of the butter in the finishing, and eating an earthy turnip with a sauce led by green notes may be a little much. But it is still admirable.

### Grouper gently poached in whey, bamboo shoots and green asparagus, sakura flower beurre blanc sauce
image: ./images/leffervescence/06.jpg | A white piece of hata grouper with green asparagus and bamboo shoots in a pale pink beurre blanc on a white plate, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
Today's fish is hata, served with a sakura beurre blanc.

I loved this dish. The fish is excellent quality, perfectly cooked, evenly done all the way through, tender but not mushy. What impressed me most was the beurre blanc: airy and not heavy, yet rich enough that the dish never feels bland. The faint scent of cherry blossom adds a dimension that makes a real difference. A very good sauce, and being lighter than a usual beurre blanc, it blends seamlessly with the asparagus. Excellent.

### Nanatani Kyoto duck wood-fired over local mizunara oak from Hinohara Village in Tokyo, port sauce, mountain vegetables, morel mushroom cream sauce 🕶️
image: ./images/leffervescence/07.jpg | Pink roast duck breast with a dark port sauce and mountain vegetables on a white plate, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
This dish is unfair. I ate it and still can't forget it. An outstanding duck, in both ingredient and cooking.

The texture of the duck is superb and very distinctive: a faint aroma of oak, a soft layer of fat with skin that has a light crunch, never chewy, and tender, juicy meat with deep flavour. This is my ideal duck.

The sauce is wonderfully fragrant and different: a light sweetness up front that never feels heavy, followed by a deep umami aftertaste that lingers in the mouth. It went perfectly with the wine pairing.

### Duck thigh and butterbur shoot raviolis, duck consomme
image: ./images/leffervescence/08.jpg | Ravioli in amber duck consommé in a white bowl, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
This is very good as well. The ravioli carry a green, vegetal scent, crunchy kinoko and duck that melts in the mouth. Varied textures that all fit together. The consommé is not too salty, full of umami and beautifully balanced. Even better, you can ask for more until you are full.

### Artisanal Japanese cheeses
image: ./images/leffervescence/09.jpg | Small pieces of several Japanese cheeses on a black stone slab, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
A selection of cheeses made in Japan, each with its own character. One is like a Brie, one like a cheese wrapped in bamboo leaf, slightly green in scent, and one is like a grilled cheese.

### Hassaku, sansho leaves, fromage blanc mousse, milk chocolate
image: ./images/leffervescence/10.jpg | A cylindrical dessert topped with yellow hassaku peel, with a green sauce, on a white plate, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
Today's dessert is candied citrus, cream cheese mousse and sansho. The candy wraps a light cream with mild chocolate hidden in the centre, then comes the citrus, not too strong, slightly astringent and gently sour. The use of sansho is really interesting: it adds pungency and a spicy, sour note to the dessert. I didn't expect it to work, yet it is balanced and delicious.

### Lemon choux / Peanut financier / Apple mousse, nutmeg / Rosemary cream sando / Wase mandarin / Chocolate, ginger
image: ./images/leffervescence/11.jpg | Six petits fours, including a choux, a financier, a cookie sandwich and mandarin jelly, on a white plate, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
Lately, petits fours at most restaurants rarely live up to my expectations, but not here. Every piece is of a very high standard, especially the financier, which is delicious: peanut and kinoko go together wonderfully.

### Matcha
image: ./images/leffervescence/12.jpg | Green matcha in a ceramic cup, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
To finish, matcha whisked at the table. It is prepared thin here, not very strong. The person whisking is so skilled and graceful that it is like watching a performance.

### World Peace
image: ./images/leffervescence/13.jpg | A pale pink drink in a wide cocktail glass, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
Imo shochu with house-made peanut milk. A combination that made me go "what on earth?". Serving a spirit with a scent as strong as imo shochu after such a light course is bold, but it is unbelievably delicious and it works.
