This is a restaurant that left a deep impression on both A and me. We came on a day of heavy rain and had to take a taxi, yet the experience here is hard to forget, and we will certainly want to return.

The first sentence of this review is that everything was perfect, befitting its three Michelin stars, as well as its Green Star and a Tabelog Silver that should really be Gold.

From the wine pairing to the tableside service to the overall experience, everything was faultless. It begins at the entrance, where staff stand waiting for us. The service is polished: you can tell they attend to every detail and watch for guests' needs without making us feel observed. But believe me, the moment a candle has burned down by a third, a waiter hurries over to replace it. The tableside service is beautiful and fluid, even the angle at which plates are set down is almost identical. As for the wine pairing, once we told them how many glasses we wanted, the sommelier arranged it superbly, especially the Morey-Saint-Denis, whose sweet cherry aroma lifted the port wine sauce on the duck dish wonderfully. It was one of the best meals of this year.

The food here is overseen by Chef Shinobu Namae, winner of the Icon Award at Asia's 50 Best 2023. Before opening this restaurant, Chef Namae worked with Michel Bras at Lake Toya (two stars) and was sous chef and pastry chef at the famous British restaurant The Fat Duck. He is not only interested in cooking but is also deeply serious about ingredients, even studying for a degree in agricultural science.

Every dish shows real care, and in terms of execution all of them are perfect, with nothing to fault. The food is classic French with a light Japanese touch. A few dishes left me personally puzzled by the combination, such as the turnip with parsley sauce, the restaurant's signature, but I must admit it was done extremely well.

On ingredients, compared with the other Green Star restaurant on this trip, I found the quality of the vegetables and the attention to the provenance of ingredients at L'Effervescence beyond merely impressive. It does better than many farm-to-table restaurants. If you visit the restaurant's website, you will find the names of many producers, from the drinking water to the duck, and what is more astonishing is that everything is produced in Japan. The flavours of every dish are rounded, without big swings, rising gradually and seamlessly to the peak at the main course, the Nanatani duck, a duck dish so delicious that I screamed inside. The food may look classic and simple, but believe me, making food that looks this simple so impressive is harder than hard.

L'Effervescence is one of those meals I ate and stayed happy about for the whole day.

Let's begin the meal.

The restaurant is decorated in black against bright white tablecloths, with large windows showing a small Japanese garden. The green of the trees is restful on the eyes, and the candles are truly relaxing.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Bouquet of vegetable crisps, tofu sour cream
image: ./images/leffervescence/02.jpg | Thin, multicoloured vegetable crisps in a paper cone on a metal stand, beside a cup of tofu cream, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
The welcome drink comes in two versions, with and without alcohol, and the waiter makes it for us at the table.
It is the restaurant's original cocktail, made from Japanese mugwort tea (yomogi), lemon, honey and Empirical spirit.
Before serving, the lemon peel is squeezed over it so the aroma stimulates the appetite. The taste is slightly fruity, with a hint of cherry, a gentle sweet-sour, light and delicate, with no obvious smell of alcohol and a very smooth texture. A very good welcome drink.

### Sasanishiki risotto with sea lettuce and hokki-clam, sea urchin, housemade caviar, shiso flowers
image: ./images/leffervescence/03.jpg | Risotto topped with sea urchin, caviar and shiso flowers in a shell resting on seaweed, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
It may look like a simple dish, but it is not. Even the seaweed it rests on is there to lend a sea fragrance to the dish. It was served warm. The large-grained Sasanishiki rice is cooked just right, soft and chewy, neither hard in the core nor mushy. A light cream helps blend the sweetness of the sea urchin with the saltiness of the caviar, and the soft rice keeps the texture from jarring against the urchin, before it is cut by the gentle, tangy sharpness of the shiso flowers and tiny pieces of clam with a slight chew. A rounded dish, yet complex and excellent.
The chef then presented the vegetables used today. Though he wore a smile, judging from the strained hands of the person carrying them, they must have been quite heavy.
Today's bread: the first was a potato focaccia,
soft, served with a rich aroma but not much sweetness. The texture is rather light, like a sponge, unlike the dense style of potato bread I often come across.
The other was a sourdough.
The sourdough here is not very sour, with only a slight chew.

### Artisanal vegetables
image: ./images/leffervescence/04.jpg | A salad of green and red vegetables on a clear glass plate, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
The chef served a salad of fifty kinds of vegetables, to be eaten with a kombu and soramame (broad bean) dressing.
The vegetables in this dish are each prepared differently to bring out the best in each one. The dressing, gently sweet and umami, lifts the character of the many vegetables. The array of aromas and textures in the mouth is great fun: citrus, oregano, leafy greens, beetroot, sweet peppers, even seaweed. A very good dish, but I was still more impressed by Chikyu, the signature dish of the three-star restaurant in Osaka.

### Tokyo turnip complexity and simplicity
image: ./images/leffervescence/05.jpg | A whole turnip on a white plate with a line of green parsley sauce, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
The waiter explained that this is Chef Namae's signature. The chef slow-cooks the turnip for four hours (I guess baked at a low temperature), finishes it by frying meunière, and serves it with parsley sauce and leek.
This was well made too. Although it did not look hot, the moment it was cut, steam poured out. The turnip flesh is very juicy, with the full sweetness and fragrance of turnip. The sauce leads with the green aroma of parsley, followed by the richness of leek, deepened further with ham.
There is only one small flaw: the two plates that day did not taste the same. I think this was probably due to the butter finish, and eating the earthy turnip with a sauce led by green aromas may be a bit much. But it is still lovely and well worth admiring.

### Grouper gently poached in whey, bamboo shoots and green asparagus, sakura flower beurre blanc sauce
image: ./images/leffervescence/06.jpg | White hata grouper with green asparagus and bamboo shoots in a pale pink beurre blanc, on a white plate, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
Today's fish dish was hata (grouper), served with a sakura beurre blanc.
I liked this dish very much. The fish is of excellent quality, perfectly cooked, the whole piece evenly done, the flesh tender and not mushy. What impressed me most is the beurre blanc, light rather than heavy, yet rich enough that the dish never feels bland. The subtle dimension from the cherry blossom aroma makes a big difference. A very good sauce, and being lighter than a typical beurre blanc, it blends seamlessly with the accompaniments such as asparagus.
Excellent.

### Nanatani Kyoto duck wood-fired over local mizunara oak from Hinohara Village in Tokyo, port sauce, mountain vegetables, morel mushroom cream sauce 🕶️
image: ./images/leffervescence/07.jpg | Roast duck breast with pink flesh, dark port wine sauce and mountain vegetables on a white plate, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
This dish is cheating. I ate it and still can't forget it. A superb duck dish in both ingredient and cooking.
The duck has a superb, very distinctive texture. A light aroma of oak, a soft layer of fat with skin that is lightly crisp, with a gentle crunch, never tough. The meat is tender, juicy and intensely flavoured. It is my ideal duck dish, truly.
The sauce smells wonderful and distinctive, led by a gentle sweetness that never feels heavy, followed by a deep, long-lasting umami aftertaste in the mouth. It goes very well with the wine pairing.

### Duck thigh and butterbur shoot raviolis, duck consomme
image: ./images/leffervescence/08.jpg | Ravioli in amber duck consommé in a white bowl, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
This was very good too. The ravioli has a green vegetal fragrance, the crunch of mushrooms, and melting duck meat, a wonderful variety of textures that fit together perfectly. The consommé is not too salty and rich in umami, very well balanced.
Even better, we could ask for more until we were full.

### Artisanal Japanese cheeses
image: ./images/leffervescence/09.jpg | Several small pieces of Japanese cheese on a black stone slab, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
A variety of cheeses produced in Japan, each with its own character.
One resembles Brie, one is like a cheese wrapped in bamboo leaf with a slight green aroma,
and one is like a grilled cheese.

### Hassaku, sansho leaves, fromage blanc mousse, milk chocolate
image: ./images/leffervescence/10.jpg | A cylindrical dessert topped with yellow hassaku citrus peel and green sauce, on a white plate, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
Today's dessert was a candy, citrus, cream cheese mousse and sansho. A candy shell wraps a light cream, hiding a mild chocolate in the centre, accented with a citrus that is not too strong, slightly astringent with a gentle sourness. The use of sansho is very interesting: it adds a pungent, tingling sourness to the dessert. A dessert I did not think would work, yet it works: balanced and delicious.

### Lemon choux / Peanut financier / Apple mousse, nutmeg / Rosemary cream sando / Wase mandarin / Chocolate, ginger
image: ./images/leffervescence/11.jpg | Six petits fours including a choux, a financier, a cookie sandwich and an orange jelly, on a white plate, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
The petits fours at most restaurants lately have rarely met my expectations, but here that is not the case at all. Every piece is of a very high standard, especially the financier, which is delicious, with a peanut flavour that goes superbly with the mushrooms.

### Matcha
image: ./images/leffervescence/12.jpg | Green matcha in a ceramic bowl, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
We finished with matcha, whisked by the waiter at our table. The tea here is brewed thin, not too strong. The person whisking is skilled, and the graceful movements are like watching a performance.

### World Peace
image: ./images/leffervescence/13.jpg | A pale pink drink in a wide cocktail glass, L'Effervescence, Tokyo
Imo-shochu (sweet potato spirit) with house-made peanut milk. A combination that made me wonder what on earth was going on: daring to serve a spirit with a character as strong as imo-shochu in a course this light. Yet it is unbelievably delicious and works well together.
