Harutaka Takashi may well be one of the best sushi shapers I have ever met. Many even call him the true successor, and quite possibly the best sushiya of the Jiro lineage today. That alone is reason enough to try a meal at Harutaka, and with a Tabelog silver and three Michelin stars as well, there is no denying this is one of the most famous sushi restaurants in Tokyo.

In its early days this place counted among Tokyo's top sushiya, with scores no lower than Saito or Mizutani. But the prices kept climbing until the value for money shrank, which affected its scores, and now it has become one of the most expensive sushiya in Japan.

Harutaka stands in the famous Ginza district, not far from Shimbashi station. The counter today is quite large, a single piece of hinoki, and some sources say it can seat as many as 14 guests. On my day there were only 12 of us and I already felt cramped. Add the chef's almost pathological stress over temperature and humidity, which means the air conditioning is not switched on at all, and I and many others at the counter found it hard to breathe. The meal felt more oppressive than enjoyable.

The meal cost around 60,000 yen (the price keeps rising, and it seems about to rise again), with a spectacular number of pieces, more than one can comfortably finish. I felt the restaurant could cut both the price and the number of pieces.

I would call the chef's style Neo Jiro. Besides serving tsumami, he is not in a hurry the way his master was. The rice is very bold in flavour, just as many bloggers have said: the shari uses Noto salt and Shiragiku white vinegar, so saltiness leads, with little vinegar aroma, and after eating a lot it can start to feel heavy. Every woman guest at that meal had to ask for less rice. A shari like this may suit certain fish especially well, but on the other hand it is out of balance with some neta, above all the marinated fish.

Setting the rice aside, Harutaka Takashi's shaping is superb, and there is no doubt he is one of the best shapers I have ever met. The chef rubs a little sauce on every piece of neta before shaping. He forms the rice very firmly, in an elongated shape, so firm that when I tested it on purpose the rice did not break at all. Yet in the mouth it does not feel dense: the shari disperses beautifully, and the perfectly crunchy grains slowly melt away, a really wonderful experience.

The ingredients that day ranged from very good to below expectation. The shellfish coming into season were excellent, and pieces such as hamaguri, sayori and meji maguro were probably among the best I have ever eaten. There was also kuro awabi, and uni from Aomori with the brand hidden, though I could guess from the taste that it was Daizen. But the maguro, in two blocks, with the lean red meat taken from the back, from Iwate, and the chutoro and otoro from a 143 kg fish from Kyoto, harakami ichiban, was a little disappointing.

In conclusion, Harutaka is worth trying once for the experience. The chef shapes superbly, but at this price I would not come back a second time.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Soramame
image: ./images/harutaka/01.jpg | Green fava beans in a dark wooden bowl, beside a ceramic cup of tea, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
The first dish was soramame, or fava beans, seasoned with salt that draws out the sweetness of the beans.

### Kinmedai
image: ./images/harutaka/02.jpg | Kinmedai topped with daikon oroshi and sliced spring onion in a dark ceramic bowl, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Kinmedai. At first I was a little puzzled that they dared open with such a strongly flavoured fish. It was kinmedai shabu served with daikon oroshi, the flesh firm and crisp rather than very fatty, and eaten with ponzu to cut the richness it was not heavy. Delicious.

### Asari Clam Dashi Soup with Fresh Wakame
image: ./images/harutaka/03.jpg | Clear soup with green wakame seaweed in a blue-and-white porcelain cup, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
The restaurant's signature: a dashi soup made from asari clams, with fresh wakame. The fresh wakame really is different, slippery and light, crunchy without being too firm, unlike the usual. Delicious.

### Sashimi: Magokarei and Torigai
image: ./images/harutaka/04.jpg | Translucent white magokarei sashimi and dark torigai with wasabi on a pale green ceramic plate, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Two bites of sashimi.

First was magokarei. A very good fish, extremely crisp and springy, top quality.

The torigai was likewise top quality, crisp but not tough, and so sweet that I was startled a torigai could be this sweet and this good.

### Kuro Awabi
image: ./images/harutaka/05.jpg | Sliced kuro awabi in a brown sauce on a folded ceramic plate, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Kuro awabi, served with a sauce made from awabi dashi mixed with soy sauce. The dish was done to the restaurant's standard and to what kuro awabi should be: two textures in a single bite, both chewy and tender. Personally, though, I felt the sauce did not yet reach the level of a good Chinese restaurant's.

### Meji Maguro
image: ./images/harutaka/06.jpg | Half-raw meji maguro in dashi-soy sauce topped with fine white strips of spring onion, in a brown flower-shaped bowl, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Meji maguro with a dashi-soy sauce and Japanese mustard. The fish was served half-cooked, half-raw, from the otoro part of the meji maguro, very sweet and melting with fat. The quality of the fish was superb, perhaps even better than the maguro in today's sushi. Eaten with the bold sauce cut by a gentle mustard, it brings out the sweetness of the fish all the more.

### Minced Hotaruika with Egg Yolk
image: ./images/harutaka/07.jpg | Minced hotaruika mixed with sliced spring onion, topped with a raw egg yolk, in a white ceramic dish, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Minced hotaruika with egg yolk. An odd bite this one, like a cream of grilled squid with a very bold, intense flavour. Personally I felt it was so intense that it may have made the course drop a notch.

### Grilled Tachiuo with Daikon Oroshi
image: ./images/harutaka/08.jpg | Grilled silver-skinned tachiuo with a round mound of daikon oroshi on a line-patterned ceramic plate, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Grilled tachiuo with daikon oroshi. The fish that day was very tender, and fairly heavily salted, set against the sweetness of the daikon. I thought it middling, done to the restaurant's standard.

### Aori Ika
image: ./images/harutaka/09.jpg | White aori ika sushi on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Aori ika. The flesh was very crisp and chewy, it must be top-grade, but I felt its sweetness was overwhelmed by a shari that was a touch too strong.

### Pickled Ginger
image: ./images/harutaka/10.jpg | Thinly sliced pale pink pickled ginger in a white ceramic bowl, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
The pickled ginger here is mild, with a light, airy sourness, presumably to counter the flavour of the shari.

### Sayori
image: ./images/harutaka/11.jpg | Sayori sushi with striped silver skin on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Sayori, a first-rate fish. The flesh was crisp and springy, and the really bold rice went with this fish superbly.

### Akami
image: ./images/harutaka/12.jpg | Deep red akami tuna sushi on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Akami from Iwate. By my estimate it had been aged around seven days. The fish was slightly jelly-like, with a lot of iron flavour even after all that ageing, and eaten with rice this bold, all manner of flavours fought in my mouth. Personally I still preferred the chutoro and otoro here.

### Chutoro
image: ./images/harutaka/13.jpg | Pink marbled tuna belly sushi on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Chutoro, from one of the best cuts. According to the chef, the block used here is Harakami ichiban. But once again, personally I felt the shari was too strong, so much so that it covered the flavour of the fish entirely.

### Otoro
Otoro. The fish was well aged, and its fat, melting slowly in the mouth, suited the restaurant's bold rice perfectly. The grains dispersing gradually in the mouth and the fish melting with fat: delicious.

### Kasugodai
image: ./images/harutaka/15.jpg | Kasugodai sushi with whitish-pink skin on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Kasugodai. This bite was an odd one: the chef cures it lightly with salt, so eaten with such a heavy shari it really is too strong.

### Gunkan Kobashira
image: ./images/harutaka/16.jpg | Gunkan kobashira wrapped in seaweed on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Gunkan kobashira, or baby scallops. From its aroma, if I am not mistaken, the seaweed is the same brand as Grandpa Jiro's (it really is different). The kobashira was sweet and crisp without being tough, and the lively flavour of the shari lifted it appropriately. Delicious.

### Kuruma Ebi
image: ./images/harutaka/17.jpg | Kuruma ebi sushi with red and white stripes on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Kuruma ebi, peeled fresh in front of you and served warm. For women, the chef cuts it for them. The prawn was of good quality, springy and crisp, with the aroma of prawn fat.

### Saba
image: ./images/harutaka/18.jpg | Silver-skinned marinated saba sushi on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Saba. The fish used that day was outstanding, and I felt a little sorry that it had been used for curing. I had expected the cure to make it too strong, but not at all. This was one of the best pieces of the day. Lightly cured, the fish had sweetness leading its sourness, and eaten with the salty rice it went together well.

### Uni
image: ./images/harutaka/19.jpg | Uni gunkan wrapped in seaweed on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
The uni was from Aomori, murasaki variety. From the taste I guess it was brand D, with a sweet, creamy flesh and an unmistakable, deeply memorable aftertaste.

### Hamaguri
image: ./images/harutaka/20.jpg | Simmered hamaguri sushi brushed with sweet sauce on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Hamaguri was an excellent piece. A large clam, simmered just right, not tough, crisp and chewy, with a lightly sweet sauce that went well with the salty, crunchy rice.

### Tekkamaki
image: ./images/harutaka/21.jpg | Three slices of tuna tekkamaki on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
The tekkamaki here is packed with rather firmly pressed rice, a very thin layer of it, with crisp, fragrant seaweed. Well done.

### Anago
image: ./images/harutaka/22.jpg | Anago sushi brushed with sweet sauce on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
Anago has to be called masterful. The flesh melted in the mouth, extremely soft, very well made. Eaten with the sweet sauce and the salty rice, it goes together beautifully. And when you think how hard it is to shape neta this soft into something so beautiful and firm, Chef Harutaka's skill is the real thing.

### Tamagoyaki
image: ./images/harutaka/23.jpg | Golden-yellow block of tamagoyaki on a glossy black counter, Ginza Harutaka, Tokyo
The tamagoyaki here has a slightly crisp surface and a distinct aroma of prawn, with a fairly dry, light texture.
