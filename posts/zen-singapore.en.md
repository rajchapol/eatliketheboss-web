ZEN is a mega-name in Scandinavian cuisine in Asia, welcoming gourmets from all over the world to this world-class dining room.

This review takes you to a restaurant that has held three Michelin stars for many years in a row, regularly appears in the World's 50 Best guide, and has won Tatler Best of Asia, and whose owner-chef Björn Frantzén is regarded as one of the most influential chefs in the world.

Inside a small white European-style building on Bukit Pasoh Road, we are greeted on entering by an open kitchen and Japandic décor that seems to warp us into another world. The meal begins in a very cosy lounge before we move upstairs to the main dining room, and it ends with dessert in a warm library on the third floor, so that the more than three hours today passed quickly.

Zén presents a Nordic-inspired tasting menu at SGD 580++, so it is undeniably one of the most expensive restaurants in the world. The menu changes with the seasons and places importance on excellent produce from every corner of the globe, especially Japan, blended seamlessly with Scandinavian cooking techniques. Every dish carries a Nordic character that subtly weaves in an Asian touch. Scandinavian food is often hard going for the unfamiliar, but compared with its sister restaurant Villa Frantzén in Thailand, I find this place easier to understand and more to the taste of Asians, and the components on the plate look simple yet intricate, befitting the standing of three Michelin stars.

The service is no less impressive: the servers are calm and professional, yet retain a fitting playfulness that leaves us feeling relaxed and warm. The wine list has a varied and interesting range of options, priced in keeping with the restaurant's standing. The dishes I liked today were the Turbot, razor clam, goat butter, artichoke, and the desserts, the Amela tomato, coconut, passionfruit & sansho, and the Sorbet of Salted carrot ice cream, Jin Xuan oolong, Vin de Paille, which have dimension, complexity and difference, yet are well balanced.

As for the other dishes, let's take a look.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Cheese cracker with fennel
image: ./images/zen-singapore/01.jpg | Two orange flower-shaped cheese crackers on a white napkin on a floral plate, Zén, Singapore
A cheese cracker with fennel. This bite has the aroma of creamy, milky cheese, faintly, and the cracker itself has a gentle cheesy fragrance. The flavour is well rounded and not sharply salty, with a light touch of fennel. The texture is soft for a cracker, yet it doesn't feel dry and crumbly. A good bite.

### Cured shima-aji, myoga, hana-sansho
image: ./images/zen-singapore/02.jpg | Thinly sliced shima-aji rolled into a bite, decorated with purple flowers and sliced myoga, on a tall grey ceramic plate, Zén, Singapore
The next bite is shima-aji cured with myoga and hana-sansho. The chef cures the fish to increase its umami, and serves it with jalapeño and finger lime, which give a mild sour heat, with added vegetables such as myoga and hana-sansho that bring a green, pungent freshness. A light-flavoured dish, yet one with dimension and superbly balanced flavours.

### Tartelette Pâté Foie gras
image: ./images/zen-singapore/03.jpg | A black tartlet holding dark red fruit and small flowers on a grey-green ceramic plate, Zén, Singapore
Tartelette Pâté Foie gras with the flowers and fruit of applecorn. At first I thought this bite would be heavier, but it is light, never too heavy. The fruit adds a sweet-tart note that cuts the richness of the foie gras perfectly. The slight astringency of the flowers adds to the astringency and ties everything together beautifully.

### Tartelette of Manganji pepper cream and Fontina cheese espuma
image: ./images/zen-singapore/04.jpg | Two tartlets topped with white cheese foam, black truffle and yellow flowers on a blue ceramic plate, Zén, Singapore
This tartelette is made from a cream of Japanese Manganji pepper and a Fontina cheese espuma, topped with truffle. It has a clear green-pepper flavour without being hot, and a gentle creaminess that goes with the aroma of truffle unbelievably well. The cheese foam on top adds saltiness. Another bite that is complex, well balanced and incredibly delicious.

### Tartelette of shimeji mushrooms
image: ./images/zen-singapore/05.jpg | A tartlet holding shimeji mushrooms and white flowers on a white scalloped-edge plate, Zén, Singapore
The shimeji mushroom tartelette. Personally, this was my least favourite bite. The mushroom is probably lightly fermented, giving an airy sourness, and it is chewy and crisp. It is fine, but I personally felt that with mushroom already in the previous bite, this one seemed a little repetitive, and compared with the other bites, whose composition is complex, this one seems simpler than the ones before.

### Crudo: Zén Réserve caviar, chutoro, argan oil, finger lime
image: ./images/zen-singapore/06.jpg | Tuna crudo topped with caviar and pink flower petals in a clear flower-shaped glass bowl, Zén, Singapore
For this dish the chef mixed akami and chutoro before making the crudo, then topped it with caviar aged in minerals from Germany. A light sourness from the finger lime leads, followed by a faint smokiness from the argan oil. The fish is slightly fatty, which is little fat for chutoro. Light sudachi zest and sour cream help blend the richness with the mild saltiness of the caviar.

### Chawanmushi, king crab, ikura, saffron
image: ./images/zen-singapore/07.jpg | Steamed egg custard topped with orange ikura and crab meat in a ceramic cup on a brown saucer, Zén, Singapore
For this dish the chef served a Scandinavian-style chawanmushi topped with king crab, ikura and sea buckthorn oil. The custard here is fairly creamy, with a faint aroma of saffron, and its texture is closer to cream than to Japanese chawanmushi. The umami of the crab and the roe is set against a gentle note of sudachi zest, with the oil acting as the link that brings it together with the seafood. It is a delicious dish, but personally I wonder whether the flavour is too intense for one of the first dishes.

### Onion, almond & liquorice
image: ./images/zen-singapore/08.jpg | Cream-coloured almond foam dusted with liquorice powder in the bottom of a wide-rimmed beige ceramic bowl, Zén, Singapore
Caramelised onion, almond foam and almond oil, topped with liquorice powder. The aroma of onion leads in this dish, with the sweet umami of the onion soup, a crunch from chopped almonds, and the texture and almond fragrance of the foam, slightly creamy, along with the aroma of liquorice.

### Turbot, razor clam, goat butter, artichoke
image: ./images/zen-singapore/09.jpg | Grilled turbot topped with razor clam and artichoke, in a cream butter sauce dotted with green oil, on a white ceramic plate, Zén, Singapore
This dish is grilled turbot with a goat's butter sauce and lemon thyme, topped with cep and razor clam, and grilled artichoke. It is very good. The turbot is firm and cooked just right, not tough, with a faint buttery aroma and a clear fragrance of lemon thyme, lightly seasoned with a little fine salt. The clam is lightly braised, firm and sweet, set against the gentle green flavour of the cep, and the faint goat's milk aroma lifts the natural deliciousness of the seafood superbly.

### French toast, unagi, blue mussel
image: ./images/zen-singapore/10.jpg | French toast topped with grilled eel and mussels on a black iron stand, Zén, Singapore
For this dish the chef served an open sandwich of kabayaki eel and mussels from Aomori. The Aomori mussels are very soft in texture, set against the crisp, fluffy bread, with a slight note of mustard and the sweetness of caramelised onion. The flavour and texture of the unagi in this bite are not very distinct, with a faint smokiness, before the richness is deepened with lardo. A dish done to the restaurant's standard.

### Grilled scallop, hot sauce, X.O., spruce, chrysanthemum
image: ./images/zen-singapore/11.jpg | Grilled scallop topped with crisp tempura and yellow chrysanthemum petals on an orange pumpkin sauce in a white ceramic dish, Zén, Singapore
It felt as though I had eaten something similar at the sister restaurant in Thailand. The chef chose hand-caught scallops from Scotland, grilled medium rare, served with an XO sauce mixed with pumpkin purée and topped with a tempura of the scallop. The first thing to praise is the quality of the scallop, which is superb and very sweet. It was cooked through at the surface with a slight crispness from the aburi with binchotan. The sauce is rather sweet, the pumpkin carries a slight heat, and there is the character of an Asian XO sauce. It is done quite well.

### A5 Sendai wagyu, Périgourdine jus, wasabi, kabu
image: ./images/zen-singapore/12.jpg | Pink grilled wagyu topped with fresh wasabi in a dark jus, beside white kabu mousse and leafy greens, on an octagonal ceramic plate, Zén, Singapore
Our main dish today is A5 beef from Sendai, served with an oxtail jus made with fortified wine and coffee. The accompaniments are a kabu mousse, kabu being the Japanese turnip, and fresh wasabi.

The beef is excellent, as standard, as it should be, but what impressed me most was how well the sauce works, with a sweet aroma from the wine and the coffee though it does not taste sweet, a faint vanilla, coffee, and the appetising aroma of the oxtail jus, all of which go superbly with the pungency and sweetness of top-quality fresh wasabi. At first I thought this dish would be heavier and more cloying than this, but it turned out very well.

### Amela tomato, coconut, passionfruit & sansho
image: ./images/zen-singapore/13.jpg | A round red-orange tomato sorbet in a glass bowl on crushed ice in a wooden box, Zén, Singapore
Our palate cleanser is a consommé with passionfruit, strawberry compote and an Amela tomato sorbet. The flavour of the sansho acts like chilli salt, enhancing the sweetness of the strawberries and of the sorbet made from excellent tomatoes. The liquid below, made from passionfruit, adds umami and dimension to the dish in an interesting way. A dish that looks simple but is distinctive, and also superbly executed.

### Sorbet (Salted carrot ice cream, Jin Xuan oolong, Vin de Paille)
image: ./images/zen-singapore/14.jpg | Carrot ice cream on white cream, decorated with a golden lace waffle and matcha powder, in a cream ceramic bowl, Zén, Singapore
This is probably one of the craziest desserts I have eaten this year. Superb. It is a salted carrot sorbet with persimmon, hazelnut cream, honey, wine from the Jura and the elegant aroma of oolong tea, then decorated with a gyokuro green tea waffle and matcha powder. Delicious, complex and elegant, the elements combine unbelievably well and with dimension. Superb.
