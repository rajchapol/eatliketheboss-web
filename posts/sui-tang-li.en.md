Sui Tang Li (随堂里) is the contemporary Chinese restaurant of The Upper House Shanghai. It sits on the second floor of the Upper House Shanghai Apartments on Shimen Yi Road (石门一路) in Jing'an, with Chef Tony Ye at the helm as Chef de Cuisine.

The restaurant describes itself as a Contemporary Shanghai Bistro, bringing Cantonese, Sichuan and Shanghainese cooking together. It has held one Black Pearl diamond for seven years running and is listed in the MICHELIN Guide Shanghai as a Selected restaurant. It opened in 2018 alongside The Middle House hotel, which was renamed Upper House Shanghai at the end of 2025.

The name sounds like the Sui (隋) and Tang (唐) dynasties, the golden age of Chinese culture, while also suggesting something relaxed and free of ceremony. The restaurant spreads over three floors linked by a spiral staircase designed by Piero Lissoni. The second floor is the main dining room, wrapped in floor-to-ceiling glass; the third floor, 随堂上, is for private events; and the ground floor has four new private rooms by Mitchell & Eades, inspired by the curves of the qipao and the lanes of Shikumen (石库门) houses.

Chef Tony Ye (叶剑) is a Shanghai native who started cooking because his grandfather taught him that a man should always have a skill to his name. At ten, he tried braising a pork knuckle and poured in an entire bottle of soy sauce until it burnt. He began his career in the 1990s in the dim sum section of the Hilton Shanghai, then spent three years working in Singapore, passed through the kitchen of Jin Sha at the Four Seasons Hangzhou, and was Sous Chef at Yong Yi Ting, a one-Michelin-star restaurant in Shanghai, before taking charge of this kitchen from the day it opened. On the question of authenticity, he once said: "Chillies only arrived in China a few hundred years ago. Before that, Sichuan food wasn't spicy. So what is authentic? Delicious is authentic." Those words sum up the food here rather well.

This is a relaxed, unpretentious place, and the team goes on research trips regularly in search of fresh inspiration. When I visited, there was a new menu inspired by Shandong cooking, called "Qilu Impressions" (齐鲁印象); Qilu is the old name for Shandong Province.

Overall, for me, Sui Tang Li is relaxed, delicious and good value: innovative cooking with no boundaries, blending every style with a real sense of fun and finished with finesse. It breaks the mould yet stays easy to understand, not only for Chinese diners but for visitors from abroad too, and for everyone, young and old.

The location is excellent and close to the shops: HKRI Taikoo Hui, home to a Starbucks Reserve Roastery, is just a few minutes' walk away. Nearby you also have the old Zhangyuan (张园) lane houses, the Wujiang Road (吴江路) pedestrian street and Plaza 66 on West Nanjing Road, so it is an easy stroll over for lunch.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Ibérico Ham Scallion Pancake with Hazelnut Foie Gras (脆糖榛果鹅肝酱，伊比利亚火腿葱油饼)
image: ./images/sui-tang-li/01.jpg | Golden fried scallion pancake rolls lined up on a long white plate with a cup of foie gras dip sprinkled with hazelnut, Sui Tang Li, Shanghai
A simple scallion pancake made more refined. Here the pancake is filled with finely diced jamón ibérico, the Spanish black-pig ham, and eaten with a foie gras dip topped with crisp candied hazelnuts. The pancake is light and crisp with a gentle saltiness from the ham, while the dip is light rather than heavy, with a faint note of liver and a sweet-salty balance. A distinctly modern touch.

### Mala Dalian Abalone with Century Egg, Laba Garlic and Charred Chilli (腊八蒜烧椒皮蛋，麻辣大连鲍)
image: ./images/sui-tang-li/02.jpg | Sliced abalone with century egg, red charred chillies and coriander on a blue ceramic plate, Sui Tang Li, Shanghai
Thinly sliced Dalian abalone with century egg, given extra depth by Laba garlic (腊八蒜), a northern Chinese vinegar-pickled garlic traditionally made on the Laba festival, the eighth day of the twelfth lunar month, until the cloves turn jade green. This was better than I expected. The sauce leads with sourness and heat, with a light thread of sweetness, never heavy, and the chilli heat at the finish cuts through any richness. The century egg is superb: wonderfully creamy, the white set to a jelly, with no odour at all. Its texture plays against the crunch of the abalone, while the garlic is sweet and sour, rather like black garlic.

### Ibérico Ham Xiaolongbao (伊比利亚火腿小笼包)
image: ./images/sui-tang-li/03.jpg | Red-and-white patterned xiaolongbao, uni siu mai and jade dumplings in a bamboo steamer, Sui Tang Li, Shanghai
Sweetness leads, with the fragrance of pork broth and umami so rich it needs no sauce at all, plus pieces of pork inside.

### Uni Siu Mai (海胆烧麦)
image: ./images/sui-tang-li/04w.jpg | Siu mai topped with orange uni in a bamboo steamer, alongside red-patterned xiaolongbao and jade dumplings, Sui Tang Li, Shanghai
Pork and prawn siu mai crowned with uni (sea urchin), with a little broth at the bottom. A delicious bite with a clear taste of the sea.

### Black Truffle Vegetable Dumplings (黑松露翡翠饺)
image: ./images/sui-tang-li/05.jpg | Jade dumplings with thin wrappers showing a green vegetable filling, in a bamboo steamer beside xiaolongbao and uni siu mai, Sui Tang Li, Shanghai
Spinach and truffle dumplings. The Chinese name, "feicui jiao" (翡翠饺), means jade dumplings, after the green of the vegetables showing through the wrapper. The wrapper is thin with a gentle chew, not tough, and cooked just right; the filling carries a soft scent of truffle, sweet first, then savoury.

### Winter Melon, Ham and Fish Maw Soup (冬瓜茸火腿鱼肚汤)
image: ./images/sui-tang-li/06.jpg | Clear golden winter melon soup with fish maw, topped with green shoots, in a white porcelain tureen, Sui Tang Li, Shanghai
A Cantonese double-boiled soup of finely minced winter melon, ham and fish maw. The aroma of Yunnan ham comes through clearly, yet the soup is not as salty as the scent suggests: gently sweet, with a soft umami. The winter melon is braised until it melts, its flesh sweet.

### Xiamen Crab with Mochi Cake
image: ./images/sui-tang-li/07.jpg | A whole red-orange crab in a rich sauce with round mochi cakes in a black bowl, Sui Tang Li, Shanghai
Inspired by the classic crab steamed on glutinous rice, but reworked with mochi cake instead. The chef uses a Xiamen crab full of roe, one of the finest crabs in China. The cake is soft and chewy and the crab meat very sweet. The sauce has an aroma that hits you straight away and looks rich and appetising, yet it is not so strong that it masks the crab.

### Peking Duck Katsu
image: ./images/sui-tang-li/08.jpg | Golden battered Peking duck katsu on a wooden tray with two small cups of sauce and a steamer of pancakes, Sui Tang Li, Shanghai
The chef wraps pulled duck in its skin, coats it in batter and fries it, then serves it with the trimmings of Peking duck and two sauces: the traditional one and a Japanese katsu sauce. The duck is nicely fried, the batter thin and crisp, the meat inside soft, light and fluffy.

### Hong Shao Rou with Abalone and Pumpkin (鲍鱼红烧肉老南瓜)
image: ./images/sui-tang-li/09.jpg | Glossy red-braised pork belly alternating with roasted pumpkin on a white embossed plate, Sui Tang Li, Shanghai
Shanghai's famous red-braised pork, hong shao rou, elevated by Chef Tony. It looks beautiful: several kinds of sugar go into it, the meat looks gorgeous, and it is served with roasted pumpkin and braised abalone. The sauce coats the pork just enough, and the pork is tender with no porky odour at all. Gently sweet and never too heavy, the flavour is perfectly judged, and it goes beautifully with hot steamed rice.

### Steamed Marble Goby with Tofu and Green Chilli Sauce (豆花青酱笋壳鱼) 🕶️
image: ./images/sui-tang-li/10.jpg | Whole steamed marble goby on silken tofu, dressed with green chilli sauce and sliced spring onion, on a long white plate, Sui Tang Li, Shanghai
Marble goby steamed with soft, silken tofu and dressed with a green chilli sauce and green Sichuan peppercorn. The sauce takes its sweetness from the vegetables and is light, with a clear scent of green Sichuan peppercorn but not much numbing tingle, and it brings out the sweetness of the fish brilliantly. The tofu is soft and wonderfully creamy. Excellent.

Marble goby (笋壳鱼) is a prized, expensive freshwater fish that the Cantonese love to steam for its fine white flesh, its sweetness and its lack of small bones. Steamed like this, it is utterly delicious.
