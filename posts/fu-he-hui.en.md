Fu He Hui, or 福和慧, translates literally as happiness, harmony and wisdom. This restaurant is a true revolution, one that raises Chinese vegetarian cooking to a new level by refusing the "mock meat" made from flour or soy that has long been a tradition of Chinese vegetarian food.

Instead, Fu He Hui chooses to celebrate the essence of nature, presenting the purity, true flavour and beauty of vegetables, wild mushrooms and seasonal produce through the lens of Zen philosophy, with its focus on calm and balance.

The restaurant brings vegetarian cooking firmly into the present, so even those who don't usually eat vegetarian can enjoy every course. The chef has a deep understanding of umami and of playing with texture and temperature, using advanced techniques that bring the culinary arts of East and West together.

The restaurant sits on Shanghai's Yuyuan Road. It opened in 2014 under Fang Yuan's Fu Group, with the vision of creating a retreat from the chaos at the heart of the metropolis. It is tucked away in a meticulously renovated three-storey villa, quietly simple and serene in true Zen style. Service is gentle, hushed and seamless; the staff know the origins and properties of every ingredient in depth and explain them in excellent English. I highly recommend the Tea Pairing: the restaurant selects rare, premium Chinese teas and brews them at precisely the right water temperature to lift each course flawlessly. Little wonder it has earned two Michelin stars and two Black Pearl Diamonds, and appears on Asia's 50 Best year after year.

Fu He Hui is truly the pinnacle of vegetarian cooking in the Eastern world. It dares to take the "vegetable", so often overlooked on the plate, and weave it into an art of flavour that is complex and luxurious. This is a gastronomic experience that proves Chinese vegetarian cuisine needs neither bold, punchy seasoning nor imitation meat to be delicious. Instead, world-class technique can draw out the full potential of vegetables, on a par with the haute cuisine of the West. A few dishes still find the balance of aromas a challenge, but overall this is a meal well worth the journey to experience at least once.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Cucumber hawthorn jam osmanthus flower
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/01.jpg | Two crisp rice crackers topped with shredded cucumber and red jam on a black stand, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
The meal opens with a welcome bite: crisp rice crackers topped with shredded cucumber, bright, sweet and sour and refreshing from the osmanthus sauce. A lovely way to wake up the palate.

### GOLDEN FUNGUS zucchini pine nut
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/02.jpg | Mushroom glazed in a glossy brown sauce on a green zucchini salad in a cream ceramic bowl, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
The mushroom is marinated and baked, served on zucchini and topped with pine nuts. Its texture is superb, rather like sea cucumber. The flavour leads with the sweet-salty notes of soy and a faint hint of Chinese celery, set against the earthiness of the pine nuts. The zucchini salad adds another dimension, lightly salty with a gentle heat from a touch of Sichuan peppercorn oil; the green mala lifts the sweetness of the mushroom perfectly.

### WALNUT tofu skin jicama
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/03.jpg | Crisp fried tofu-skin rolls standing in a row, dusted with green seaweed powder, on betel leaves on a dark brown plate, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
Jicama, beans and walnut wrapped in tofu skin, sprinkled with seaweed powder and served on betel leaves. The flavour is more unexpected than you might think: sweetness first, with the distinctive aroma of jicama, then the scent of white beans and the earthiness of walnut, all led by the aroma of seaweed. It looks like a simple dish, yet the flavours come together beautifully.

### CHESTNUT napa cabbage bamboo pith
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/04.jpg | Yellow napa cabbage heart in a golden-orange sauce with chestnut powder sprinkled behind, on a dark ceramic plate, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
The heart of a napa cabbage, steeped in vegetable broth and served with chestnut sauce and chestnut powder. Sweetness leads, the texture is wonderfully soft and tender, the chestnut comes through clearly, and an umami aftertaste lingers in the throat. At its centre, a filling of wood ear mushroom and bamboo shoot adds crunch.

### TARO cauliflower gorgon fruit
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/05.jpg | A tall dark brown cup topped with a fluffy white cloud of crisp fried taro, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
Taro from Guangxi, cauliflower from Ningbo and fox nuts (gorgon fruit). The fox nuts bring crunch against a silky cauliflower purée, topped with crisp fried taro as light as cotton, with a salty lead. A dish that balances salt and sweetness in perfect harmony.

### BEAN CURD PUFF daylily bud dried opo squash pea sprout
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/06.jpg | Brown tofu-skin rolls beside tender green shoots on a wide-rimmed dark ceramic plate, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
Tofu skin soaked in Ningbo squash broth, wrapped around lily buds and squash, and served with the greens of the day. The sauce is sweet with a soft note of Chinese spices, led by the soy aroma of the bean curd. The tender shoots are beautifully chosen, each carrying a light breath of wok hei, truly showing off the fresh crunch of the vegetables.

### ROOT lily bulb, truffle, ficus hirta, burdock
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/07.jpg | Baked lily bulb lightly charred on the outside, beside fine strands of crisp fried burdock root, on a layered black plate, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
Lily bulb baked with black garlic sauce, truffle and onion, topped with crisp fried burdock root (gobo). The sweetness of the onion balances the earthiness of the truffle just right, the garlic and onion whet the appetite, and the crisp gobo adds texture. The real triumph is that the truffle never steals the show; it melts harmoniously into the various vegetables.

### EIGHT TREASURE lotus root water chestnut ginkgo baby corn choy sum celery arrowhead bell pepper
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/08.jpg | A small mound of stir-fried mixed vegetables, baby corn, red bell pepper and greens, in a dark bowl, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
Eight kinds of vegetable stir-fried in a Jiangnan-style sweet and sour sauce. The flavour is light, sweet first with a gentle sourness that brings out the natural taste of the vegetables, with a faint toasted note from the wok. It goes nicely with the pairing, a smoky Tieguanyin roasted with corn. The vegetables are cooked just right, still crisp and fresh.

### MUSHROOM KUT TEH maitake, nameko, wheat gluten 🕶️
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/09.jpg | Clear brown mushroom soup with maitake and assorted mushrooms in the centre, sprinkled with sliced spring onion, in a dark grey ceramic dish, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
One of the restaurant's signatures, a 100% vegetable soup. On the tongue it feels soothing and moist in the throat, with clear herbal notes of Chinese yam and liquorice, yet it stays soft and gentle, with no pepper at all. All of the umami is drawn from a host of mushrooms, led by maitake. It isn't the intensely rich, punchy kind of soup you find in typical fine dining, but it is beautifully rounded and utterly unforgettable.

### BAMBOO SHOOT dried mustard green, soybean milk
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/10.jpg | Grilled bamboo shoot with a white soy cream sauce in the centre of a brown stone-patterned ceramic plate, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
Bamboo shoot wrapped in bamboo leaf and grilled over charcoal, served with a fermented soybean sauce and pickled mustard greens. It recalls the food of Yunnan or Myanmar. The bamboo shoot is naturally crisp and delicious, with a soft green scent from the bamboo leaf. The sauce is creamy from the soy, but the pickled mustard greens push a bitter, earthy note to the front, which I felt didn't quite sit well in this dish.

### CARROT wild carrot dried apricot daikon
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/11.jpg | Baked rice with sliced orange and yellow carrots, spooned into a brown leaf-shaped ceramic bowl, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
Wuchang rice baked with orange and yellow carrots, vegetable stock and dried apricot. The carrots are sweet and tender, cooked evenly all the way through. The rice at the bottom turns golden and crisp, almost like a rice crust, yet with no burnt bitterness: a perfect cook. The technique and ingredients are flawless, but in terms of flavour, I think a touch more salt to cut through would make this dish even more complete.

### PERSIMMON gum karaya almond
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/12.jpg | Almond pudding topped with a clear orange persimmon sauce in a tall metal cup on a wooden table, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
Almond pudding with persimmon, given a slightly gooey texture by gum karaya, the tree resin often used to make "snow bird's nest". The persimmon comes in several textures, as both sauce and fruit. The pudding is silky, with a cool, fragrant aroma. Eaten separately, each part is delicious, but together the persimmon doesn't quite blend with the cool fragrance of the almond as well as it should.

### SWEET POTATO pumpkin pumpkin seed sesame red glutinous rice
image: ./images/fu-he-hui/13.jpg | An oval sweet potato cake with a caramelised top in a yellow sweet potato and pumpkin sauce, dusted with cinnamon, in a black bowl, Fu He Hui, Shanghai
Inside is a glutinous rice cream, mochi-like but looser, blended with sweet potato and roasted sweet potato. The red glutinous rice at the centre has a lovely texture, with a light hint of cinnamon, a crisp surface and a gentle sweetness. Pumpkin seeds and sesame add crunch, and it is finished with a sweet potato and pumpkin sauce. It brings to mind the Thai orh nee with ginkgo, but this version is in a different league altogether.
