Les Amis, which I am reviewing today, is a thoroughly classic restaurant with one of the longest histories in Singapore's fine dining scene.

I was welcomed warmly, and the staff began a polite yet friendly conversation, after I had spent a long time looking for Les Amis. The restaurant is tucked away on Scotts Road, not far from Orchard Road, the island's famous shopping district.

Les Amis is one of the best restaurants in Southeast Asia, ranked 33rd in The World's 50 Best (2020), awarded three Michelin stars (2020), and best of all, it is not hard to book.

Chef Sébastien Lepinoy's Les Amis cooks classic Haute Cuisine using seasonal ingredients. Perhaps because Singapore is not limited by local produce, the food comes together beautifully. There may not be a combination that wows, or a surprise in every dish as at the newer Innovative restaurants, but every dish is well rounded and excellent, perfect even by the highest standards. The price may look a little high given the number of dishes, but compared with what you get, with caviar and truffle crammed in with a bang in nearly every dish, I think it is acceptable.

The restaurant currently has two kinds of menu, à la carte and set menus. The set menus are a four-course menu combining the restaurant's signature classic dishes (295 SGD) and a six-course seasonal menu, which at the moment is built around truffle (460 SGD). With a price difference of around 170 SGD and a much more spectacular-looking menu, it was an easy decision to choose the truffle set.

The service was excellent, especially the sommelier, who recommended wines that were not very expensive but of high quality and matched the food perfectly, such as the Louis Moreau Premier Cru Vaillons, which paired interestingly with the truffle, and a sweet wine, the Rivesaltes 1979 from Riveyrac, with a coffee aroma that went very well with both desserts.

The truffles used are a specially selected grade from the brand Plantin in Provence; every truffle must be larger than 80 g and perfectly round.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Amuse Bouche Tartlet
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/01.jpg | Tartlet topped with caviar on a wooden board, Les Amis, Singapore
Lobster mousse, cherry vinaigrette and chopped onion, topped with crystal caviar. The pastry in this bite is very thin and crisp. The flavour is mild, with sourness leading, followed by the sweet umami of the smooth lobster mousse.

### Bread and butter
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/03.jpg | A cart of assorted breads in the dining room, Les Amis, Singapore
The bread cart here is spectacular, with raisin and nut bread, baguette, mini croissant and sourdough, and we can choose any of them and ask for more at any time. The highlight for me was the choux pastry with 48-month aged cheese, served piping hot, which was absolutely delicious. The dough is light and slightly chewy, and the cheese mildly salty, rich and very mellow.

The butter at Les Amis comes as a very large block. The restaurant uses butter from the Loire Valley, from the Pamplie cooperative, which still uses the old-fashioned, handmade method of butter production before pasteurising it and sending it directly to Les Amis.

### Mushroom tart with confit onion and truffle
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/05.jpg | Mushroom tart topped with sliced truffle on a white ceramic bowl, Les Amis, Singapore
A mushroom tart with onion confit cooked in bacon fat and Madeira, topped with truffle. Done superbly: the pastry is wafer-thin, and the aromas of the two mushrooms go with the bacon perfectly, followed by a flavour that is complex yet truly well judged. There is the saltiness of the bacon, the slight acidity of the Madeira and the gentle salty umami from the onion.

### Watercress soup with chicken bouillon and truffle
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/06.jpg | A cup of watercress soup topped with truffle beside black sesame bread, Les Amis, Singapore
Watercress velouté with chicken bouillon, topped with truffle, eaten with black sesame bread sprinkled with black sesame. This dish too is very well balanced. The gentle saltiness of the chicken soup cuts the sweetness of the bread, tinged with the slight bitterness of the watercress. The earthy aroma of the sesame, the green aroma of the watercress and the delicate truffle go together very well.

### Le Crabe "Kegani" & Caviar
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/07.jpg | Hairy crab roll topped with caviar beside a red tomato rose on a dotted plate, Les Amis, Singapore
A roll of Hokkaido hairy crab with an herb sauce, with a rose made of tomato on the side; we mix everything together and eat it. This dish is excellent too. There is a faint, refreshing herb fragrance and a well-rounded flavour, as the mild saltiness of the caviar sets off the gentle sweetness of the crab perfectly.

### La Langoustine de Loctudy & Olive oil
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/08.jpg | A server pouring sauce over langoustine wrapped in courgette and topped with caviar, Les Amis, Singapore
Langoustine from Loctudy in Brittany, wrapped in courgette and dressed with an olive oil sauce from Provence. The large langoustine is cooked to perfection, springy and very sweet and rich, and it goes with the courgette, which helps blend the seafood flavour with the fresh-scented olive oil. The caviar on top adds a mild saltiness that boosts the umami of the langoustine very well. Absolutely heavenly.

### Le Zéphyr De Céleri À La Truffe Noire
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/09.jpg | Celeriac soufflé covered in sliced truffle in a white ceramic bowl, Les Amis, Singapore
A celeriac soufflé with truffle, finished with olive oil from Provence and truffle. The technique in this dish is very good throughout, but something seems to be missing. The truffle is appetisingly fragrant and earthy, and goes well with the soft tenderness of the soufflé, which is mildly salty. My one criticism of this dish is that the celeriac aroma doesn't really come through.

### Le Turbot En Demi-Deuil
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/10.jpg | Turbot with fresh truffle and potato soufflé on a brown-rimmed plate, Les Amis, Singapore
Turbot with a truffle hollandaise emulsion, eaten with fresh truffle and a potato soufflé. This dish is good in the classic way, with everything cooked perfectly. It looks plain, but every element is immaculate. The fish is of the highest quality, with crisp skin and juicy, sweet flesh, and the salty, rich sauce scented with truffle goes with it beautifully.

### Le Boeuf À La Moelle Et Truffe Noire
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/11.jpg | Wagyu tenderloin topped with bone marrow and truffle in a dark bordelaise sauce, Les Amis, Singapore
Wagyu tenderloin with Bordelaise sauce, topped with truffle and diced beef bone marrow. It is another utterly classic dish of Haute Cuisine, and another good, well-rounded one. The beef is very tender, and the fat of the marrow has a faint, appetising fragrance of truffle. The Bordelaise leans sweeter than usual, which I think takes into account the balance with the carrots served as the garnish. It is good, but I have a small criticism: there is an olive oil aroma in this dish that I think doesn't go with the bone marrow.

### Sable Carrot
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/12.jpg | Carrots sautéed in butter and orange in a pale blue ceramic bowl, Les Amis, Singapore
Carrots sautéed in butter and orange. The restaurant uses sable carrots, famous from the La Manche coast in Normandy, which are covered in sand to preserve their fresh sweetness. These carrots are extremely sweet with a slight sourness, partly from the orange, I suppose. There are faint herb and pepper notes and a little butter fragrance that is not cloying. So delicious I wanted to ask for more.

### Chocolate Tart
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/13.jpg | A triangular slice of chocolate tart on a white plate, Les Amis, Singapore
A cocoa tart, for which the restaurant blends Ecuadorian and Venezuelan cocoa. The texture is very soft, but neither sticky nor mushy. It is bitter and just slightly sweet, exactly right, even though nothing has been added to sweeten it; the sweetness probably comes from the milk. It is delicious and remarkable that a dessert is made without any sugar at all. This dish goes very well with the sweet wine, with its coffee and chocolate aromas.

### Le Café Sphere
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/14.jpg | A golden caramel sphere on coffee sauce, Les Amis, Singapore
A caramel sphere, coffee ice cream, coffee mousse and coffee coulis, made with Jamaican Blue Mountain coffee. I liked the layering and the balance of flavour in this dish, with every element perfectly executed. The first bite gives the bittersweet taste of coffee, beginning with the lightness of the mousse followed by the coffee, then the rich, sweet and salty caramel, crisp at first before turning chewy in the mouth, as the bitterness slowly fades away. A well-made dessert with real class.

### Petit Four
image: ./images/les-amis-2020/15.jpg | Canelé, chestnut tart and chocolate on a blue-and-white patterned plate, Les Amis, Singapore
The petits fours here include a canelé, which is crisper than any I have had before, not sweet and vanilla-scented, very well done. There is a chestnut tart, very fragrant but a touch too sweet, and a hazelnut chocolate, not very sweet, bitter with a slight sourness at the end, balancing the cocoa and hazelnut very well. The pastry chef here is very good with chocolate.
