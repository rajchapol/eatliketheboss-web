ZEA is a Latin American fine-dining restaurant from Chef Joaquín Elizondo Hourbeigt, an Argentine chef, tucked away in a quiet lane in Da'an (大安) on the east side of Taipei. The restaurant earned one MICHELIN star in 2023 and has held it every year through 2026.

The name "ZEA" comes from the Latin genus name for corn (Zea mays), a humble ingredient that stands for Latin American cooking.

Chef Joaquín studied cooking in Buenos Aires and worked with the Park Hyatt group before moving to Hong Kong, where he was Sous Chef at MONO under Chef Ricardo Chaneton, the man who pushed him to go back and explore his own Latin roots. He then opened ZEA in 2022 with his Taiwanese wife, Di-Harn Yeh, who runs the front of house. The cooking here pairs French technique with fiery Latin flavours, but what sets it apart is that the chef aims to use around 80% Taiwanese produce. Every dish on the menu therefore comes with the name of the place its ingredients come from, from Chiayi corn to Mangalica pork from Changhua.

For me, Latin American food has a flavour profile that feels rather more unfamiliar than the Asian food I know.

The kitchen plays with new and unusual ingredients from both Asian and Latin American cooking. Playing with bitterness, from vegetables and flowers alike, and the textures of all kinds of vegetables, are what make this place stand out.

The tortillas, meanwhile, are seriously delicious. Looking back, Southeast Asian and Latin American cooking share a lot of ingredients. The service can slip a little when the room is busy, with plating, clearing bottles or wiping the coasters, but overall this is a meal for anyone who wants to explore something new.

The food here may not be for everyone, but there is no denying that the execution on the plate is superb. The food is playful, and it is a style that is hard to find in Asia.

Before the meal began, the team brought over a tray of the Taiwanese produce used that evening: cactus, chilacayote squash, citrus and sakura shrimp. Let's see what was on the menu.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Cereales (Chiayi Corn / Pingtung Sakura Shrimp / Tainan Star Fruit)
image: ./images/zea/01.jpg | Fried corn topped with sakura shrimp and herbs on a white ceramic plate, beside an earthenware cup of amber drink, ZEA, Taipei
Fried corn with a texture rather like waxy corn, with a spicy, sour salsa. The sakura shrimp on top bring a sea aroma, umami and crunch, and the star fruit is astringent. It was paired with the restaurant's own craft beer, which has an intriguing taste rather like a sour beer: sweet-sour up front, it tames the heat of this bite, though a little chilli warmth lingers on the finish.

### Caldito (Changhua Celtuce / Yerba Mate)
image: ./images/zea/02.jpg | Pale yellow pieces of celtuce stem in a clear broth, in a grey ceramic bowl, ZEA, Taipei
Caldito means a small cup of broth. The chef pours the broth at the table over pieces of celtuce stem. The broth is made from celtuce with yerba mate (Argentina's national herbal tea), kombu and onion. It is bitter, with a distinctive light yerba mate aroma. Both main ingredients in this dish have a bitterness all their own, and the chef said he wanted to bring them together into something that is both Latin and Taiwanese. A truly interesting dish.

### Vuelve a la Vida (Yilan Red Shrimp / Changhua Tomato)
image: ./images/zea/03.jpg | Red shrimp in tomato salsa scattered with pinkish-white garlic flowers, in a blue ceramic bowl, ZEA, Taipei
A South American-style shrimp cocktail whose name means "back to life". It originally comes from Mexico and is known as a hangover cure. It tastes lighter than you would expect, led by the scent of garlic flowers, with sweet shrimp. The tomato salsa underneath does not mask the Taiwanese red shrimp, and a shrimp-head jelly adds a gentle umami. A good palate cleanser.

### Pan de Campo (Our Bread / Zuccardi Family Olive Oil)
image: ./images/zea/04.jpg | Two olive oils in a multi-well ceramic dish, beside homemade bread in a white napkin, ZEA, Taipei
The restaurant's homemade bread, served with two olive oils from Familia Zuccardi, the well-known winemaking family from Mendoza, Argentina.

### Tallarines Verdes (Nantou Chilacayote Squash / Taixi Clam / Yunlin Ceylon Spinach)
image: ./images/zea/05.jpg | Noodles on a green foam sauce in a wide-rimmed cream ceramic bowl, ZEA, Taipei
Tallarines verdes are Peruvian noodles in green sauce. This is another dish that plays with bitterness. The noodles are green from chilacayote squash (a squash native to Central America), served with a foam sauce of clams, parsley and coriander, and there is bitterness from the chayote shoots underneath. The dish is gently astringent and umami, not as intensely bitter as the broth.

### Taco de Mar (Penghu Blackspot Snapper / Nantou Nopale / Taitung Pepper Leaf) 🕶️
image: ./images/zea/06.jpg | Crisp fried fish on a corn tortilla with green sauce, on a scalloped ceramic plate, ZEA, Taipei
Excellent. The tortilla is soft and chewy and smells of corn, beautifully made. Its texture contrasts with the fish, which has two layers of crunch from the batter and the puffed, crisp fried scales, while the flesh in the middle is not overcooked: wonderfully moist and juicy. Eaten with a gently spicy jalapeño mole, the peppery astringency of Taiwanese pepper leaf, and nopal, the cactus paddle Mexicans eat as a vegetable, this fish taco still stays with me to this day.

### Tierra Adentro (Nantou Lion Mane Mushroom / Cassava / Pipian Verde)
image: ./images/zea/07.jpg | Orange-red nasturtium flowers and green leaves with a crisp white chip, on a white ceramic plate, ZEA, Taipei
The name means "deep inland". The sauce is pipián verde, Mexico's green mole, which ZEA makes from sesame, pumpkin seeds and chilli. The lion's mane mushroom is confit in chicken stock and then grilled: juicy, crisp-skinned and full of umami. It is eaten with cassava crisps and garnished with nasturtium leaves and flowers. The sauce tastes like a Thai curry, only gentler. The mushroom has a wonderful texture, chewy and tender, against the crunch of the fried cassava.

### Granja Finka (Changhua Mangalica / Nantou Sunchoke / Ecuador Coffee)
image: ./images/zea/08.jpg | Two slices of pink roast pork with a dark jus and cream-coloured purée, on a scalloped ceramic plate, ZEA, Taipei
Mangalica, the Hungarian breed with a curly coat like a sheep's. Loin and shoulder are marinated in herbs, coffee and koji before grilling, then served with Jerusalem artichoke purée and a jus with Ecuadorian coffee. Complex and layered, the meat juicy with a good bite. The sauce has great depth, bitterness first, followed by a not-too-intense umami, against the sweetness of the purée.

### Queso y Cebolla (Pingtung Onion / Filone di Mozzarella)
image: ./images/zea/09.jpg | A golden crisp fried pastry on a cylindrical wooden stand, ZEA, Taipei
A curry-puff-style pastry filled with onion and Taiwanese cow's-milk cheese. Take a little care eating this one: the filling inside is hot and molten. Simple flavours, well made, though personally I found it perhaps a little too simple next to the other dishes.

### Chuyaco (Taiwan Citrus / Taitung Prickly Ash / Guajillo)
image: ./images/zea/10.jpg | A bright orange citrus salad in several textures topped with thin translucent shards, in a white ceramic cup, ZEA, Taipei
Chuyaco is a Colombian citrus salad; here it is a Taiwanese citrus salad, with an orange-juice gel, orange granita and calamansi powder, topped with guajillo, Mexico's dried red chilli. Fruity and sweet with a gentle heat that lifts the sweetness of the citrus in an interesting way.

### Mazamorra y Tostones (Kaohsiung Plantain / Panela)
image: ./images/zea/11.jpg | Curled golden banana chips on white ice cream and fried plantain, in a cream ceramic bowl, ZEA, Taipei
Tostones are Caribbean green plantains fried twice, and panela is unrefined cane sugar. This dish uses plantains at several stages of ripeness: green ones are fried, just-ripe ones become ice cream and banana chips, and overripe ones are mixed with herbs and sugar into a sauce. Every bite has you exclaiming "banana!" (please don't mispronounce it), yet it brings many textures.

### Dulces (Alfajor / Tamal / Horchata / Pastelito)
image: ./images/zea/12.jpg | Closing sweets on a marble table: a leaf-wrapped tamal with brown sauce on a round wooden dish, a sandwich cookie and a small pastry on a wooden board, beside a glass of drink and a dark ceramic cup, ZEA, Taipei
The closing sweets: an alfajor (a cookie sandwiched with dulce de leche, Argentina's national sweet), a tamal (corn dough steamed in a leaf wrapper), horchata (a rice drink with cinnamon) and a pastelito (a small pastry).
