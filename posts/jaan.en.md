Boring British food is probably something etched in everyone's memory, a byword for the monotony of this country's cooking. That is probably why I wanted to try it, and chose to visit Jaan by Kirk Westaway, one of the very few British restaurants in Asia to have received Michelin stars. Besides its Michelin Stars, the restaurant won Tatler Best of Asia in its first edition and regularly appears on the 50 Best list. Jaan has just been renovated. The moment we step out of the lift on the 70th floor of the Swissôtel The Stamford, we are greeted by an impressionist painting, before entering a dining room with a hand-blown crystal chandelier tree, decorated piece by piece, and a beautiful view of Singapore waiting for us. Jaan is a restaurant that re-innovates British cuisine, and after tasting it I have to say that British food is not boring. With a lunch price of 238 SGD, it is one of the cheapest two-Michelin-star restaurants on the island, and together with the beautiful panoramic view and the contemporary, easy-on-the-eye atmosphere, that is reason enough to visit this dining room. Chef Kirk Westaway, a Cornishman, presents modern British food that focuses on light textures and complex dimensions, choosing fresh local British produce and blending it with the chef's childhood memories of the English countryside and his training in France, all of which are moulded and clearly expressed on the plate. My favourite dish today was the Guinea fowl pie with truffle. The pie itself is made of potato, with morel and truffle in the middle, topped with a jus from the guinea fowl. This dish was done brilliantly. The guinea fowl was not dry and was very tender, and it kept its bounce, done superbly. Its flavour goes very well with the morel mushroom, which pairs well with poultry; the sauce is well rounded and not too salty, and the truffle is appetisingly fragrant. It is a dish that looks simple but turned out better than expected. I recommend eating it alternately with the green salad and the chanterelle mushrooms that came with it, which break the richness of the pie and add dimension to the dish. Overall, the experience at this restaurant was a wonderful and good-value meal, especially at lunch, and fitting for the restaurant's standing. There were a few dishes where I felt the flavour was a little overwhelming, such as the cheese pancake, which felt too heavy once it had both cheese and caviar, and the use of rosemary smoke in so many dishes, which may have taken a little of the fun out of the meal.

## The art of taste in this meal

### WHITE ASPARAGUS
image: ./images/jaan/01.jpg | White asparagus meringue decorated with yellow flowers on a white stand, Jaan, Singapore
This bite is very good. It is a meringue made from white asparagus, with crème fraîche. The cream is creamy with a gentle sourness like sour cream and onion, which sets off the sweetness of the meringue, light and airy like a sponge, perfectly. The texture is very good, not sticky, with a mild flavour of asparagus. Delicious.

### FISH & CHIPS
This bite is an interpretation of the famous English dish that everyone knows. The chef chose to serve pomme dauphine with fish mousse and pickles. Although it doesn't look like it, it tastes of sea fish and crisp, light potato, finishing with pickles that add an intense saltiness.

### GOOSE MOUSSE
This bite is a goose liver mousse tart. The flavour is not rich or heavy, with the distinctive aroma of the liver and a faint metallic note, followed by rosemary smoke. It is lightly seasoned and eaten with a vinaigrette jelly that gives a gentle sweetness.

### CHEDDAR PANCAKE
A cheddar cheese pancake. The texture of the pancake is very good, warm and fluffy, with a faint aroma of asparagus slipped in to break the richness, but I think it is not enough, because when the intensely salty cheese in the pancake meets Oscietra caviar applied so generously, it becomes a little too much.

### SCALLOP BBQ
image: ./images/jaan/05.jpg | Grilled scallop skewered with barbecue sauce on a white stand, Jaan, Singapore
This one is a gift from the chef. The Scallop BBQ is normally in the larger course. It is a grilled scallop with barbecue sauce. Delicious. At first I thought the barbecue sauce would overpower the scallop, but it sets off the scallop well, with the spicy heat of chilli that is not smoky, and a slightly sweet-sour note. What must be praised in this bite is the texture of the scallop: very chewy but not tough, and supremely sweet, with a surface that is not hard but fragrant from the grill. A good dish.

### CHARRED LEEK & POTATO SOUP
image: ./images/jaan/06.jpg | A thick soup sprinkled with chopped spring onion in a square white bowl, Jaan, Singapore
This simple bowl of soup is one of the dishes that I feel expresses the identity of British food while adding interesting touches. It is made from a broth of charred leek, topped with a potato foam before macadamia nuts and chives are added. This dish was served warm. The soup has umami and an appetising aroma, with a mild flavour, and the nuts are well roasted and crunchy. The chef added chopped king oyster mushrooms at the bottom for chewiness and breadcrumbs on top for crunch. The pungent chives, added in just the right amount, lend the dish dimension and round it off nicely.

### BREAD
image: ./images/jaan/07.jpg | A small loaf with butter and a runny egg on a stone plate, Jaan, Singapore
Today's bread plate was butter bread and dark rye bread, served with English butter infused with lemon thyme leaf. The bread here is very well made. The rye is fragrant, not very chewy, with some sourness, and the crumb is fluffy with a slightly rustic crust. The butter has the distinctive cool aroma of lemon thyme leaf, gently, which creates a very relaxing feeling.

### KING CRAB
image: ./images/jaan/08.jpg | Alaskan crab in an orange sauce on a white plate with a ridged rim, Jaan, Singapore
This dish is Alaskan king crab with sliced almonds, served with a tomato sauce and yoghurt ice cream. This may be my least favourite dish of the meal. The sauce has a mild yoghurt character, sweet at the front, with sourness from the tomato. The masala added helps lend dimension and heat. The lemon zest, chives and almonds add dimension, but personally I found the almonds rather astringent, overpowering the flavours of the other elements and making everything feel as though it was combined unevenly.

### HEN'S EGG 🕶️
image: ./images/jaan/09.jpg | A dotted bowl holding a beautifully decorated egg yolk, with an explanatory card, Jaan, Singapore
The chef's famous dish, inspired by childhood memories of the English countryside, using English hen's eggs cooked two ways: the yolk is served onsen-style, and the white is made into a curd smoked with rosemary smoke. It is topped with an onion vinaigrette, nettle purée and caviar, and served with toast and mushroom cream. The chef recommends dipping the bread into the egg and eating it. This dish comes together in a curiously good way. The egg is lightly seasoned with salt, which goes well when dipped with the bread. The onion vinaigrette adds contrast with its sweet, sour and pungent notes, so we don't feel it is too heavy. The faint green aroma of the nettle leaf brings to mind the English countryside. Personally I felt the rosemary smoke was unnecessary and overpowered the nettle and the creaminess of the egg a little, but overall this dish was fun and delicious.

### OCEAN TROUT
image: ./images/jaan/10.jpg | Grilled trout with a cream sauce and vegetables in a deep plate, Jaan, Singapore
For this dish the chef served trout with pickled greens and a caper cream sauce, topped with mussels. At first I thought it would be heavy, but the flavour turned out airier and lighter than I expected, a very good dish to break up the meal. The trout, gently poached, is very tender, and goes with the light, sour greens with a faint lemon scent and the caper sauce, which is slightly salty and a little creamy. I recommend eating everything together; it goes very well.

### GUINEA FOWL
image: ./images/jaan/11.jpg | A potato pie with morel mushrooms as sauce is poured from a small jug, Jaan, Singapore
Guinea fowl pie with truffle. The pie itself is made of potato, with morel and truffle in the middle, topped with a jus from the guinea fowl. This dish was done brilliantly. The guinea fowl was not dry and was very tender, and it kept its bounce, done superbly. Its flavour goes very well with the morel mushroom, which pairs well with poultry; the sauce is well rounded and not too salty, and the truffle is appetisingly fragrant. It is a dish that looks simple but turned out better than expected. I recommend eating it alternately with the green salad and the chanterelle mushrooms that came with it, which break the richness of the pie and add dimension to the dish.

### EARL GREY
image: ./images/jaan/12.jpg | An Earl Grey tea dessert with ice crystals in a glass cup, Jaan, Singapore
When we think of England, we probably think of tea first. The chef served a sorbet of tea and lemon, served with various kinds of citrus such as pomelo, grapefruit and clementine, with milk infused with native Singapore flowers below, made into a granita. This dish is superb. The aromas are complex yet blend together very harmoniously. The tea is not too strong, so it doesn't overpower the other flavours in the dish. The citrus gives a slight bitterness and acidity, which cleanses the palate wonderfully and makes us want to keep eating.

### STRAWBERRY
image: ./images/jaan/13.jpg | Layered strawberry meringue with ice cream on a white plate, Jaan, Singapore
For this dish the chef served a meringue cake built up in layers, with strawberry sorbet, strawberry jam and fresh cream. It is eaten with yoghurt ice cream, vanilla cream and fresh strawberries. This dish was done well, a dish that looks simple but is prepared to perfection. The meringue is light and crisp, with a very good texture, a rich fragrance of vanilla and strawberries that are sweet with a slight tartness. What more could we want?
