Suhring is one of the fine dining restaurants I would like everyone to try at least once. Its style of cooking is distinctive even on a world level. Simple-looking German food, blended and reworked into haute cuisine the Suhring way, is really interesting, and it has earned the restaurant two MICHELIN stars. We usually see a lot of fine dining in the molecular style, but German food, which many people think of as just sausage (wurst) or pork knuckle (Eisbein): how far can it go as fine dining? This review has the answer.

Fine dining prices in Thailand have come down quite a bit lately, which is a good opportunity for anyone who wants to try. Suhring now costs only 3,000 baht for the short course, which drops two dishes and the petits fours (now 3,500), and 5,000 for the full course. You can also order additional dishes one by one. If you have never tried Suhring, I think this is a restaurant worth visiting at least once.

The kitchen is run by the twins Thomas and Mathias, both from Berlin, who worked at several world-famous restaurants, including Aqua (3★) in Wolfsburg, La Pergola (3★) in Rome, De Librije (3★) and Mezzaluna (2★), before finally opening Suhring.

Suhring is on Yen Akat, in a beautiful two-storey house with a shady garden that is lovely every time I visit. Because it feels like a home, tables are set in different rooms, and if you like a particular area you can request it. My favourite spot, especially at lunch, is the Winter Garden glasshouse, green and easy on the eyes.

One problem at Suhring is parking: you have to use valet parking, and if many people arrive or leave at the same time, the wait can be quite long.

Coming back to Suhring this time, I found that the German twins' food has moved up another level. Each bite is more rounded, more balanced and has more dimension. Dishes that were already interesting have had their tricks refined and been made more delicate, especially the amuse-bouches, just as I had heard many people say, and as befits two MICHELIN stars.

I still feel the service is not yet at the level it should be for a two-star restaurant. I can see they have tried to improve, but individual staff mistakes still happen, which shouldn't at this level. Personally, I feel the standard varies from person to person. For example, the receptionist remembered guests' names and made small talk that showed warmth and care, the kind of small attention you can't put into a manual. Among the servers, the one who introduced the menu gave excellent advice and explained everything in detail. In contrast, the server who brought the food explained less than the one who introduced the menu, and seemed rushed. Sometimes he even tapped his heels in rhythm as if tap dancing, which made the meal much less pleasant. A dish was also served out of order, and if I hadn't spoken up, I would have missed it.

Today I ordered the short course and added a single dish. Let's see what was served.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Amuse Bouche
image: ./images/suhring/01.jpg | A bright green first bite on a black stone with yellow flowers, Suhring, Bangkok
The first bite is smoked pike with sorrel jelly, lemon gel, crisp wholegrain bread, herbs and pike roe. Very well done, a big step up from my memory. Excellent flavour and superbly balanced. The slight sweetness and gentle sourness of the gel don't cover the saltiness and smoky aroma of the pike, and the freshness of sorrel is a wonderful finish. Almost flawless. The one thing I didn't like was the bread, a little too hard, which made it clash too much with what was on top.

### Seaweed tartlet
image: ./images/suhring/02.jpg | A seaweed tartlet decorated with flowers on a white ribbed plate, Suhring, Bangkok
Next, a seaweed tartlet with mousse, decorated with shrimp and flowers. Still excellent. In the mouth you get the gentleness of seaweed, a little saltiness and the air of the sea hitting you full on the moment the crisp tartlet around the smooth mousse disappears, cut by a sweet-sour lemon scent at the tip of the nose, before it all vanishes in a flash. Very delicious.

### Labskaus
image: ./images/suhring/03.jpg | Bread topped with pink-purple corned beef on a white plate, Suhring, Bangkok
Another classic on the menu: bread with corned beef and mustard. The bread is again hard and crisp, which contrasts well with the meat. The beef here is fragrant and juicy, lightly seasoned and gently salty, so the flavour of the meat comes through, cut by the sweet-sour mustard. Nicely rounded.

### Toast Hawaii
image: ./images/suhring/04.jpg | Toast topped with a red cherry on a white plate, Suhring, Bangkok
The chefs took inspiration from a German TV show for this bite. It is easy to make: bread, ham, pineapple, cheese and a maraschino cherry. The bread here is softer than in the earlier bites, and the flavour is stronger, which suits it as the last bite. It feels like homemade toast: the sweetness of pineapple and a hint of cherry against salty ham and cheese. A bit like a Hawaiian pizza. It is tasty, but compared with the other bites it is much less interesting.

### Duck foie gras waffle (Enleta)
My favourite bite of the meal, and I don't regret ordering it as an extra. A duck foie gras waffle (Enleta) eaten with vinegar made from Pinot Blanc grapes imported from Germany, and it is beautifully balanced. The saltiness and the fragrant batter of the waffle pair perfectly with the dense, slightly salty foie gras terrine filling. The real star is the sweet-sour vinegar, which lifts the flavour and goes very well with the waffle, cutting the richness and leaving a sweet, umami aftertaste.

### Currywurst
image: ./images/suhring/06.jpg | A paper box holding currywurst on the table, Suhring, Bangkok
The chefs use pork sausage imported from Germany. It is nicely crisp, with a little snap to the skin and not chewy, lightly salted with little spice, focused on the taste of the meat. You dip it in house-made tomato sauce and curry powder, and eat it with potato crisps and paprika powder. Well made but not different, and the portion is rather small, which makes you think about the value for the price.

### Raw scallop
image: ./images/suhring/07.jpg | Raw scallop with orange sauce and flowers on a white plate, Suhring, Bangkok
Next, raw scallop lightly aged by the chefs to concentrate the flavour, eaten with orange and ginger sauce, topped with lemon gel, butternut purée and pumpkin seeds. The sauce is both a strength and a weakness. On one hand it is amazingly delicious: sweet-sour, fragrant with orange, with a lovely ginger heat at the tip of the tongue. At the same time there is too much of it for the scallop, so it covers some of the sweetness and character of the shellfish. The butternut and pumpkin seeds are a very good pairing, adding richness and texture to the scallop and the sauce. A well-made dish.

### Bread
image: ./images/suhring/08.jpg | Several kinds of bread with flavoured butters on a blue and white plate, Suhring, Bangkok
The bread includes a sourdough made with their own starter, pretzel, wholegrain bread and a white rosemary bread. The butters are unsalted with salt on the side, garlic butter, and Obatzda, a Bavarian-style cheese dip, all made with German cream. The plain butter is just fine. The one to try is the bread made from their own dough: a thin, crunchy crust and a texture a little like cake. Excellent, especially with the strong Obatzda or the wonderfully smoky garlic butter.

### Lobster
image: ./images/suhring/09.jpg | Lobster with beetroot gel and a dark sauce on a white plate, Suhring, Bangkok
The lobster is gently poached and served with pickled vegetables, berry gel, beetroot, roasted beetroot and claw, with a lobster sauce and vanilla oil. Well done and quite interesting, especially the lobster sauce, which tastes like a bisque and goes amazingly well with the vanilla oil. Personally, I think the sauce is a little too strong and covers some of the sweetness of the lobster. The lobster is perfectly cooked, as always here. The pairing with sweet-sour, earthy pickled vegetables still lacks a little something for me. Overall it is okay, not bad, but not that exciting.

### Before the main course
image: ./images/suhring/10.jpg | A chestnut pastry decorated with green leaves on a black plate, Suhring, Bangkok
Before the main course we get this pretty little pastry. The dough is made from chestnut and eaten with chestnut cream. It finishes rich and slightly sweet.

### Main course
image: ./images/suhring/11.jpg | Turbot with caviar and wine sauce on a white plate, Suhring, Bangkok
Since I have eaten Suhring's duck several times, I chose the turbot today, served with Riesling sauce, potatoes and herbs. On the other side is lobster topped with Kristal caviar, eaten with fennel and herbs. The Riesling sauce is very good: a light scent of white wine, followed by creaminess led by salt, and a little sweet-sourness that is really distinctive, with a faint, appetising aroma. Very well done. But I think the fish was a little undercooked and slightly fishy. The potatoes are cooked just right, not mushy, with a fresh green herb scent and a gentle saltiness. In the other bite, the ratio of caviar to meat isn't balanced, so the saltiness of the caviar covers the sweetness of the lobster. The fennel side is delicious: the crunch, the richness and the distinctive scent of fennel go wonderfully with the lobster.

### Winter dessert
image: ./images/suhring/12.jpg | A berry dessert with vanilla cream on a white plate, Suhring, Bangkok
A winter dessert of raspberries in red wine, eaten with vanilla custard, vanilla cookie, vanilla foam and frozen berries. Pretty, and another interpretation of a German-style winter dessert, but for me the flavour doesn't wow yet.
