My meal at Maaemo is one of the most memorable I have had in all my years of eating fine dining and writing reviews. The experience at Maaemo 1.0 has always stayed with me.

Chef Esben Holmboe Bang decided to give up the three stars he had worked so hard to win and close Maaemo 1.0, one of the pillars of New Nordic cuisine, moving the restaurant a short distance away to become Maaemo 2.0. It won back three Michelin stars just the same, but it has also become far more expensive.

This visit brought mixed feelings, tinged with a sense of loss. I preferred the old restaurant. The new one has a raw, much darker décor, while the old place felt easier on the eye and, to me personally, cosier.

The atmosphere inside, with white chef's jackets set against black décor, concrete, wood-fire smoke and an open kitchen at the centre of the story, left me feeling tense and rather self-conscious, even though the waiting staff remained very friendly and professional. A chef serves and explains every dish. There was one lapse: they forgot my cutlery once, which was inexcusable.

I felt Maaemo was seriously short-staffed. Everyone seemed rushed and run off their feet, with no one free to notice what guests needed, which made me feel apologetic. The first half of the course was served efficiently, but by the later dishes I had to wait 30-40 minutes per dish, even though the service team tried to offer drinks to ease the situation. So after more than four hours the meal ended with me feeling tired and not as impressed as I should have been, because the food was very good.

The food here remains, without doubt, among the best restaurant cooking in the world, in composition, technique, flavour and its stylish, contemporary presentation of Nordic cuisine. But I felt it has abandoned the message the food once set out to convey, the ingredients and terroir it used to have, and now puts more weight on technique and ever more complex composition.

Few restaurants in the world make me feel that food can be complex and novel yet so well resolved as it is at Maaemo. There are many superb dishes, even by very high standards and expectations.

At 5,200 NOK, with a course whose quantity doesn't quite suit the price, even allowing for Scandinavia's sky-high cost of living, the meal left me fairly full, perhaps helped by the bread.

Don't get me wrong: Maaemo still shows superb cooking, but the overall experience I had at Maaemo 1.0 made me expect more from Norway's finest restaurant.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Raw scallops elderflower and almond
image: ./images/maaemo/01.jpg | Raw scallop tartlet topped with white foam, on a tall black ceramic stand, Maaemo, Oslo
The first bite is a tartlet of raw Norwegian scallop served with a white asparagus and almond foam, along with white asparagus, Norwegian cheese and elderflower. The quality of the chopped Norwegian scallop is superb, and the chef balances the flavours brilliantly into a very fine combination. The white asparagus has a gentle, fresh green aroma yet is creamy, matching the sweetness of the scallop and the saltiness of the cheese. Every element is assertive without fighting for attention, and they sit together beautifully. A pity, though: I felt there was a little too much oil on the tart, which dulled the crispness of the pastry.

### Lompe (smoked cod and horseradish)
image: ./images/maaemo/02.jpg | Lompe topped with golden grated egg yolk, on a grey ceramic dish beside a napkin, Maaemo, Oslo
Next, the chef served lompe, a Norwegian-style potato pancake, with sour cream, smoked fish and cured egg yolk. This bite too is intricately composed and very well balanced: the saltiness of the fish, a hint of smoke, creamy sourness and the gentle sweetness of the pancake, cut by a little lime zest, dill and chives.

### Slowly cooked reindeer shanks (aged cheese, black pepper and preserved chanterelles)
image: ./images/maaemo/03.jpg | Doughnut filled with reindeer, topped with pickled mushrooms, stacked on a white ceramic cup, Maaemo, Oslo
A doughnut stuffed with braised reindeer, eaten with cheese, pepper and pickled chanterelles. This bite is simply crazy. The reindeer is not gamey and very tender, lightly salty, set against the gentle sweet-and-sour of the slightly earthy pickled mushrooms so that it never feels heavy, and it matches the mild cheese perfectly. It finishes with the heat of pepper and leaves a soft, sweet aftertaste lingering in the mouth for a long time. A very good bite.

### 'Our Caviar' sauce of dried beef and blackcurrant leaves
image: ./images/maaemo/04.jpg | Caviar with cauliflower purée in beef broth, in a thick-rimmed cream ceramic bowl, Maaemo, Oslo
This bite is again superb, unbelievably so. The chef serves the restaurant's special caviar, made together with a caviar house in Copenhagen under the Rossini brand, seasoned with chives and served with a roasted cauliflower purée and a broth of dried beef. A dish of full umami. The richness of the caviar and beef doesn't feel heavy; it is a richness in which the two tones don't clash in the mouth, with the chives and the light creaminess of the cauliflower linking the flavours into a rounded whole. Excellent.

### Oyster 'Tradition 2010'
image: ./images/maaemo/05.jpg | Oyster under a cream sauce dotted with green dill oil, in a carved ice vessel, Maaemo, Oslo
A classic that is now more than 14 years old. The restaurant uses flat oysters from the North Sea, which taste meatier. They are covered with a jelly of oyster juice and an emulsion sauce of mussels with dill oil. The jelly sets up a play on temperature: the oyster has been chilled ice cold while the sauce is slightly warm, which makes the mouth feel wonderfully playful. The oyster's flavour is clearly mineral and meaty. Its firm crispness against the chewy jelly, the ice-cold temperature and the creamy sauce fragrant with the sea remain timelessly classic. The gentle dill oil doesn't overpower the sea but cleanses the palate superbly at the end.

### King crab gently cooked in hazelnut milk mountain potato and cep
image: ./images/maaemo/06.jpg | King crab custard covered with white foam, in a thick-rimmed dark grey stone bowl, Maaemo, Oslo
This dish is a custard with Kalix Löjrom (Kalix vendace roe) and king crab cooked slowly in hazelnut milk and sour cream. It is served with a creamy sauce made from mountain potato, elderflower and hazelnut oil.

What I love about this dish is the texture. The custard is creamy-smooth like a steamed egg, set against the springy chew of the crab meat. It has a faint, creamy eggy aroma, set against the mild sharpness of chives. The flavours of crab and roe combine to create umami in the mouth, before being cut superbly by the earthy aroma of hazelnut, potato and elderflower. The interwoven textures are done wonderfully; it is another dish complex in both flavour and texture, closing with an aftertaste of lingering umami. A very good dish, and one that I still find unforgettable.

### Cod steamed with salted butter and juniper roasted chicken "superior" broth and jasmine
image: ./images/maaemo/07.jpg | Steamed white cod in cream sauce, on a curved cream ceramic plate, Maaemo, Oslo
Cod steamed with butter, then glazed with chicken fat, served with leek purée, a smoked eel, onion and ginger sabayon, and a chicken broth with jasmine oil.

What on earth! The combination in this dish is astonishing yet well resolved, and feels luxurious and elegant. The cooking of the fish at Maaemo remains superb as ever. The flesh is soft, firm and sweet, set against a gentle richness that the chicken fat knits into the sauce. The sabayon has a faint ginger aroma, sour-led and creamy; it goes well with the fish, but I wonder whether the flavour is too heavy. Personally, though, the umami of the chicken broth and the cool, gentle fragrance of jasmine are intriguing and make you want to explore them. One small gripe: the aroma was disturbed by the leek purée underneath, which has a faint smoky note and is fairly intense, perhaps masking some of the flavour of the fish and the broth. But don't get me wrong; it is another very good dish.

### Reindeer cooked over embers walnuts and preserved berries / Warm milk bread brushed with juniper, garlic and butter
image: ./images/maaemo/08.jpg | Grilled reindeer topped with berries and walnuts in brown sauce on a black ceramic plate, with milk bread behind, Maaemo, Oslo
Our main course was reindeer from a sustainable farm in Norway, which the chef cooked with walnuts, served with a sauce of Jerusalem artichoke and assorted berries, alongside a purée of fermented vegetables. It came with milk bread seasoned with juniper and garlic.

The meat is very good, and all the more astonishing for being farmed reindeer: extremely tender, with a mild aroma and no bloody gaminess at all, even cooked fairly rare. The meat carries a faint walnut aroma. The sauce leads with sweet-and-sour from the berries and the depth of the artichoke, which suits the reindeer perfectly. The purée adds richness to the plate appropriately without overpowering the sauce.

### Soothing reindeer 'Stew' (roasted chestnuts and sour cream)
image: ./images/maaemo/09.jpg | White sour cream foam dusted with dark powder, in a silver-grey ceramic dish, Maaemo, Oslo
The next dish is reindeer tongue smoked with juniper, with chestnut purée and a sauce of sour cream. The first thing to say is that it isn't gamey: there is a faint wild scent mixed with the juniper smoke. The texture is not tough but pleasantly chewy and soft, boosted by the sweetness of the chestnut and the sourness, before the astringency of the oxalis leaves helps tone down the flavours of the course and prepares us for the next dish.

### A warm broth of celeriac, gooseberries and lemon verbena
image: ./images/maaemo/10.jpg | Amber broth with a green oil rim, in a speckled ceramic bowl with a scalloped edge, Maaemo, Oslo
Our palate cleanser is served warm: the sweet-and-sour, the gentle green freshness of the celeriac and the aroma of lemon verbena create a feeling of comfort and cleanse the palate well.

### Toasted hay ice cream
image: ./images/maaemo/11.jpg | A single scoop of white ice cream on white cream, in a pale pink ceramic dish, Maaemo, Oslo
This ice cream is very good. The richness of milk, a little saltiness, the smoky fragrance of hay and a gentle sweetness: richness, saltiness, softness and sweetness come together beautifully in a dessert that is utterly simple yet executed superbly.

### lavender, egg yolks and raspberry leaves
image: ./images/maaemo/12.jpg | Glossy orange-red dessert at the bottom of a brown-grey ceramic bowl, Maaemo, Oslo
This dessert is rather puzzling. The astringency of raspberry leaves, a smoky lavender aroma, eaten with a cream of egg yolk. Personally I preferred the first dessert.

### Hazelnut cone brown butter ice cream
image: ./images/maaemo/13.jpg | A small ice cream cone on a black wooden stand beside a wine glass, Maaemo, Oslo

### Petit four (Ollebrod plum kernel and toasted rye / Butter from Roros hazelnuts and coffee molasses / Eggnog whisky and bell heather honey / Freshly baked buns generously glazed with birch syrup)
image: ./images/maaemo/14.jpg | Two glossy brown glazed braided buns on a cream ceramic plate, Maaemo, Oslo
The first petit four is caramel in the centre, chewy, with cold ice cream; rich, a touch nutty, simple.

Eggnog with whisky and a chocolate truffle inside. Unusual, with a base that isn't heavy, and a light smoky whisky.

A cinnamon bun glazed with birch and brown butter. Good.
