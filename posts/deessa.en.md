Deessa is the fine dining restaurant of Chef Quique Dacosta, set in the Mandarin Oriental Ritz hotel in the heart of Madrid. It currently holds two Michelin stars in the Michelin Guide España 2026, having won the second in the Michelin Guide 2023, and it is the chef's first restaurant in the Spanish capital.

As for the name, Deessa means "goddess" in Valencian. The restaurant sits in the hotel's Alfonso XIII hall, with high ceilings, white linen tablecloths and a terrace overlooking the Ritz garden. The atmosphere is one of traditional Madrid luxury that suits the hotel very well, and it is one of the dining rooms that I found truly special.

Chef Quique Dacosta is a self-taught chef who began working in kitchens at 14 and joined a restaurant in Dénia, on the Mediterranean coast, between 1986 and 1988, before taking his restaurant Quique Dacosta up to three Michelin stars, which it kept in the Michelin Guide 2026, the same year he also received Michelin's Mentor of the Year award. Chef Quique is known for cooking built on the local produce of Valencia: rice, oranges, olive oil and the red prawns of Dénia. The person running the kitchen at Deessa is Domenico Vildacci.

There are two main menus here: the Historical Menu, which gathers classic dishes from the restaurant in Dénia, and the Contemporary Menu, created anew specially for Deessa, telling the story of sea and land through seasonal ingredients. Today I chose the Contemporary Menu.

Personally, I think the first highlight here is the staff. Everyone is attentive, colourful and humorous, happy to chat about whatever interests the guest, yet they keep their manners and a professional distance. The service is precise at every moment, from opening the door and showing you to the restroom to setting down plates and glasses. Truly impeccable.

As for the food, although it looks simple, the flavours are surprising and fun. Combinations that seem ordinary turn out to hide tricks, with simple dishes that feel luxurious to eat and complex combinations that feel entirely new. The plates and cutlery are all expensive, and the silverware gives a real sense of luxury. I think this is a restaurant worth travelling for, once, when you come to Madrid.

There were two dishes this meal for which the Boss had to take his glasses off to taste: the chilled beetroot soup with kefir ice cream and salmon, and the rice with dried octopus. After the latter, you understand at once why Chef Quique is known as a master of Spanish rice.

Let's see what was on the menu.

AT THE KITCHEN TABLE

## The art of taste in this meal

### Consommé
image: ./images/deessa/01.jpg | Amber consommé in a stemmed glass on a white table, beside two dark red migas on a white plate, with the chef's hand and moss behind, Deessa, Madrid
A mushroom consommé with Iberian ham, dusted with bell pepper powder. Iberian ham is the ham from Spain's black Iberian pig. Umami, with the sweetness of pepper but no heat, and a faint warm aroma of Spanish cooking.

### The Migas of "Mama Mari"
image: ./images/deessa/02.jpg | Two crisp dark red migas topped with a small round disc on a white plate, Deessa, Madrid
Migas are a Spanish country dish in which leftover bread is sautéed or fried with other ingredients. This version comes with the Spanish flavour of tomato and ham. The bread looks hard outside, but when you eat it, it is neither tough nor crunchy. Soft and good.

APPROXIMATION

### Gilda with Cockles
image: ./images/deessa/03.jpg | Gilda with cockles in a green broth in a glass bowl, with a small glass holding an orange sphere, Deessa, Madrid
A gilda is the classic pintxo of the Basque Country, with anchovy, olive and pickled chilli on one skewer, named after Rita Hayworth's character in the 1946 film. This dish comes in two parts. The first is a puffed fried potato chip dusted with pepper, with a cream scented with yoghurt, anchovy and lemon: simple yet complex. The second is cockles served with pickled vegetables, pickled shellfish and coriander. The cockles carry a faint blood-like aroma, with sourness leading, and go well with the potato. The vegetables are crisp, with the green aroma of coriander and a gentle olive note from the pickled chilli. The slippery jelly contrasts with the chewiness of the shellfish. Complex yet simple, a familiar flavour like blanched cockles, but feeling different.

### Crème Brûlée with Roasted Onions and Toasted Pork Chops and Seasonal Mushrooms
image: ./images/deessa/04.jpg | Roast onion crème brûlée surrounded by small pieces of pork and mushrooms in a yellow sauce, Deessa, Madrid
Crème brûlée with roasted onion, grilled Iberian pork and seasonal mushrooms. The aroma of truffle wafts up. The Iberian pork is tender, with a faint egg aroma and a creamy feel, a rich mushroom flavour, and smooth caramelised onion with garlic of an appetising fragrance. The part of the pork with skin is pleasantly chewy, while the loin is supremely tender.

### Cold Beetroot and Dill Soup, Kefir Ice-Cream and Salmon 🕶️
image: ./images/deessa/05.jpg | Chilled beetroot soup with a green-patterned quenelle of kefir ice cream, diced salmon and red flowers, Deessa, Madrid
Chilled beetroot soup with pink salmon, shredded beetroot, kefir ice cream and olive oil. Kefir is a fermented milk with a mild sourness. Everything goes together in a puzzling way. The ice cream has a faint kefir aroma that tempers the earthy smell of the shredded beetroot just right. The cool, lightly smoked pink salmon adds the gentle saltiness missing from the ice cream and completes the dish, balancing it. A light aroma of dill adds an aromatic dimension to this chilled soup. Excellent, truly excellent.

LA SAL

### Ritz Caviar Selection and Cured Fish Roe Selection
image: ./images/deessa/06.jpg | A shell-shaped tray of caviar on mother-of-pearl spoons, with cured fish roe and a tin of orange roe, Deessa, Madrid
Ritz's selection of caviar and cured fish roe, to pair as you choose. There is Spanish bottarga, the dried fish roe similar to Japanese karasumi, eaten with Pedro Ximénez sherry; trout roe with Champagne, a pleasantly surprising pairing, better than I expected; Albino caviar from Poland with a seaweed aroma and a gentle, light umami; Oscietra caviar from Bulgaria, a simple classic; Kaluga caviar aged two months; and Manchego cheese with olive oil. For me it was a dish for combining, experimenting and having fun with.

### Coastal Oyster and Sea Cracker
image: ./images/deessa/07.jpg | Whole boiled red prawns from Dénia in a white bowl, with a green-and-orange drink glass and a white dome beside them, Deessa, Madrid
It comes in two parts. The first is a smoked oyster with kombu, served with kombu pesto and various seaweeds, and a seaweed cracker topped with uni. The second is boiled red prawn from Dénia, a famed ingredient of the Valencian coast, served with a prawn-shell broth with chives in a glass. The oyster leads with smoke, umami and a strong salinity, contrasting with the sweetness of the uni. The prawn leads with salt and umami, and its flesh is very tender. Pure deliciousness from the depths of the sea.

### Extra Virgin Olive Oil Bread
image: ./images/deessa/08.jpg | Extra virgin olive oil bread on an embossed gold plate, Deessa, Madrid
Bread from Alicante, the same province as Dénia, the chef's home town, served with olive oil. Crisp outside, soft within.

### Sole Fish with "Beurre Blanc" and Aged Sake
image: ./images/deessa/09.jpg | Sole in a white beurre blanc topped with strips of daikon and black strands on a white plate, Deessa, Madrid
Sole, grilled and fried and then pressed together, with a beurre blanc made with a full-bodied aged sake, or jukushu, and mirin, topped with rice vinegar. It is served with strips of daikon and shirataki noodles for added texture. Beurre blanc is the classic French white butter sauce; here the butter is smoked, so the sauce has a smoky aroma, while the sake gives the sauce an aroma similar to Japanese ume, and plays the role that yellow wine plays in the traditional French version. The fish is springy, firm and juicy. Another dish that reinterprets a classic very well.

### Rice with Dried Octopus 🕶️
image: ./images/deessa/10.jpg | Rice with dried octopus sprinkled with octopus flakes and crisp fried kale on an embossed white plate, Deessa, Madrid
Rice in a seafood and squid ink sauce, topped with dried octopus tentacle cooked with kombu and shoyu to deepen the taste of the sea, and crisp kale. Eating it, you understand at once who the god of Spanish rice is. The rice is firm in the centre and cooked on the outside, like al dente. The rice is very sweet and the sauce intense, while the bitter crisp kale cuts through, and the octopus tentacle adds a sea aroma. Fragrant and rounded, a simple dish done very well. Very, very good.

### Duck from the Albufera to Madrid
image: ./images/deessa/11.jpg | Albufera duck with a rich sauce, a red sphere and a fried ball beside it, with white powder at the plate's rim, Deessa, Madrid
Grilled mallard. The chef uses duck from the Albufera, the lake near the city of Valencia. The sauce leads with sweetness, the skin is chewy, and the duck meat is dense and springy yet melts so tenderly it came as a shock. It is a strange and very interesting texture. I have eaten a great deal of duck and have never met a restaurant that achieves a texture like this. It is served with a duck liver sphere that is made to the usual standard.

SWEET DEESSA

### The Pine
image: ./images/deessa/12.jpg | The Pine: a yellow dome on a white meringue base, with a pale ball, a pink sheet and edible flowers, Deessa, Madrid
A mango and lychee sphere with sweetcorn ice cream, meringue and an assortment of flower petals. The meringue is crisp, the bitterness of the flowers cuts the sweetness, the lychee is sour-sweet, and the sweetcorn ice cream is creamy.

### Chocolate Berliner with Iced Vanilla Powder
image: ./images/deessa/13.jpg | Chocolate berliner quenelle with a small chocolate cake and golden sugar threads, Deessa, Madrid
A berliner, the German filled doughnut, reinterpreted as a dry chocolate cake with gianduja chocolate ice cream, the Italian chocolate and hazelnut blend. It is served with candied ginger.

### Petits Fours
image: ./images/deessa/14.jpg | Three petits fours on a golden cage filled with bright green and yellow moss, Deessa, Madrid
Bread, Cheese and Hazelnuts close the meal.
