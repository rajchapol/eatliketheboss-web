"If Ishikawa offers you a seat, book it quickly," as a friend of mine put it. This was one of the restaurants I had set out to book in the first place, and the only one I got (I am short on luck). I received seat offers, ahead of the booking opening on the well-known website, from both Kohaku (three stars), which has a higher Tabelog score, and Ishikawa, but I wanted to meet the superb Ishikawa-san at least once, so I chose Ishikawa.

I would christen him the Alain Ducasse of Japan, and I had wanted to taste Hideo Ishikawa's cooking for a very long time. Besides being one of the most popular restaurants on Omakase.in, Hideo Ishikawa is one of the chefs with the most Michelin stars in the world, yet he is rarely talked about. He holds three Michelin stars for the main restaurant I am reviewing today, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, which also holds a Tabelog silver. That alone would be impressive, but did you know that Kohaku, his sister restaurant, also has three Michelin stars and a Tabelog silver? His group includes many other Michelin-starred restaurants too: NK with one star, Hatou with one star, Ren Mishina with one star, as well as a Tabelog bronze, Guchokuni with one star, and Jinen in Tabelog's top 1000, plus another restaurant in the top 5000.

He is one of the Japanese chefs with the most Michelin stars today (10 stars in the most recent year), and few Japanese chefs can claim more success. Chef Ishikawa's cooking philosophy is teaching, which is how he has achieved so much. His teaching earned him the Mentor Chef of the Year award from the Michelin Guide Tokyo in 2023.

Another reason I wanted to eat here: most chefs with this many Michelin stars do not do particularly well on Tabelog, but not him, who has kept the affection of Japanese and foreign diners alike.

The chef speaks little English, but even at his age he keeps learning and says he is studying English. I sensed a cheerful, smiling personality, yet one that is strict about the food.

The restaurant has no sign: a black wooden building. Inside, you find a small garden with a fish pond. The restaurant is not large but compact, with a tiny counter in a small room of just a few seats, which the chef serves entirely by himself.

The food here is kaiseki at a very high price, 57000 yen, but what you get is very good indeed: uni, caviar, ise ebi, top-grade beef and more. Every fish and every cut of meat is of very high quality, and the technique is flawless in every dish, with several dishes leaving you astonished at how they were made, such as the soy-grilled cabbage, which was wonderful, or the grilled squid with three textures in a single squid. The flavours are bold enough that even an ordinary diner could enjoy the meal without difficulty. Let's see what we had.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Ise Ebi with Mochi Rice
image: ./images/ishikawa/01.jpg | Grilled ise ebi lobster on mochi rice in a cream-yellow ceramic dish, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
The opening dish came with something powerful: ise ebi with mochi rice. The grilled lobster was very sweet, with a light scent of ginger and the fragrance of the shell. The rice used for the mochi resembles sticky glutinous rice, lightly seasoned with salt to contrast with the sweet lobster. The chef suggested mixing in the rich lobster fat as well, which brings everything together.

### Steamed Uni, and Shiro Ebi with Caviar
image: ./images/ishikawa/02.jpg | Shiro ebi topped with caviar beside steamed uni with jelly and shiso flowers on a green-and-red patterned porcelain plate, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
Steamed uni with a vinegar dashi jelly and hanahojiso; on the other side, shiro ebi with caviar and dashi jelly.

The steamed uni was very good, not cooked until firm. Its sweetness was intense, and the sweet-and-sour jelly was very concentrated yet not overwhelming.

The shiro ebi was top-grade too, very sweet, but the caviar, piled on too lavishly, though a fine product with a complex aroma and many dimensions of flavour, seemed to cover some of the flavour of the prawn. But who would complain when you are given caviar heaped up that high?

### Wakame Shinjo, Takenoko and Fish Dumpling
image: ./images/ishikawa/03.jpg | Clear dashi soup with shinjo and green kinome leaf in a black-and-red lacquer bowl, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
The soup course was wakame shinjo, takenoko (bamboo shoots from Kyoto) and a fish dumpling. The dashi here is crystal clear, but the flavour is deep, mellow and rounded, full of umami, in contrast with its appearance, with an aftertaste that lingers long in the mouth. The fish dumpling is sweet and smooth, slightly sticky but not springy or crunchy. The seaweed is silky going down, and the bamboo shoots are fragrant and crisp, with a distinctive character. A really delicious soup.

### Amadai and Squid Sashimi
image: ./images/ishikawa/04.jpg | Amadai and squid sashimi with vegetables and wasabi on a ceramic plate, beside a blue-and-white soy sauce dish, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
The sashimi course included lightly grilled amadai, the skin crisp but not tough, with a different fragrance from the fried version we are used to. The fish was top-grade, springy, crisp and sweet. The aromatic squid beside it, sweet and creamy, was also a fine product.

### Grilled Squid (Komochi Yari-ika)
image: ./images/ishikawa/05.jpg | Sliced grilled yari-ika squid on a coarse grey ceramic plate, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
Grilled squid. At first I thought it might be a little ordinary, but the moment I ate it, this was clearly the ideal grilled squid. The crisp, very sweet squid was made to shine by a lightly sweet sauce. The three pieces of komochi yari-ika were grilled to different degrees of doneness, so we got three textures of the squid's roe on the tongue, dry, chewy and somewhere in between. Superb.

### Grilled Cabbage with Dashi-Soy and Sake Sauce 🕶️
image: ./images/ishikawa/06.jpg | A large piece of pale green grilled cabbage in an oval ceramic dish, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
This is the dish that felt like nothing while I was eating it, yet I still can't forget it today. It is grilled cabbage with a dashi, soy and sake sauce. The chef grilled it until crisp, neither hard nor too soft, the surface not burnt but fragrant with smoke. The salty-sweet sauce with sake goes well with the sweetness of the vegetable. But the marvel is that although the vegetable is crisp, it has no green smell and is not too firm. It looks ordinary but is certainly not ordinary. I was impressed.

### Steamed Mebaru with Wild Vegetable Shoots
image: ./images/ishikawa/07.jpg | Steamed mebaru with green wild vegetable shoots and sauce on a butterfly-patterned ceramic plate, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
The next dish was steamed fish with wild vegetable shoots. The chef chose mebaru, served with a Japanese-style douchi sauce. The fish was tender and rich, the skin slightly chewy and springy. The sweet douchi sauce contrasts with the lightly salty soy-grilled vegetables. Done to the restaurant's standard. P.S. The wild vegetable shoots were delicious.

### Ankimo and Steamed Hairy Crab
image: ./images/ishikawa/08.jpg | Ankimo topped with orange daikon oroshi beside steamed hairy crab meat on a shell-shaped ceramic dish, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
Ankimo with ponzu sauce, spring onion and daikon oroshi; on the other side, steamed hairy crab. Hairy crab is delicious in its own right, and served steamed with almost no seasoning it is certainly delicious, to the usual standard. The ankimo was served with a smooth, soft, creamy texture and a gentle sweetness, before its richness was cut by a mildly sour ponzu sauce and a daikon oroshi spicier than usual.

### Sakura Masu Simmered with Japanese Onion
image: ./images/ishikawa/09.jpg | Sakura masu simmered in broth, topped with kinome leaf, in a black-and-red lacquer bowl, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
Sakura masu simmered with Japanese onion. The sakura masu was firm and sweet, not very fatty, and not overcooked, so it kept its texture. Its sweetness went with the top-grade onion, remarkably sweet, crisp and without any sharp, pungent smell. The broth too was intensely umami and very sweet. Impressive.

### Somen with Wagyu and Hana Sansho 🕶️
image: ./images/ishikawa/10.jpg | Pink sliced wagyu topped with green hana sansho flowers in a clear glass bowl, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
Somen with wagyu and hana sansho, with a tonyu sauce. The fresh somen here is probably among the best somen I have ever eaten: light, with the fragrance of flour, the noodles al dente and slightly chewy. Somen is quite hard to make delicious because the noodles are so thin. Eaten with the sauce made from soy milk, rich and creamy, it blends the noodles with the beef well. The beef was A5 wagyu, as you would expect, its richness contrasting with the distinctive, green, mildly sour pungency of hanahojiso. Another dish done very well, above all the noodles and the sauce.

### Baked Rice with Mixed Vegetables and Chicken
image: ./images/ishikawa/11.jpg | Rice baked with vegetables and chicken in a ceramic bowl, beside a black lacquer soup bowl and a plate of pickles, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
Baked rice with mixed vegetables and chicken. Good. Rice dishes of this kind are usually rather bland, but here the flavour came out very well. Every vegetable was cooked just right and crisp. The rice was not strongly flavoured, fragrant with all kinds of vegetables, so you could keep eating it. The varied textures of the many vegetables, the tiny pieces of chicken thigh and the rounded flavour of the rice made a delicious end to the savoury courses. My only slight complaint is that the rice was a little too soft for me, only a little, truly.

### Kinkan and Strawberries
image: ./images/ishikawa/12.jpg | Halved kinkan and red strawberries on a cut-glass plate, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
Kinkan (tiny citrus) and strawberries. This citrus can be eaten whole, skin and all. It was sweet and delicious enough that I thought it had been candied, the skin neither thick nor bitter. The strawberries were very sweet, top-grade as well.

### Kuromitsu Jelly
image: ./images/ishikawa/13.jpg | Dark brown jelly in a green-glazed ceramic bowl, Kagurazaka Ishikawa, Tokyo
The last dessert was kuromitsu jelly.
