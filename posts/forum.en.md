The second restaurant I had set my heart on in Hong Kong. I actually went first and am writing it up later: Forum. It is a Chinese restaurant holding three Michelin stars that is probably more affordable than the others, and it is easy to book. If you are looking for a good dim sum lunch, Forum is a decent choice.

The lunch and dinner menus differ. Lunch offers a dim sum tasting menu and seasonal specials, which on my visit meant hairy crab. Dinner has no dim sum; it is replaced by an à la carte menu. The dish not to miss (if you can afford it), and which I missed by going at lunch, is of course the abalone, the restaurant's signature. Forum's founder, Yeung Koon Yat, is known in the culinary world as the "King of Abalone", and the chef has served many famous guests, including Queen Elizabeth II and France's Jacques Chirac. Many will know the chef's name from Ah Yat Abalone, which has branches around the world. Forum is the flagship, and there is never a second restaurant called Forum, which says a great deal about how special this place is.

For me, Forum Hong Kong is a Chinese restaurant that is easy to book, with a relaxed, unstressful atmosphere, dim sum of a good standard, and prices that are reasonable for Hong Kong and for comparable dim sum rooms in hotels. The dim sum is made to the standard of a two- or three-Michelin-star restaurant. Every piece has well-made dough, and the dough has its own character: very soft without being mushy or sticky, with a slight chewiness that makes it ideal for bringing older relatives. The other food is good too, and the real highlights are the dipping sauces, several of which you will want to order extra, such as the XO sauce or the chilli sauce. The selection of alcohol and wine is wide as well, with many famous labels at not-too-cruel prices. Today I chose a half bottle of Puligny-Montrachet from Alain Chavy at 780 HKD. Another interesting point is the expensive sake list, with names such as Aramasa, Juyondai and IWA.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Ah Yat Pineapple Bun
image: ./images/forum/01.jpg | Three golden pineapple buns with crisp sugar tops on a white plate, Forum, Hong Kong
The pineapple bun looks like another simple dish, but it is different from elsewhere. The caramelised sugar crust on top carries a faintly sour pineapple aroma and is not very sweet, with a slight crunch. The dough is chewy and soft, never hard, and its texture is chewier than usual. To balance the crust, the filling is not strongly flavoured: char siu cut into pieces and stir-fried with pineapple, whose sweet-and-sour taste goes nicely with the dough.

### Black Bean and XO Sauces
image: ./images/forum/02.jpg | Black bean sauce and orange-red XO sauce in white porcelain dishes with spoons, Forum, Hong Kong
The restaurant's black bean sauce and XO sauce.

### Ah Yat Steamed Premium Shrimp Dumpling
image: ./images/forum/03.jpg | Four translucent-wrapped shrimp har gow in a bamboo steamer, Forum, Hong Kong
The original-style har gow. This is the benchmark dish of a Chinese restaurant, and when a restaurant dares to put its own name on a special signature recipe, you simply have to try it. Having eaten it, I must say I was very impressed by the har gow that day. The filling is whole shrimp, crisp and bouncy, with not much pork fat mixed in and a light umami broth. It is delicious on its own. The wrapper looks thick but is soft with a slight chew, not hard at all. Very delicious.

### Crispy Rice Roll Stuffed with Scallop
image: ./images/forum/04.jpg | Rice noodle rolls stuffed with a crisp pastry layer, arranged on a long white plate with green gai lan, Forum, Hong Kong
The rice noodle roll with shrimp. The wrapper here is thick but soft and slippery, with a faint fragrance of rice flour. The pastry inside is lightly crisp, giving two textures in one piece. The sauce is gently salty, with a hint of celery. I felt the wrapper was a little thick relative to the amount of filling, so the texture may not be especially fun, but with the crisp pastry it becomes enjoyable. A bit more filling would have made it better balanced. Done to the restaurant's standard.

### Steamed Shrimp and Vegetable Dumpling
image: ./images/forum/05.jpg | Two translucent shrimp and vegetable dumplings with green filling showing through, in a bamboo steamer, Forum, Hong Kong
The shrimp and vegetable dumpling. The translucent wrapper is lovely. Though it looks thick, it is soft with a springy chew, neither hard nor sticky. The chopped shrimp filling is crisp and bouncy, and contrasts perfectly with the light green flavour of the assorted vegetables.

### Hairy Crab Roe Fried Rice with Shrimp
image: ./images/forum/06.jpg | Fried rice topped with orange hairy crab roe sauce and spring onion, in a white porcelain dish with handles, Forum, Hong Kong
This dish is delicious, and the portion was so generous it startled me. It is not only the sauce that has crab roe: the rice underneath is also fried with crab roe. The fried rice is well made, with separate, loose grains, a light wok aroma and the fragrance of crab roe. The sauce leads with sweetness, with the aroma of Chinese wine, crab roe and a hint of the sea, so good you want to keep spooning it into your mouth. Delicious.
