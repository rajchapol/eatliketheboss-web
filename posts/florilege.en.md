Florilège is an innovative restaurant in Aoyama, the same chic neighbourhood as Miyasaka, where I ate the night before. It sits on the B1 floor, close to Seizan, another two-star restaurant with a Tabelog Gold. The weather was lovely that day, so I stopped for a latte before walking over.

Florilège is so well priced it is almost annoying. Never mind Aoyama, that kind of value is hard to find anywhere in Tokyo, and in this age of inflation it is even better value: lunch was only 7,500 yen. Add two MICHELIN stars, a Green Star, a place on Asia's 50 Best and a Tabelog Silver, and if you want a fine dining experience in Tokyo, Florilège is a very good choice.

Chef Hiroyasu Kawate's food is well made, with no real mistakes, but it doesn't reach the level that makes you say wow. Personally, I think the tricks and the innovation feel a little dated now. I believe anyone can enjoy Florilège's food without much effort. There are slightly unconventional dishes, like the carpaccio of retired wagyu cow, and fairly safe ones, like the pork or the dessert crêpe. There are plenty of interesting ingredients too, from old wagyu cows to pork raised on cashew nuts. The wait between dishes felt a little long, perhaps because guests arrived at different times. The flavours are French, quite bold but easy to understand. For me the course is a little too heavy: eating the very rich cheese custard and the wagyu with consommé one after the other is brutal. A dish to break the flavour in between would make it more balanced.

As for service, every server and chef is attentive and constantly watching, to the point that it felt tense, as if they were worried something was missing. They asked for comments on every dish, some two or three times, and at times I felt rather scrutinised. I also noticed that my dishes sometimes seemed to jump the queue. Let me say it here: I am not a World's 50 Best voter or a MICHELIN inspector, just someone with money who likes to eat.

Let's look at the food in the photos.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Edamame churros
image: ./images/florilege/01.jpg | Golden churro-shaped pastries on grey stones, topped with white cream and green apple, Florilège, Tokyo
Edamame churros with a yogurt espuma. Light and sour rather than creamy, with the sweetness of the beans. The pastry is fragrant and slightly crisp on the surface, warm and fluffy inside. A light dish that wakes up the senses well.

### Prawn and frozen cauliflower cream
My favourite dish of the day: prawn with frozen cauliflower cream and yuzu jelly, served with shallot and quinoa chutney and wasabi oil. What makes it great is the courage to serve it ice cold. The cauliflower comes as ice crystals that play against the crisp, bouncy texture of the minced prawn, the crumble at the base and the jelly in a really interesting way. The flavour is deep, with the sea of the bisque, the creaminess of the cauliflower and a little citrus sweetness from the yuzu, before the shallot and quinoa break the richness with bitterness and the freshness of vegetables. If that isn't enough, the wasabi oil does the job very well. A complex dish that comes together beautifully. Superb.

### Steamed bread
image: ./images/florilege/03.jpg | A round cream-coloured steamed bun in a wooden bowl, Florilège, Tokyo
The bread here is soft and fluffy, like a cross between bread and a mantou, and slightly sweet.

### Sustainability Beef
image: ./images/florilege/04.jpg | Thinly sliced beef carpaccio on a white cream sauce in a white bowl, Florilège, Tokyo
It is called sustainable because the chef uses meat from older wagyu cows, served as a carpaccio with smoked potato purée, parsley oil and beef consommé.

The idea is good, but I don't think the flavours are balanced. The beef is lightly cured, a little like prosciutto, which makes meat from an already mature cow (saying "old" out loud hurts, even quietly) saltier and chewier. With the strong consommé it becomes even saltier. The parsley oil and the sweetness of the rather loose potato purée try to break the flavour, but for me it is still too strong.

### Shiitake and cheese
image: ./images/florilege/05.jpg | Creamy shiitake soup topped with thinly sliced mushrooms and truffle in a black wooden bowl, Florilège, Tokyo
Served warm. The base is a mushroom egg custard, eaten with mushroom and cheese soup, fresh cheese, shiitake and truffle. Apart from the truffle, every ingredient comes from Japan. Mellow and creamy from the egg and cheese, with depth from the cheese. The flavour is rounded yet has several layers. The shiitake are crunchy with an earthy scent, but I think the cheese soup is a little too strong and covers the mushroom too much. Paired with the other very heavy dishes, the course feels out of balance.

### Grilled Kurobuta pork
image: ./images/florilege/06.jpg | Sliced roast pork with red wine sauce and small bites on a white plate, Florilège, Tokyo
Today's pork is Kurobuta, raised on cashew nuts.

Our main course is roast pork with a red wine and onion sauce, eaten with a meatball made from pig's head stuffed with parsley dough, and salsify wrapped in kombu with yogurt and ham.

I loved this dish. It is rare to find a restaurant that makes pork this impressive. This may be one of the best pieces of pork I have ever eaten: tender, sweet and with no porky smell at all. The sauce is very good too. It is called an onion sauce, yet there is no sharp onion smell, only deep sweetness and umami with a light touch of red wine. My guess is they used shin tamanegi, the new-season onion, to make the sauce (luxurious). One small point: the skin was a little chewy. If it were crisp, this would be even more delicious.

The meatball is very good. The dough is soft and not heavy, the pork has a nice bite, and there is sweetness from onion and lightly pickled mustard seeds, probably Dijon-style, to cut through.

### Passionfruit
image: ./images/florilege/07.jpg | A round purple dessert dusted with white ice, on a cloth and a wooden tray, Florilège, Tokyo
Our first dessert. The chef serves passion fruit as jelly and ice cream, with the seeds folded in for texture, topped with a mint and lemongrass foam. I liked it: the light sweet-sour of passion fruit and a range of textures, with the foam giving a cool, fragrant note that matches the passion fruit beautifully. Delicious.

### Omelette Chocolate
image: ./images/florilege/08.jpg | A chocolate omelette on a grey-green plate with cream and espresso sauce, Florilège, Tokyo
The last dish: a chocolate omelette with espresso sauce, served with cream and cacao nibs.

The chocolate is Peruvian. A really gentle dish. The omelette filled with chocolate cream goes well with the bitterness of the coffee. The Peruvian chocolate is fragrant without being very fruity-sour or very bitter, mild in taste but very aromatic. A simple dessert, well put together and delicious.
