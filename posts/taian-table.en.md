This review has been waiting in the wings for quite a while. I visited early this year, and only now has the right moment come to share a superb experience with you all. This meal brought together the "Best of 2025", the all-time favourite dishes of one of Shanghai's legendary restaurants.

Taian Table is not just a restaurant: it breaks down the walls of traditional fine dining with its "Private Dinner Party" concept, as if you had been invited to dine at the home of a friend who happens to be a world-class chef. The horseshoe-shaped 'Chef's Counter' wraps around the open kitchen, so just 20 guests can watch every move of the kitchen team up close, as if seated in a theatre.

The restaurant was born in Shanghai in April 2016 from the vision of Chef Stefan Stiller, a key pillar of Western fine dining in China, with experience at one- to three-Michelin-star restaurants across Germany. His philosophy is Refined, Modern, and Predominantly European: rooted in classical technique and traditional German flavours, cleverly woven together with seasonal produce and Asian touches. The flavours here may not chase the flashy, playful dimensions in fashion today, but they stand out for superb technique and the solid, weighty character of truly German fine dining, the chef's homeland. Flavours in the German, Austrian and Hungarian vein are hard to find in Asia. Prices are considered the highest in Shanghai, and it has long been the city's top-tier restaurant.

The care begins at the booking stage: the restaurant asks for your nationality so it can prepare the menu in your own language, alongside English and Chinese. Every dish is presented by the chef responsible for it, in a relaxed, friendly way, creating an interaction that makes the meal all the more lively.

Taian Table is a powerful fine dining experience, brimming with high-level technique. Holding three Michelin stars for so long is no accident; it comes from the precision and deep understanding of ingredients of Chef Stefan Stiller. Some dishes may be complex to the point of being hard to read, or carry echoes of something familiar from elsewhere, but this is a meal worth the journey for anyone who loves the art of European technique blended harmoniously with Asian accents.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Amuse Bouche
image: ./images/taian-table/01.jpg | Seaweed chips and Parmesan chips standing in a black holder beside a bowl of macadamia nuts, Taian Table, Shanghai
The meal opens with delicious nibbles: a Chip Seaweed and a Parmesan Chip, light and crisp, followed by moreish macadamia nuts.

### Mushroom Tea | Beurre Noisette
image: ./images/taian-table/02.jpg | Mushroom soup in a white porcelain cup with a cream-coloured butter foam on top, on a white saucer, Taian Table, Shanghai
A mushroom consommé drawing its flavour from the finest dried Yunnan mushrooms, shiitake, porcini and morel, topped with a brown butter foam. At first I expected the foam to be heavy and creamy, but it turned out light and airy, never masking the mushrooms. Porcini leads the aroma, and the soup is well rounded, without an overly strong earthiness. A very well-made soup.

### Beetroot Smoked Eel Sphere
image: ./images/taian-table/03.jpg | Three bites in a row: a deep purple beetroot sphere on a wavy white plate, salmon tartare topped with salmon roe on a white macaron, and an orange tart on a sea-urchin-patterned ceramic base, Taian Table, Shanghai
The outer shell is made of beetroot, crisp and delicate, hiding beetroot juice and a small piece of smoked eel inside. The cherrywood smoke of the eel comes through clearly, the earthy bitterness of the charred beetroot outside plays against the sweetness within, and lemon zest adds freshness. A fine bite, superbly balanced.

### Lobster roasted bell pepper caviar
It feels rather like barbecued lobster, served in fine strands, tender and sweet, never tough. It comes with a bell pepper tart, black garlic purée and myoga (Japanese ginger flower, with its distinctive cool fragrance). Sourness leads, with the aroma of sweet pepper and a gentle heat, set off perfectly by the saltiness of the caviar. The lightest of the first three bites, it works nicely as a palate cleanser.

### Salmon tartare wasabi leaf caviar
New Zealand salmon tartare, served with a wasabi macaron and salmon roe (ikura). Personally, I found the fish a little fishy, and the wasabi macaron so sweet that its flavour didn't quite come through against the saltiness of the roe. Compared with the other bites in this set, it was the least delicious.

### Wasabi | Juniper Berry New Zealand King Salmon
image: ./images/taian-table/06.jpg | Green wasabi sherbet topped with a leaf, resting on orange salmon in a clear sauce on a white plate, Taian Table, Shanghai
A complex yet balanced opener. Cold-smoked salmon, mellow and soft, served with a vinaigrette made with juniper berry (a berry with a pine-like scent, often used to make gin). The sauce is light, never sharply acidic. An apple compote adds a lovely sweetness without distracting from the main flavours, cut by a smooth wasabi sherbet with a gentle green pungency. A beautifully rounded pairing.

### Chawanmushi | Snail | Bone Marrow | Parsley
image: ./images/taian-table/07.jpg | A round crisp cheese tuile covering a steamed egg custard, with green parsley sauce and herbs in the centre, in a black ceramic bowl, Taian Table, Shanghai
This dish surprises from the very first look. On tasting, there is none of the eggy aroma of a typical custard; instead, parsley oil and celery lead. The snail is wonderfully tender, a crisp Comté cheese tuile adds texture, and it finishes with the smoky note of small crisp-fried pieces of beef bone marrow. It feels fresh and green, yet hides a certain warmth. Set aside the idea of chawanmushi, and this is a thrilling combination, very well executed.

### Eclair d'Epice | Foie Gras | Cherry Kosho | Pistachio 🕶️
image: ./images/taian-table/08.jpg | Spiced éclair filled with foie gras mousse, topped with pistachios, purple shiso leaves and a chocolate disc, on a round white ceramic base, Taian Table, Shanghai
A spiced éclair, exquisitely decorated, filled with a three-layer foie gras mousse, each layer with its own texture, and wrapped in Cherry Kosho jam (a Japanese fermentation technique adapted with cherries in place of yuzu), gently bittersweet with a faint sourness. It is topped with Italian pistachios, a dark chocolate disc, purple shiso leaves and lightly salted cheese crisps. The bitterness of the chocolate gives it real weight. One of my favourite dishes of the night.

### Bao | Torched Wagyu | Smoked Caviar
image: ./images/taian-table/09.jpg | A soft white bao topped with torched wagyu, caviar and herbs on a black ceramic base, Taian Table, Shanghai
Torched wagyu, meltingly tender with a gentle aroma, paired with smoked Sichuan caviar, light and never aggressively salty, and a soft bao filled with white asparagus. Technically it is good, flawless even, but conceptually I still don't quite grasp the message the chef wants this dish to convey.

### Dosa | Torched Aji | Aloo Masala
image: ./images/taian-table/10.jpg | Torched aji fish on a square dosa on a white ceramic stand, Taian Table, Shanghai
Indian potato curry (aloo masala), made silky smooth with French technique, wrapped in a dosa and served with torched aji (horse mackerel) and a gently spicy sauce. The cooking is excellent throughout, but I found the dosa a little oily, and the aji didn't really stand out. The dish is already complete in itself; it doesn't need the protein at all.

### Confit Toothfish | Oyster | Fennel | Bouillabaisse
image: ./images/taian-table/11.jpg | White-fleshed toothfish topped with an oyster wrapped in fried puff pastry, with orange bouillabaisse poured over, on a wave-shaped white plate, Taian Table, Shanghai
South American toothfish, confit (cooked slowly in fat at a low temperature) in butter until evenly tender throughout, served in a bouillabaisse that is mellow and full of umami, its taste of the sea never too strong, and not overly sweet or sour. It comes with fennel purée and a fennel salad dressed with mandarin and lemon for a cool, luxurious fragrance, topped with an oyster wrapped in wonderfully crisp fried puff pastry. The technique here goes through the roof.

### Charcoal Grilled Pigeon | Preserved Blackberry | Sauce Rouennaise
image: ./images/taian-table/12.jpg | Two slices of charcoal-grilled pigeon breast in a deep red sauce with white bean purée and blackberries on a white plate, Taian Table, Shanghai
Pigeon from Huzhou, dry-aged for 4 days until tender with no gamey odour, charcoal-grilled just right and dressed with Sauce Rouennaise (a classic French sauce on a bordelaise base enriched with poultry liver), which the chef makes lighter and less bitter than I expected. It comes with white bean purée and pickled wild blackberries.

### Fermented Mandarin Sorbet | Jasmin Flower
image: ./images/taian-table/13.jpg | A coiled ribbon of cream-coloured sorbet on clear jelly in a black ceramic bowl, Taian Table, Shanghai
A wonderfully refreshing palate cleanser. The cool fragrance of jasmine flower jelly meets the bright sourness of finger lime, before it ends on the gentle, bittersweet tang of mandarin. It cleanses the palate beautifully.

### New Zealand Lamb | Kashmiri Spice | Seasonal Vegetables
image: ./images/taian-table/14.jpg | Pink lamb with green tortellini, seasonal vegetables and a brown sauce on a white plate, Taian Table, Shanghai
Lamb saddle cooked medium rare, a beautiful rosy red with almost no gamey odour, leaving only the light grassy note of New Zealand lamb. It comes with a lamb jus you can feel the collagen in, spiked with Kashmiri chilli for heat at the tip of the tongue, and tortellini filled with minced lamb shoulder. The dish is delicious and up to standard, but felt the most ordinary of the meal, as its textures and presentation inevitably reminded me of another three-star restaurant in Singapore.

### 68% Chocolate Gateau | Roasted Plum | Almond Gelato
image: ./images/taian-table/15.jpg | Chocolate cake topped with white almond gelato and pink flower petals on a pleated black ceramic plate, Taian Table, Shanghai
A 68% chocolate cake (a lucky number), soft and light, its chocolate fruity rather than deeply bitter, served with roasted plum, plum jelly and almond gelato. At first I thought the almond might not fit, but its cool fragrance and the plum's distinctive sweet-sour character gave the chocolate cake real dimension, quite convincingly. A very good dish.

### Petit Fours
image: ./images/taian-table/16.jpg | Three small sweets, a red cherry chocolate, a golden choux and a square chocolate, on cocoa beans on a wooden plate, Taian Table, Shanghai
To finish, little sweets: Cherry & Dark Chocolate, Peanut Praline and Citrus Choux.

### Vanilla "Kipferl"
image: ./images/taian-table/17.jpg | Crescent-shaped cookies dusted with icing sugar in a grey cast-iron pot, Taian Table, Shanghai
German-style crescent-moon cookies, crisp outside and light within, with a simple, traditional sweetness.

### Chocolate Box
image: ./images/taian-table/18.jpg | An open black wooden box with assorted colourful chocolates arranged in compartments, Taian Table, Shanghai
A range of flavours: Umeboshi, Beetroot, Caramel Sea Salt, Raspberry & Pistachio, and Earl Grey Passionfruit.
