Another restaurant that has everything except the final MICHELIN star. The experience today was impressive, from before I walked in until the end of the meal.

Kadeau has held two MICHELIN stars for a long time, along with a Green Star, and is on the World's 50 Best extended list of 100 restaurants, at number 91.

Before you even enter, the reception team waiting outside welcomes you warmly, joking and making conversation. They then take you past the open kitchen before finally leading you to your table.

Kadeau tries to care about every detail: from light bulbs that photograph beautifully without the moiré of some LEDs, to a lavender and geranium aroma diffuser matched to the hand towels. What impressed me most was our server, who was not a sommelier yet could expertly recommend a wine from the enormous wine list based on the taste profile we described. Today I had Taille aux Loups, 'Clos de Mosny', Montlouis-sur-Loire. For drinks here, like many bloggers, I recommend wine by the bottle. It seems better value than the pairing, whose fierce price is almost as much as the course itself.

I visited as winter was setting in, when the restaurant serves its Preservation menu, built around fermenting and pickling, for 5,100 DKK all in. I have to admit this is one of the most expensive restaurants in the world, but there are a great many dishes and the ingredients are truly good, especially the seafood. There are several interesting dishes, like oysters and leaves, raw shrimps and canned figs, and cherry leaf with havgus and ham.

My favourite today was the signature smoked salmon. The chef cures the fish with shio koji, cold-smokes it over cherry wood for three days, then hot-smokes it each day to build texture. It comes two ways: the slightly crunchy outer part with all kinds of pickled vegetables, and the inside, which almost melts in the mouth, with lavender butter.

To be honest, opinions on this restaurant are quite divided. Some love it and some don't like it at all. Personally, I find the food, especially on this menu, very heavy on fermentation and pickling. The techniques are close to three-star level, but the service starts to wobble when the room is full, some cutlery doesn't suit certain dishes, and there was fine dust on the wine glasses that stood out under the restaurant's lights.

## The art of taste in this meal

### tomatoes, crab and gooseberries
image: ./images/kadeau/01.jpg | Dried tomato stuffed with crab on a round plate set in a nest of twigs, Kadeau, Copenhagen
The first dish is dried tomato stuffed with brown crab, eaten with dried gooseberries and elderflower butter. Served at room temperature. Sweet-sour leads, against the sweet-sour of the cape gooseberry, with two chewy textures playing off each other in the mouth. The crab brings savouriness, and everything is pulled together by the richness of butter, which never feels heavy because the sourness of the pickles cuts it. Excellent.

The next course is a seafood platter. Sorry, the photo was taken after I had already cleared it.

### razor clam with rose and saffron
image: ./images/kadeau/02.jpg | An empty razor clam shell and seaweed in a nest of twigs, Kadeau, Copenhagen
Razor clam from the Netherlands, brined with pear and kombu. A clear sweet-sourness from the pear, umami from the seaweed to balance it, then a touch of salt. Very delicious.

### cockles and hazelnuts
Danish cockles served with hazelnut milk. An unusual one: the nut milk takes away the bloody, fishy note of these excellent, bouncy cockles in an interesting way.

### queen scallop with blackcurrants
Scallop from Norway, intensely sweet, every bit as intense as the pickled blackcurrants, which are slightly astringent. Very well done again.

### cured squid
Squid cured with kombu and salt, served with magnolia oil and rice bran. The squid is creamy and chewy, not fishy, with a salty umami taste.

### oysters and leaves
image: ./images/kadeau/06.jpg | White foam with dark shellfish and green leaves in a brown clay bowl, Kadeau, Copenhagen
Next the chef serves cabbage with oysters that have been confited, then served with an oyster emulsion. You get the sweet crunch of the cabbage, never hard, the firmness and sweetness of top-class oysters, and a salty finish with the air of the sea from the emulsion.

On the other side is a pickled shiso leaf stuffed with leek, served with coriander seeds. This bite is stronger than the first, so eat it second. You get the full, distinctive taste of the vegetables, deep and salty with umami, finishing with the crunch of coriander seeds. A slightly unusual dish, but very delicious.

### cherry leaf with havgus and ham
image: ./images/kadeau/07.jpg | A cherry leaf on a round dark brown dough on a wooden tray, Kadeau, Copenhagen
Cherry leaves from Bornholm, picked by the restaurant's team in spring and dried, served with havgus cheese aged 24 months and air-dried pork, sesame, a yeast emulsion, beetroot and cherry blossom oil.

At first I thought it would be dry in the mouth, but not at all. The cherry leaf is crisp without being hard, which is unusual, and roasted black sesame inside adds aroma and crunch. The meat is rustic, lightly salty and gentle. There is the scent of the yeast foam, the earthiness of beetroot and the scent of cherry made clearer by the oil. A dish that looks simple but is complex and well balanced.

### raw shrimps and canned figs
image: ./images/kadeau/08.jpg | Thinly sliced raw shrimp with figs on white cream in a round bowl, Kadeau, Copenhagen
This one is puzzling, yet it is delicious and simply works. The chef serves shrimp from Norway with two kinds of fruit, concentrated by drying them a little, and walnuts.

What I loved was the cream underneath. Its scent is a little like green curry but without the heat, gentle and creamy, and it goes perfectly with the other ingredients, bringing every flavour together. The lightly salted roasted walnuts bring out the shrimp superbly and add texture. Using nuts to lift the sweetness of shrimp is really interesting. The figs and strawberries add another dimension with their deep sweetness.

### Signature: hot and cold smoked salmon with lavender
image: ./images/kadeau/09.jpg | Smoked salmon in a stone bowl in front of a stone slab holding orange salmon, Kadeau, Copenhagen
The signature smoked salmon: the chef cures the fish with shio koji, cold-smokes it over cherry wood for three days, then hot-smokes it each day to build texture. It is carved at the table and served two ways. The slightly crunchy outer part, the salmon crust, comes with pickled vegetables such as rapeseed, white asparagus and cucumber.

Calling this the most delicious smoked salmon in the world is no exaggeration. The inside, which almost melts in the mouth, still stays with me today: the fragrance of salmon, the juiciness of the flesh, a gentle saltiness, and the scent of cherry wood smoke blending perfectly with lavender in the mouth. Onion gives crunch, a slight sharpness and a little sweetness that cuts the salmon.

The crust has a texture a little like dried fish yet stays juicy, chewy, stronger in taste than the inside, with a clearer wood smoke scent. I recommend eating it with the salad of many pickled vegetables, which cuts the fish. Personally, I find this dish a little too intense.

### horse mussel waffle
image: ./images/kadeau/10.jpg | An orange waffle on a patterned plate beside shells on a wooden table, Kadeau, Copenhagen
A waffle served with buckwheat, pumpkin seeds, pumpkin and a horse mussel emulsion.

The waffle here is fragrant with roasted buckwheat, crisp outside and dry inside, slightly sweet and a little buttery, with a light smoky scent a bit like dried fish. Truly distinctive. With all the grains and seeds, it is great fun in the mouth. The one flaw: a hard buckwheat grain slipped through.

### apples with osetra royal caviar
image: ./images/kadeau/11.jpg | Apple with caviar in a glass bowl decorated with dried grass, Kadeau, Copenhagen
The chef serves apple cooked in brown butter with a generous amount of caviar, sunflower seeds and sour cream. Up to the restaurant's standard: sweet apple and salty caviar, the cream bringing the flavours together, a little richness and crunch from the sunflower seeds, and apple oil adding complexity to the aroma.

### grilled root vegetables with bee pollen
image: ./images/kadeau/12.jpg | Grilled root vegetables with bee pollen in a yellow bowl surrounded by foam, Kadeau, Copenhagen
A variety of root vegetables, such as carrot, beetroot and dried beetroot, with a sauce of shellfish jus, yeast and brown butter. The sauce is very well done: savoury, gently salty and lightly creamy, with the yeast linking the butter and the vegetables in an interesting way. The vegetables are sweet, with crunchy, soft and chewy textures, and the pickles on top bring sourness. Very good. A little ham tucked in adds saltiness, chew, smoke and meatiness. Very delicious.

### maitake pie
image: ./images/kadeau/13.jpg | Maitake mushrooms on red autumn leaves, Kadeau, Copenhagen
The pastry is made from rye flour and served with duck leg and maitake mushrooms. The duck here is wild duck from Denmark. I like the use of rye, which gives the pastry its own distinctive scent. The earthy wild mushroom and the butter blend perfectly with the bouncy duck.

### wild duck with chestnuts
image: ./images/kadeau/14.jpg | Sliced grilled wild duck with sauce and berries on a ceramic plate, Kadeau, Copenhagen
The chef chooses breast of Danish wild duck, grilled gently over charcoal and seasoned with salt, served with autumn produce such as chestnuts, walnuts and plums, and a sauce made from duck bones, chicken wings and wild berries.

The duck is dense and chewy, with a smoky scent and light saltiness. The sauce has real dimension and is very distinctive: beyond the umami of a usual jus, it has body from the collagen in the chicken bones and a gentle sweet-sourness of berries in the background. A herb that I think was sage or lemon balm adds another layer with finesse. Personally, I found this dish too plain compared with the others.

### preserved fruits
Berries from Bornholm, such as blackcurrant, white currant, green strawberry, mulberry and pear, served in many forms: candied, dried, frozen and as sweets. "A whole range of berry flavours" is probably the best description of this dish.

### berry kelp
image: ./images/kadeau/15.jpg | A round black sheet on white cream in a round ceramic bowl, Kadeau, Copenhagen
Here the chef serves pork fat, seaweed, cream and pine nuts underneath. The seaweed is like kombu, chewy and firm, lightly sweet. The pork fat is lightly seasoned, like an Italian lardo, served chilled so it is chewy like fudge. A good idea, and the more you chew, the more the umami of the seaweed comes out. But for me the texture is too hard and the cream doesn't fit.

### cloudberry tart
image: ./images/kadeau/16.jpg | A small tart topped with orange cloudberries on a white plate beside a cup of cream, Kadeau, Copenhagen
A wheat tart with cream and cloudberries. The pastry is buttery, the berries have their own fragrance and are sweet rather than sour, and the cream brings a yogurt-like sourness. A finish to the meal that isn't too heavy.
