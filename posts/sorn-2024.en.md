This review is a Special Edition that brings together my two most recent meals at Sorn (ศรณ์). The first was in May 2024, after the renovation, when there were whispers that the restaurant was aiming for a third star. The second was in March 2025, after Chef Ice's dream came true: Sorn became the first restaurant in Thailand to receive three MICHELIN stars, and the first Thai restaurant in the world to do so, the highest honour a restaurant can receive.

Sorn is the Southern Thai restaurant of Chef Supaksorn "Ice" Jongsiri, set in a restored two-storey house on Sukhumvit Soi 26. It received one star in the MICHELIN Guide Thailand 2019, rose to two stars in 2020 and earned its third star in 2025.

Chef Ice's family roots are in Nakhon Si Thammarat. He grew up helping his grandmother cook Southern food, then worked at Baan Ice, the family's Southern Thai restaurant, before opening Sorn in June 2018. Almost the entire Sorn team also came from Baan Ice. Chef Ice is self-taught, and once said in an interview: "Don't lie to yourself. Be honest with yourself and with the ingredients, and do your best."

Every ingredient at Sorn comes directly from the 14 provinces of the South, 13 of which lie on the coast, so the backbone of the course here is seafood.

The first thing I want to say is that Sorn is, without a doubt, the best Thai restaurant in the world. There can hardly be a foodie anywhere who hasn't heard of this famous Southern restaurant. The price is high, but Sorn holds the highest standard of any Thai restaurant, and what the team creates is worth every baht. For me, the ingredients and the cooking here are first class, something Thailand should truly be proud of. Personally, I think the food after the renovation has moved up a level and gained real depth. The meal after the renovation was simply superb and unforgettable.

On service, there are still small details that could be better, such as a phone manner that sounded unfriendly. Overall the service isn't bad: from the welcome at the door to showing the way to the bathroom, everything is in order, but to me it still lacks a little charm. The room smelled of disinfectant with a hint of damp. Ingredients are displayed in the room without any explanation of what they are, which may leave foreign guests a little puzzled.

Most importantly, on this visit I felt the heat had been toned down. The course plays more with the melody of flavours and how they build, with dishes that break the flavour and dishes that cool the spice. It has more rhythm and more balance. At the time I believed Sorn would get its third MICHELIN star in the end, and Chef Ice and the Sorn team did exactly that.

Let's take a look at what was served.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Hua Sai fragrant rice, river prawn fat and sam kler
image: ./images/sorn-2024/01.jpg | A round puffed rice cracker topped with orange prawn fat and coriander on a grey stone plate | focus=.5,.4
The meal opens with the greeting "Let's eat, have you eaten yet?": fragrant rice from Hua Sai in Nakhon Si Thammarat with river prawn fat and sam kler, a dip of coriander, chilli and vinegar, and fried sago. Sam kler is the basic paste of the Thai kitchen, garlic, coriander root and white pepper pounded together. The flavour has real depth: hot pepper, sourness from lime, the scent of coriander and the light crunch of fried sago.

### Phuket abalone yum
image: ./images/sorn-2024/02.jpg | Slices of abalone stacked in a clear dressing in a glass bowl | focus=.5,.5
Abalone from Phuket dressed with mangosteen, mamuang bao, salak from Trang and ginger. Mamuang bao is a small, sour, crunchy mango from Songkhla. The abalone has a great chewy texture, and the mango dressing is lightly sweet and sour, fresh and bright.

### Fried sand whiting marinated in turmeric
image: ./images/sorn-2024/03.jpg | A curled fried sand whiting marinated in turmeric with fried leaves behind, on grey stone | focus=.5,.65
Sand whiting marinated in turmeric and fried, with scallop sauce, served with fried Polynesian ivy leaves in curry paste. The fish is crisp. To me the scallop flavour is stronger than the fish itself. A beautiful dish, and very Thai in flavour.

### Surat Thani blue crab cured in fish sauce and Phuket rum
image: ./images/sorn-2024/04.jpg | Blue crab claw, spiny lobster and babylon snail arranged on ceramic stones of different sizes on a wooden table | focus=.5,.5
Blue crab from Surat Thani cured in fish sauce and rum from Phuket, served with a som jeed (calamansi) granita, a coarse Italian shaved ice. There is a little ice in each bite, a good umami from the fish sauce, then gentle citrus from the calamansi. Excellent flavour, and the crab itself is excellent.

### Phuket painted spiny lobster
Painted spiny lobster from Phuket with mamuang bao, watermelon sauce and coriander sauce. The meat is bouncy. Personally I found the lobster flavour didn't quite come through, but the texture was excellent.

### Krabi babylon snail
Babylon snails from Krabi in a Thai-style yum with chilli oil and tao jiew, salted fermented soybean. Rich and savoury, and the spiciest bite of the meal.

### Squid stuffed with spiny lobster
image: ./images/sorn-2024/07.jpg | Stuffed squid glazed with squid ink sauce and dotted with red seeds, on a grey stone | focus=.5,.5
Squid stuffed with spiny lobster, served with grilled eggplant with sam kler, Palian pepper and a sauce made from squid ink. Palian pepper comes from Palian district in Trang. The squid is tender, and the pepper is quite citrusy and fiery.

### Daikon and ginger pickled in wild honey
image: ./images/sorn-2024/08.jpg | A whole daikon with its leaves and ginger pickled in wild honey, in a rough white bowl | focus=.5,.5
The scent of pickled daikon, gentle ginger and the sweetness of honey.

### Kan chu piang 🕶️
image: ./images/sorn-2024/09.jpg | A crab claw standing in a granite mortar over orange crab roe curry paste | focus=.5,.55
Soft-set crab roe with yellow chilli and a crab claw from Surat Thani. This time it is version 2.0: a smooth curry paste, rich crab roe, very spicy, hot and slightly sour. The crab is huge.

### Coconut soup with liang leaves
image: ./images/sorn-2024/10.jpg | A coconut-shell bowl with flower-shaped pieces of grilled coconut before the liang leaf soup is poured | focus=.5,.5
Coconut soup with liang leaves, coconut flesh, grilled coconut cream and crab shell oil. Liang is a local vegetable of the South. The soup is light, green and sweet, slightly astringent from the leaves, with the crab oil tying the flavours together. This dish cools the heat of the previous one.

### Grilled cockles kolek 🕶️
image: ./images/sorn-2024/11.jpg | Skewered cockles brushed with curry paste, grilling on a small charcoal grill | focus=.5,.5
Cockles grilled kolek-style with dried abalone shaved over the top. Kolek is the Malay-style grilling with a coconut curry paste from the lower South. The cockles have a wonderful texture, the sauce is sweet, the curry paste is good, and the umami of dried abalone fills in what the cockles lack.

### Khao yum with Sai Buri budu
image: ./images/sorn-2024/12.jpg | A colourful ring of rice salad with budu dressing on a blue and white patterned plate | focus=.5,.5
Khao yum with budu from Sai Buri, made with turmeric puffed rice, torch ginger flower and pomelo. Budu is a Malay-style fermented fish sauce, and Sai Buri in Pattani is famous for it. Sweet up front, with a green astringency from the many vegetables, and light, crisp rice. This dish is the salad of the course.

### Songkhla Lake fragrant rice
image: ./images/sorn-2024/13.jpg | Fragrant rice from Songkhla Lake cooked in a clay pot | focus=.5,.55
Hom Rachinee rice from Songkhla Lake, cooked in Ranong mineral water until it rises in the pot.

### Side dishes
image: ./images/sorn-2024/14.jpg | Fresh vegetables and side dishes in a four-well leaf-shaped tray beside a bowl of vegetables | focus=.55,.5
Besides vegetables and chilli fish sauce, there are red quail eggs pickled in chilli fish sauce, dried kaeng som paste with sweet shrimp, and pork crackling with wild mafai chilli dip.

### Stir-fried catfish with sator
image: ./images/sorn-2024/15.jpg | Spicy stir-fried catfish with sator beans and a bowl of broth in the centre of a turquoise plate | focus=.5,.55
Chewy-skinned catfish stir-fried spicy with sator beans, eaten with young leaves of mataum khaek. The fish is excellent, incredibly tender, and the sourness of the leaves tames the heat.

### Stir-fried morning glory
image: ./images/sorn-2024/16.jpg | Green morning glory on a cream ceramic plate with a brown rim, topped with crispy prawns and scallops | focus=.5,.5
Chilled morning glory with the restaurant's own oyster sauce and crispy prawns and scallops. A little on the sweet side, but with a contrast of crunchy and soft textures.

### Phuket tiger prawn omelette
image: ./images/sorn-2024/17.jpg | A tall omelette topped with large pieces of tiger prawn on a pale blue plate | focus=.45,.5
A towering "condo" omelette with big pieces of prawn, the dish that breaks the flavour of the feast.

### Pickled plum soup
image: ./images/sorn-2024/18.jpg | Cloudy pickled plum soup in a flower-shaped ceramic bowl | focus=.45,.5
Soup with the restaurant's own pickled plums and a broth simmered from stingray bones and skin, served with stingray from Phuket. Light in flavour, the final break of the feast. I would call this set the pinnacle of the Thai sumrub, the shared feast.

### Phatthalung roti with beef curry and winter melon
image: ./images/sorn-2024/19.jpg | Squares of beef on roti with yellow curry in a granite bowl | focus=.5,.55
Roti from Phatthalung with beef curry and winter melon. A middle-of-the-road curry paste, not very concentrated, mild in flavour and not very well rounded, but the roti has a good texture and the beef is good.

### Black sticky rice
image: ./images/sorn-2024/20.jpg | Black sticky rice with an orange rolled sweet and a wafer on a white plate | focus=.55,.5
Black sticky rice with jasmine coconut ice cream and fried beans, eaten all together. Rich and creamy.

### Rambutan loy kaew with mango and jasmine water ice cream
Tastes just like loy kaew, fruit in jasmine syrup.

### Songkhla khanom khai
To finish, khanom khai, little egg cakes from Songkhla.

🕰️ Looking back at the first meal in 2020, the two-star era

Back in September 2020, Sorn was the last MICHELIN-starred restaurant in Thailand I had yet to visit, until the restaurant switched to online booking. It had just moved up to two stars. After that meal I wrote that Sorn was one of the best restaurants in the country, with amuse-bouches among the best in Thailand, but that some of the main dishes tasted alike and the desserts still lacked a wow. My favourite dishes that night were the kaeng som with sand whiting and the signature kan chu piang.
