Hanakoji Sawada is another restaurant I put on my list for this trip, because it is the only three-Michelin-star Kappo Kaiseki on the island of Hokkaido, alongside Moliere, the French restaurant I visited on the first day of this trip, and Sushi Miyakawa, where I had also dined earlier. Compared with my experiences at those two, I have to say I was very disappointed in Sawada.

Hanakoji Sawada is not far from Susukino subway station. It was winter, so the sky grew dark quite early, and I shopped along the Tanukikoji shopping street before heading to our destination. The snow on the ground was thick that day. For those not used to walking on snow, I recommend leaving a little extra time in Hokkaido's winter, because the roads are very slippery.

Hanakoji Sawada is located in the Plaza 7.4 building. After stepping out of the lift on the third floor, I found the restaurant just as I walked in. Incense is burning, giving a sense of calm, which suits the interior, finished entirely in clean white wood, in contrast with the lively Susukino outside.

The service at Hanakoji Sawada is faultless, and the staff speak English quite well.

The menu has a fair range, but the restaurant is very reasonably priced for a three-Michelin-star place. Every course starts from 10,000 yen and up. Today I ordered the restaurant's starter set at 12,000 yen, partly because even though the restaurant holds three Michelin stars, its Tabelog score is only 3.69, which left me unsure of its skill and its value for money.

My advice is to order the top course. The two Japanese guests next to me had a menu that looked much better than mine. The starter set is not very good value: it is rather expensive for what is served, and the menu was almost exactly identical to a review by a foreign blogger that I had come across, written more than five years ago.

As for the owner-chef, Tomoya Kago began as a French chef before changing his path to Japanese cuisine after his training. He returned to become head chef of Sawada for several decades before the restaurant was finally renamed Hanakoji Sawada.

Chef Kago's food feels simple, cooked at a good level, with good ingredients, but overall I was rather disappointed. It does not stand apart from other top restaurants enough to justify its three-Michelin-star status. I have eaten at several Kappo Kaiseki restaurants in Tokyo that were cheaper and had more interesting menus than this.

The menu at Hanakoji Sawada looks rather ordinary and unexciting. The strength of this restaurant, for me, is the basic dishes, which are done very well. If I wanted to bring someone who had never had good Japanese food, I would recommend this place. It has dishes accessible to everyone, from children to adults, but for a serious eater, coming here is a bit of a waste of time (I had a nomihodai party with friends to get to afterwards ;P).

Let's see from the photos what we had today.

## The art of taste in this meal

### Tofu
image: ./images/hanakoji-sawada/01.jpg | Fried yamaimo tofu topped with ikura and wasabi flowers in a white ceramic bowl, Hanakoji Sawada, Sapporo
The first dish today was tofu mixed with yamaimo, or mountain yam, battered and fried, then topped with wasabi flowers, ikura, ume bando and yuzu peel, and drizzled with yuzu dashi jelly. This was one of the good dishes of the meal: varied flavours, rounded and gentle, with many textures in the tofu itself, the crunch of the yam, the very soft tofu, a crisp, rich, chewy batter, and a silky jelly. The saltiness of top-grade ikura against the lightly sweet, lightly salty dashi was very good. My only small complaint is that I felt the bitterness of the ume bando held everything back a little.

### Corn Kakiage
image: ./images/hanakoji-sawada/02.jpg | Crisp Hokkaido corn tempura on paper in a square dish, Hanakoji Sawada, Sapporo
The next dish was the island's famous Hokkaido corn, fried as tempura. This restaurant fries corn tempura very well, crisp all over without being burnt. The slightly salty batter lifts the flavour, and the corn is of excellent quality and sweet. It is well done, but there is nothing outstanding about it.

### Kegani
image: ./images/hanakoji-sawada/03.jpg | Hairy crab meat served with lemon on a leaf on a square plate, Hanakoji Sawada, Sapporo
The next dish the chef served was steamed hairy crab with lemon and a vinegar sauce. The chef gave me crab in three parts, each with a different texture and flavour. The ginger-scented sauce, with a faint sweet-and-sour taste, lifted the crab meat well. The crab meat itself, very fresh and sweet, was excellent. Another good dish, but too ordinary.

### Uni & Eggplant
image: ./images/hanakoji-sawada/04.jpg | Grilled aubergine stuffed with miso and topped with orange uni in a green ceramic bowl, Hanakoji Sawada, Sapporo
Now on to the grilled dishes, and one of the best of the evening. It looks ordinary: grilled aubergine stuffed with miso and topped with uni. Miso is usually intense, so I rather wondered whether it would cover the flavour of the uni, but it turned out very well. The combination goes together unbelievably, with top-grade Hokkaido sea urchin, sweet and creamy, and hot, tender aubergine with a faint smokiness. But the most wonderful part of this dish is the sweet miso, which was so good that it holds everything together. Very delicious.

### Taro
image: ./images/hanakoji-sawada/05.jpg | Battered fried taro in a dashi and yuzu sauce in a blue-and-white bowl, Hanakoji Sawada, Sapporo
Next was battered fried taro in a dashi and yuzu sauce. The taro was very sweet, with a crunchy rice-flour batter with a faint aroma of rice. The yuzu peel added dimension to the dish. The katsuobushi dashi sauce, lightly salty, set off the taro's gentle sweetness very well. But although the dashi was well made, it still lacked dimension and depth worthy of the restaurant's standing, and it was quite ordinary compared with restaurants of the same level.

### Sushi
image: ./images/hanakoji-sawada/06.jpg | Yari-ika and chutoro sushi on a round ceramic plate, Hanakoji Sawada, Sapporo
Next the chef chose to serve sushi, in two bites: yari-ika and chutoro. The sushi at Hanakoji Sawada has slightly warm rice, which is okay but still ordinary compared with good sushi restaurants. The toro had a slight smell of blood, rather disappointing for a restaurant of this level. The yari-ika was high-quality, with yuzu zest on top, served with salt. The squid was sweet and creamy with a sharp citrus note at the tip of the nose. Delicious, even compared with good sushi restaurants.

### Amadai
image: ./images/hanakoji-sawada/07.jpg | Grilled amadai with crisp skin served with sudachi on a blue-and-white plate, Hanakoji Sawada, Sapporo
Another highlight of the meal arrived with grilled amadai. Although grilled, it was more like fried: the skin was crisp like a wafer-thin fry, thin and crisp with no burnt smell despite the grilling. The fish was rich, springy and juicy, with a slight sweetness and richness. The flavour may have been a touch bland, but it let us perceive the sweetness of a top-grade fish very well. Delicious.

### Wagyu Sukiyaki
image: ./images/hanakoji-sawada/08.jpg | Hokkaido beef sukiyaki with sweet beans on an olive-green plate, Hanakoji Sawada, Sapporo
The next dish the chef served was Hokkaido beef sukiyaki topped with sansho pepper, then coated with a thin layer of raw egg yolk. The dish looks very simple, but it was well made. The sweet beans served alongside, crisp outside and sweet, suited the beef well. The sukiyaki sauce, salty-led with a sweetness and a complex flavour, brought out the fine wagyu, fragrant and rich but not too cloying. Delicious, but compared with it, a good sukiyaki restaurant can make this dish too.

### Rice & Soba
image: ./images/hanakoji-sawada/09.jpg | Baked crab rice in a lidded bowl and chilled soba with seaweed in a red bowl, Hanakoji Sawada, Sapporo
Now the last dish of the meal. This one is rather special because both noodles and rice are served: baked crab rice with chilled soba. I recommend eating the rice first, because its flavour is considerably milder than the soba's, and if you eat it afterwards it will turn out bland. The rice was cooked okay, fragrant with a light crab aroma, lightly seasoned, with a slight crab sweetness, nothing with a really strong crab fragrance. The soba, on the other hand, quite surprised me, because the noodles were very chewy, chewy beyond what soba should be. But it was good: the springy noodles with crisp, lightly fragrant seaweed, set against the salty dashi sauce and the spicy-sweet wasabi.

### Pear
image: ./images/hanakoji-sawada/10.jpg | Halved chilled pear on a blue-and-white plate with hojicha to close the meal, Hanakoji Sawada, Sapporo
Dessert was a chilled pear. This one used genuinely good fruit. The pear was fragrant and sweet, slightly crisp and rich, with an aroma like a mix of pear and apple. Eaten with hojicha, it brought our meal to a complete close.
