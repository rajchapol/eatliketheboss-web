When it comes to Thai restaurants that have held firm to the roots of authentic flavour while winning international recognition, **R-HAAN** (อาหาร), holder of two Michelin stars, is surely on the list that gourmets from around the world always make a point of visiting. The restaurant's ninth year arrives with the concept **"9th Wisdom of Thai Happiness" (ภูมิปัญญาสำรับอภิรมย์)**, under the philosophy "fish in the water, rice in the fields", reflecting the abundance of the Thai land, and awakening the taste buds through nine dimensions of flavour. The ninth-anniversary menu will be served through to the end of the year. The true "next step" this season is that **Chef Chumpol Jangprai** has been willing to break the frame of the localism that once confined him to 100% domestic ingredients, and become a full-fledged **Global Thai Gastronomy**. He brings in global luxury ingredients such as foie gras, lobster, caviar and A4 Japanese wagyu and weaves them into Thai spices and herbs, lifting the flavours to the standard of international fine dining seamlessly, without losing the Thai spirit. The atmosphere remains classic, with immaculate service, and feels like a Western sit-down dinner. The course sequence is designed in an international format, except for the Main Course, which is still served as a Thai "samrub" to preserve the tradition of sharing. For this ninth-anniversary course, I personally rate it as the finest expression of the harmony and Culinary Maturity of Chef Chumpol that I have ever eaten: foreign influences blended into Thai food in an ultra smooth way. Awakening the palate through nine dimensions of flavour is a clever way to open the course, and the chef uses world-class technique to tell the story in a playful way, before closing with the samrub-style spread to preserve the Thai tradition of eating together, with nine kinds of brown rice baked with herbs and fragrant with garlic, and first-light jasmine rice from Ubon. The drinks pairing here goes all out, with nine styles of pairing, from tea and mocktails to Champagne and whisky. Pairing wine with the heat and spice of Thai food is already very difficult, a challenging but charming undertaking. Still, the world of gastronomy is vast, and opening your mind to other pairings, such as a special tea blend or cocktails made from Thai spirits, is a voyage into new dimensions that surprises just as well.

It is one of the few traditional Thai restaurants to have kept its two Michelin stars for so long. The restaurant continues to act as a cultural ambassador, taking tom yum goong into the UNESCO campaign, presenting GI (Geographical Indication) ingredients on an ongoing basis, and raising local Thai rice to stand alongside the world's ingredients. AMUSE-BOUCHE (The 9 Elements of Taste): the nine tastes of Thai food through nine bites, with the chef recommending that you eat the umami first.

## The art of taste in this meal

### UMAMI (Rounded) - Laab Croquettes
This one! A combination of nine fermented and pickled ingredients (pla ra, pla som, kapi, fish sauce, blue cheese, salted fish, and Thai, Japanese and Korean pickles) made into a croquette (a French-style fritter, crisp outside and juicy within), using fermentation techniques that draw out the ultimate umami. The rounded savouriness explodes in the mouth.

### SOUR (Zesty) - Som Tum Spheres
Thai papaya salad served as clear, round jelly spheres, using the molecular gastronomy technique of spherification (turning a liquid into a pearl-like skin, like fish roe). Bite into it and it bursts in the mouth, giving a refreshing sweet-and-sour tang, as zesty as its name.

### RICH (Swanky Nine) - Foie Gras Terrine
A terrine (meat or liver ground, seasoned and pressed into a mould in the French style). This bite uses fine duck liver, fragrant with Thai brandy, its richness cut with a berry sauce, giving a soft, gentle dimension of richness.

### BITTER (Bitter Is the Medicine) - Parmesan sphere & Bitter-melon jelly
Spanish sweet prawn (Carabinero, renowned for its juicy sweetness) steeped in fish sauce, served with a Thai bitter-melon (mara khi nok) jelly. The faint bitterness of the bitter melon lifts the sweetness of the prawn superbly.

### FIZZY (Singha Soda) - Three-breed free-range chicken
Chicken of three breeds (such as Betong chicken and free-range village chicken) grilled over charcoal until the meat is tender and springy, tossed with makhwaen (a northern Thai spice that acts something like mala), giving a tingling, fizzy sensation with a light numbness at the tip of the tongue.

### SALTY (Salt) - Salted sun-dried beef
Sun-dried beef with just the right salty richness. The meat is not tough, and is fun to chew.

### SWEET (Palace Sweetness) - Crispy rice noodles in golden cups
Golden cups made with longan blossom honey, filled with crispy rice noodles in a sweet-and-sour flavour.

### SPICY (Extolling the Pepper) - Steamed pork sausage
A fresh-chilli mu yo shaped like a chilli. The outside is jelly and the inside is mu yo (Thai steamed pork sausage) fragrant with fresh chilli, with a fiery heat that is just right.

### ASTRINGENT (Herbs in Support) - Northern Thai pounded jackfruit salad
Pounded jackfruit and Indian gooseberry, giving an astringency that cuts through the flavours and cleanses the palate perfectly.

### COURSE 2 | WISDOM OF THAI CUISINE (Fish in the Water, Rice in the Fields)
image: ./images/r-haan/10.jpg | Torched otoro topped with caviar on rice mixed with mackerel chilli dip, R-Haan, Bangkok
Rice mixed with mackerel nam phrik and mackerel roe, served in a sushi-like form, using Japanese rice from Chiang Rai topped with torched otoro (the fattest part of the tuna belly) and Tibetan caviar. The rice is chewy and springy, the shrimp paste is clearly present, and there is mackerel flesh tucked into the rice. The punchy nam phrik cuts the richness of the otoro well. Personally, I think that if this dish swapped the caviar for "uni", the creaminess and sweetness of the sea urchin would blend with the nam phrik more smoothly and beautifully.

### COURSE 3 | PEARL OF THE ANDAMAN (Thailand's Lobster Tom Kha) 🕶️
image: ./images/r-haan/11.jpg | Lobster with asparagus on a plate with an orange-and-white rim, R-Haan, Bangkok
Grilled lobster at Medium Rare, the flesh springy, sweet and juicy. The sauce is fresh coconut milk blended with Champagne and coriander oil. The first touch on the tongue feels like a Beurre Blanc (the French white butter sauce often eaten with fish), but as you swallow, a gentle aroma of tom kha rises up to touch the nose. The sauce is light, with a faint acidity from the Champagne at the tip of the tongue, cradling the flavour of the lobster completely.

### COURSE 4 | GULF OF THAILAND (Fiery Red Grouper)
image: ./images/r-haan/12.jpg | Steamed grouper in red curry sauce with snow peas, topped with crispy fish floss with chilli and ginger, in a grey bowl, R-Haan, Bangkok
The chef presents it in a style like stir-fried noodles: wide noodles mixed with sakura shrimp, served with steamed grouper, red curry sauce and crispy fish floss with chilli and ginger, lightly fragrant with chilli and ginger. It closes with the freshness of snow peas.

### COURSE 5 | WISDOM OF WELLNESS (Ancient Ways)
image: ./images/r-haan/13.jpg | Grilled A4 wagyu tenderloin on a dark brown sauce on a white plate, R-Haan, Bangkok
A4 wagyu tenderloin marinated in tea-seed oil and fresh turmeric before grilling, melting in the mouth. It is eaten with an old-style kaeng khi lek (cassia leaf curry) fragrant with krachai (fingerroot). The chef boils young cassia leaves in several changes of water to remove the bitterness until the flavour is just right. At first I secretly worried it might be too heavy, but the gentle bitterness of the cassia leaves cut the richness of the wagyu perfectly. A very good and balanced pairing. PALATE CLEANSER SORBET | T-THAI 9 FRUITS: Thai tea with a selection of nine Thai fruits sorbet. It is a sorbet of Thai tea with nine kinds of fruit, sweet-and-sour and fragrant with Thai tea leaves.

### MAIN COURSE (The Royal Samrub)
image: ./images/r-haan/14.jpg | Brown rice and jasmine rice in a brown clay pot on a Thai-patterned saucer, R-Haan, Bangkok
Served as a "samrub" to preserve the Thai tradition of eating together, with nine kinds of brown rice baked with herbs and fragrant with garlic, and first-light jasmine rice from Ubon (rice that receives the fullest sunshine, distinctively sweet and fragrant).

### Tom Yum Goong: Thailand's Pride, UNESCO
image: ./images/r-haan/15.jpg | River prawn tom yum in a Thai-patterned bowl, with broth being poured from a siphon, R-Haan, Bangkok
The chef's signature, using the Syphon technique (a vacuum extraction device similar to those used to brew coffee) to draw out the fragrance of the herbs. The GI (Geographical Indication) river prawns from Ratchaburi are large, with firm, springy flesh. The broth leads with sourness without being sharp, sweet from the prawn stock, flawlessly rounded. The chef used this dish to compete on Iron Chef, and it is also the dish he pushed into the UNESCO campaign.

### Blue Crab Curry (King of Herbs)
image: ./images/r-haan/16.jpg | Yellow-orange blue crab curry in an embossed brass bowl, R-Haan, Bangkok
A Phuket-style kaeng khua curry of crab, with large pieces of crab meat. The flavour is rich, fiery and punchy, with the full force of southern curry paste, its saltiness cut by "chakhram leaves"

### Lanna Black Pork Ong
image: ./images/r-haan/17.jpg | Lanna black pork ong served with fresh vegetables and pork crackling on a blue-and-white plate, R-Haan, Bangkok
Made with premium Iberico pork, the black pig of Spain, in a nam phrik ong. The flavour leads with sweet-and-sour and is not very spicy. Although it is meant as a brake on the heat of the crab curry, I feel that the overall flavour of the dishes in this samrub is rather forceful. If a lighter dish, such as something fried or grilled, were slipped in between, this samrub would be flawless.

### A CELEBRATION OF HIMMAPAN (Himmaphan)
image: ./images/r-haan/18.jpg | Himmaphan dessert of ripe mango and fruit garnished with a black crisp on a white plate, R-Haan, Bangkok
A dessert themed on the Himmaphan forest: a crisp green tea thong muan roll, eaten with a mango parfait, khao niao khiao ngu (snake-fang sticky rice), and a novelty of coconut ice cream truffle that goes together surprisingly well.

### PETIT FOURS
image: ./images/r-haan/19.jpg | Petit fours arranged as the Milky Way, with durian macaron and bael fruit canelé on a blue plate, R-Haan, Bangkok
It closes with petit fours plated as the "Milky Way": toddy palm soufflé cake (khanom tan), sato chocolate bonbon, Monthong durian macaron, and the restaurant's signature **Bael Fruit Canelé (คานาเล่มะตูม)**.
